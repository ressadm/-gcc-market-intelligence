## 1. Brazil — Serpro (with Dataprev): is the state IT company a credible national sovereign-cloud champion? (Full profile)

### Takeaway
Serpro is the closest thing Brazil has to a national cloud champion, but it is a government-mandated *sovereign-cloud operator and multicloud broker for the federal state*, not a commercial cloud or AI champion: its "Nuvem de Governo" runs on foreign stacks (AWS Outposts, Google Distributed Cloud, Huawei Cloud Stack; Oracle and Microsoft in homologation) inside two Serpro-owned data centres, and government-cloud revenue was only about R$89m in 2025 against R$4.7bn total net revenue. In 2026 the government opened a separate "Nuvem Brasileira" process (MGI–Serpro–ABDI–BNDES) to procure a licensable cloud software platform with source-code access and a "disconnection test" — still at the non-binding market-sounding stage as of September 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Overview, ownership, mandate**
- Serpro (Serviço Federal de Processamento de Dados) is a federal state-owned IT company; it hosts more than 5,000 government systems and operates Gov.br (about 160 million registered users, about 1.2 million documents signed per day, about 50 million biometric validations per month) — statements by Serpro CEO Wilton Mota, 11 May 2026 **[SELF-REPORTED]** — [Mobile Time, 11 May 2026](https://www.mobiletime.com.br/noticias/11/05/2026/serpro-nuvem-soberana/)
- Formal demand-creation rule: Portaria SGD/MGI nº 5.950/2023 defines which cloud services federal bodies can contract and from whom; an MGI rule requires bodies to prioritise the Nuvem de Governo unless the body's Digital Governance Committee gives a technical justification — [Convergência Digital, 10 Jun 2025](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/governo/governo-lanca-nuvem-soberana-com-serpro-e-dataprev-sem-abrir-mao-das-big-techs/)
- On 10 June 2025 the Ministry of Management and Innovation (MGI) released two Nuvem de Governo service catalogues operated by Serpro and Dataprev, stating more than R$1bn invested **[SELF-REPORTED by MGI; breakdown undisclosed]** and availability to more than 250 federal executive bodies (addressable base, not actual users); MGI set maximum prices — [Convergência Digital, 10 Jun 2025](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/governo/governo-lanca-nuvem-soberana-com-serpro-e-dataprev-sem-abrir-mao-das-big-techs/)
- Vendors at launch: Dataprev — AWS, Huawei, Oracle; Serpro — AWS, Huawei, Oracle and Google. The outlet's headline framing: "sovereign cloud … without giving up the big techs" — [Convergência Digital, 10 Jun 2025](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/governo/governo-lanca-nuvem-soberana-com-serpro-e-dataprev-sem-abrir-mao-das-big-techs/)
- A November 2025 report said 11 federal bodies had actually migrated to the infrastructure **[ACTUAL, via search summary]**; Minister Esther Dweck said contracts with Huawei, Microsoft, Oracle and Google would not be terminated immediately — [DCD Brasil](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/br/not%C3%ADcias/governo-pretende-migrar-dados-estrat%C3%A9gicos-para-infraestrutura-soberana-brasileira/) and [Convergência Digital](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/?p=45958)

**Technology and sovereign design**
- Nuvem de Governo runs in two Serpro-owned data centres (Brasília and São Paulo) operated entirely by Serpro employees; homologated stacks: AWS Outposts, Google Distributed Cloud, Huawei Cloud Stack; Oracle and Microsoft "in homologation" (Aug 2026). Services include analytics, AI (including GPUs for model training), compute, databases, connectivity, security. GPU counts and MW are not disclosed — [Agência Gov / Serpro, 26 Aug 2026](https://agenciagov.ebc.com.br/noticias/202608/nuvem-de-governo-do-serpro-mantem-dados-sigilosos-sob-jurisdicao-brasileira)
- Target: migrate classified ("sigilosos") data to the sovereign cloud by 2027, listed as Action 27 of the PBIA **[TARGET]** — [Agência Gov / Serpro, 26 Aug 2026](https://agenciagov.ebc.com.br/noticias/202608/nuvem-de-governo-do-serpro-mantem-dados-sigilosos-sob-jurisdicao-brasileira)
- Serpro describes a graded model: public data continues on hyperscaler public clouds under a Serpro-managed contractual umbrella (brokerage); data classified as secret/strategic moves to the sovereign environment under Brazilian jurisdiction — [Serpro news, 2026](https://www.serpro.gov.br/menu/noticias/noticias-2026/serpro-soberania-na-nuvem)
- CEO Wilton Mota (11 May 2026): when Serpro sought a fully isolated cloud with no external orchestration, Huawei was the only vendor that initially responded; Google, AWS and Microsoft later approached Serpro. Mota explicitly said this does not mean sovereignty has been achieved; Serpro runs 300 AI-enabled services; it withstood a DDoS of "1 TB" the previous week **[SELF-REPORTED]** — [Mobile Time, 11 May 2026](https://www.mobiletime.com.br/noticias/11/05/2026/serpro-nuvem-soberana/)
- Serpro's 2026 institutional strategy targets: implement 10 platform features in 2026, deliver 3 AI services on the Nuvem de Governo, obtain two security certifications by 2027, and make a second region available **[TARGET]** (via search summary) — [Serpro Transparência, Estratégia 2026](https://www.transparencia.serpro.gov.br/governanca/estrategia-institucional/estrategia-2026)
- Oversight: the Federal Court of Accounts (TCU) reportedly required Serpro and Dataprev to present within 180 days a geographic-redundancy plan for the Nuvem de Governo and ordered Serpro to review its AWS contract (date of ruling not confirmed in-session; via search summary) — [Convergência Digital](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/?p=52736)

**Financials (Serpro)**
- 2025 **[ACTUAL]**: net profit R$755.1m (2024: R$685.2m), net revenue R$4.7bn (2024: R$3.93bn), total spending R$4.1bn, year-end cash R$2.5bn (via search summary of official statements) — [Agência Gov, Apr 2026](https://agenciagov.ebc.com.br/noticias/202604/serpro-impulsiona-transformacao-digital-do-brasil-e-registra-lucro-recorde-em-2025) and [Serpro 2025 accounts (PDF)](https://www.transparencia.serpro.gov.br/acesso-a-informacao/contas-anuais/2025/10-2025_publicidade_legal_contas_completas_jornal_versao_digital.pdf)
- Government-cloud revenue rose from R$1.26m in 2024 to more than R$89m in 2025 **[ACTUAL, via search summary]** — i.e., roughly 2% of net revenue — [Serpro strategy performance report Q4 2025 (PDF)](https://www.transparencia.serpro.gov.br/governanca/estrategia-institucional/desempenho-estrategia-4tri.pdf)
- Serpro flagged the impact of payroll-tax "reoneração" on results while crediting government cloud for profit growth — [Convergência Digital](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/governo/serpro-tem-alta-no-lucro-com-nuvem-do-governo-mas-alerta-para-impacto-da-reoneracao-da-folha/)

**Dataprev (second operator)**
- 2025 **[ACTUAL]** (disclosed 16 Apr 2026): net profit R$899.7m (+77%), net revenue R$2.5bn (+29.5%), gross revenue R$2.7bn; financial institutions (payroll-loan/"consignado" services) were 60.6% of revenue; INSS the largest public client at R$526.8m (19.2%); capex R$147.4m (+52.1%) (via search summary) — [Convergência Digital, Apr 2026](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/governo/com-consignado-dataprev-tem-lucro-de-r-900-milhoes-e-crescimento-de-77-em-2025/)

**"Nuvem Brasileira" (2026) — the next step, not yet procured**
- Technical Cooperation Agreement nº 134/2026 between MGI, ABDI, Serpro and BNDES to explore a national cloud solution with high sovereignty requirements — [MGI, ACT and work plan (PDF)](https://www.gov.br/gestao/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/convenios-e-transferencias/acordos-de-cooperacao-tecnica-act/2026/sei_mgi-63786294-acordo-de-cooperacao-tecnica-e-plano-de-trabalho.pdf)
- ABDI Public Consultation Notice nº 1/2026: registration opened 24 Aug 2026; virtual public hearing 3 Sep 2026 — [Agência Gov, Aug 2026](https://agenciagov.ebc.com.br/noticias/202608/escuta-de-mercado-sobre-o-projeto-nuvem-brasileira-tem-inscricoes-abertas); reference document — [ABDI, Caderno Nuvem Brasileira (PDF)](https://www.abdi.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Caderno_nuvembrasileira.pdf)
- Design requirements presented 3 Sep 2026: runs on public infrastructure in Brazil, operated by a public company; software licensed so the State can operate/maintain/evolve it if the private partner exits; reversibility clause with irrevocable source-code access; shared IP; knowledge transfer; R&D in Brazil; "Teste de Desconexão" (platform must function with international links cut; test battery grew from about 70 to nearly 500 tests); "Sovereignty Gradient" weighting data 30% / operational 40% / technological 30% across 15 criteria; bidders must file a Sovereignty Matrix and a Declaration of External Dependencies. Scope is software only — hardware/colocation excluded at this stage; no budget or deadline given; stage is preliminary and non-binding — [Convergência Digital, 3 Sep 2026](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/governo/nuvem-brasileira-vai-funcionar-em-infraestrutura-publica-localizada-no-pais-e-sera-operada-por-empresa-publica/)
- Serpro states the Nuvem Brasileira will not replace commercial clouds but be an additional option alongside Serpro, Dataprev and public cloud providers; a tender (edital) via ABDI is to follow — [Serpro, "Soberania sem isolamento", 2026](https://www.serpro.gov.br/menu/noticias/noticias-2026/soberania-sem-isolamento); press reports expect the edital by end-2026 **[ANNOUNCED]** — [IstoÉ Dinheiro](https://istoedinheiro.com.br/nuvem-brasileira-governo-avanca-dados-soberanos)

### Inferences
- Serpro's champion status rests on a *captive-demand mandate* (priority rule in federal procurement) plus *operational sovereignty* (own sites, own staff, Brazilian jurisdiction), not on technological sovereignty: every homologated stack is foreign (US or Chinese). Serpro's own CEO concedes sovereignty "has not been achieved".
- Actual uptake is still small relative to the narrative: about R$89m cloud revenue (2025) and 11 migrated bodies (Nov 2025) versus "250+ bodies" addressable and "R$1bn+ invested".
- The 2026 "Nuvem Brasileira" design (disconnection test, source-code escrow/reversibility, sovereignty scoring matrix, dependency declarations) is the most transferable policy instrument found in this research: it converts "sovereignty" from a vendor marketing claim into testable procurement criteria. It is unproven — no tender had been issued as of the latest sources (Sept 2026).
- Including Huawei as a homologated sovereign stack shows Brazil is hedging between US and Chinese suppliers rather than choosing a bloc; that is a geopolitical choice other countries (Morocco) may not have the same latitude to make.

### Gaps
- No disclosed MW, rack or GPU counts for Serpro/Dataprev data centres; no breakdown of the "R$1bn+" investment figure (period, capex vs contract value).
- Date and full text of the TCU ruling on geographic redundancy and the AWS contract not verified.
- Whether the Nuvem Brasileira edital was published in Oct–Nov 2026 is unknown (no source after Sept 2026 found).
- Year Serpro was established (1964) and Dataprev (1974) and their supervising ministries (Finance / MGI) are well known but were not sourced in-session.
- 2026 year-to-date Nuvem de Governo revenue and number of migrated bodies not found.

## 2. Brazil — other candidates (Maritaca AI, SoberanIA, Magalu Cloud, Scala, TikTok/ByteDance) and the national ecosystem (PBIA, Redata, BNDES/Finep, LNCC)

### Takeaway
Brazil has an active ecosystem but no private company that credibly qualifies as a national cloud/AI champion: Maritaca AI is a small, technically credible Portuguese-LLM start-up with undisclosed funding; Magalu Cloud is a BNDES-backed domestic cloud of modest scale; Scala is a foreign-PE-owned wholesale data-centre developer; the largest physical projects (TikTok/ByteDance in Ceará) are foreign demand. The state is the organising force: PBIA (R$23bn 2024–2028), a new R$1bn-class AI supercomputer tender (Aug 2026), and the Redata tax regime signed into law on 15 Sept 2026.

- Earlier one-year stocktake: of 54 planned initiatives, 25 had deliveries and 15 were in execution; MCTI's Henrique Miguel said about one-third of targets had been met — [DPL News, "Um ano de PBIA"](https://dplnews.com/um-ano-de-plano-brasileiro-de-inteligencia-artificial-o-que-esta-sendo-feito/)

- BNDES also approved a R$180m credit line for Scala Data Centers (reported in Spanish-language trade press) — [Canal Solar](https://canalsolar.com.br/es/Interior-del-centro-de-datos-de-TikTok-en-Cear%C3%A1/)

### Inferences
- Brazil's model is state-led and layered: (1) state IT companies as sovereign operators; (2) public HPC via LNCC/universities; (3) tax incentives (Redata) to attract mostly foreign-owned hyperscale capacity; (4) BNDES credit to domestic players (Magalu Cloud, Scala). No single entity integrates infrastructure, cloud stack and models.
- Maritaca is a credible "language-model specialist" but has no disclosed funding, revenue or state anchor contract; the state's attention is fragmenting toward competing Portuguese-LLM efforts (SoberanIA/Piauí, PBIA sovereign-model action, China "Rota 2"), which weakens any one candidate.
- Announced supercomputer capacity and PBIA "64% executed" should not be read as delivered capability: the Macaíba machine had not yet been tendered/awarded as of Aug 2026 and is realistically a 2028 asset.
- Redata is an attraction instrument (demand from ByteDance etc.), not a sovereignty instrument; critics argue it trades tax revenue, water and power for limited local value.

## 3. Turkey — Turkcell: the strongest "national cloud champion" candidate (Full profile)

### Takeaway
Turkcell is Turkey's most credible national cloud/data-centre champion: a listed, Wealth-Fund-anchored telecom with 54 MW of active IT capacity across four sites, about €600m cumulative data-centre investment, and — since November 2025 — the designated local infrastructure partner for a Google Cloud region (Ankara hyperscale build, Google US$2bn over ten years, Turkcell about US$1bn to 2032, operations targeted 2028–2029). But data centre and cloud are only about 2% of group revenue, 2026 growth has undershot guidance, the hyperscale capacity will be "fully allocated to Google", and no GPU/AI-compute or proprietary cloud-stack scale has been disclosed.

### Cited Findings

## 4. Turkey — Turkish LLM efforts (T3 AI, TÜBİTAK "Bilge", Havelsan) and the national ecosystem

### Takeaway
Turkey has a dense, state-aligned AI ecosystem (defence primes, TÜBİTAK, the Baykar-linked T3 Foundation, Wealth-Fund telecoms) and a new "AI Vision and Action Plan 2026–2030" (June 2026: ≥1 GW data-centre capacity by 2030, ≥US$10bn mostly private capital, ≥2% of public investment for AI, state as lead buyer of domestic AI). But there is no single AI-model champion: three parallel Turkish-LLM efforts exist, none with a confirmed general public release or independent benchmarks as of the latest sources.

### Cited Findings

**Strategy and institutions**
- "AI Vision and Action Plan for 2026–2030" unveiled at the Türkiye Artificial Intelligence Summit in Istanbul (June 2026; article dated 26 Jun 2026) with President Erdoğan, VP Cevdet Yılmaz and Industry and Technology Minister M. Fatih Kacır. Targets **[TARGET]**: data-centre capacity ≥1 GW by 2030; ≥US$10bn mobilised, mostly private, for AI infrastructure; ≥2% of public investment programmes allocated to AI; public sector as main buyer of domestically developed AI; National AI Research Fund and AI Growth Fund; AI growth zones; 5 million citizens trained in two years; 10,000 advanced specialists and 100,000 application professionals; National Data Library with ≥2,000 public datasets; 30-business-day single-window roadmap for foreign investors; expected added value >TRY 1 trillion — [Invest in Türkiye (Presidential Investment Office)](https://agencyadm.invest.gov.tr/en/News/News-from-Turkey/Pages/turkiye-unveils-ai-action-plan-artificial-intelligence-summit.aspx)

**Data-localisation / public-cloud rule (official interpretation, 2026)**
- The Cybersecurity Presidency's FAQ on the Information and Communication Security Circular (document dated July 2026) states: the circular "draws a framework" for cloud use and "aims for the country's data to remain in the country"; it does **not** prohibit using space, servers or services from domestic *or foreign* cloud providers **provided the data are stored in data centres inside the country and security controls are ensured**; "institutional control" means controls such as which personnel access the systems and at what privilege level, whether servers are dedicated to the institution, and traceability of access/transaction logs. - Havelsan MAIN platform: includes a 9bn-parameter LLM optimised for Turkish, supporting 200+ languages for translation/summarisation **[SELF-REPORTED]**; cited by Erdoğan at the summit alongside TÜBİTAK and T3/Baykar — [Daily Sabah/AA, 26 May 2026](https://www.dailysabah.com/business/economy/in-ai-turkiye-wants-to-replicate-gains-achieved-in-defense-industry); [Invest in Türkiye](https://agencyadm.invest.gov.tr/en/News/News-from-Turkey/Pages/turkiye-unveils-ai-action-plan-artificial-intelligence-summit.aspx)

### Inferences
- Turkey's localisation rule is the clearest example in this set of *regulatory demand creation*: because public and critical-sector data must sit in domestic data centres, foreign hyperscalers need an in-country region — which is exactly what the Google–Turkcell deal delivers. The rule makes local data-centre owners (Turkcell, Türk Telekom) indispensable landlords without banning foreign technology.
- LLM development is deliberately plural and politically networked (Baykar/T3 is closely tied to the presidency; TÜBİTAK and Havelsan are state bodies). This yields redundancy rather than a champion; none has disclosed compute scale, benchmarks or commercial uptake. Reliance on EuroHPC (MareNostrum5) for training indicates domestic AI compute is still thin.
- The 2026–2030 plan's numbers (1 GW, US$10bn) are targets with private capital expected to carry most of the cost; Turkcell's disclosed 54 MW and 100 MW-by-2032 target show how far the 1 GW goal is from current domestic capacity.

### Gaps

- Trendyol LLM, Aselsan AI programmes, TRUBA specifications, ARF hardware (GPU count) — no sources collected.

## 7. Critical assessment — is the "champion" label warranted, what is replicable (esp. for Morocco), and what failed or stalled?

### Takeaway
Across the three countries the "national champion" label is warranted only in qualified form: Serpro is a *state sovereign-cloud operator/broker* (strong mandate, foreign tech, small cloud revenue); Turkcell is a *telecom data-centre landlord allied to a hyperscaler* (real capacity and capex, thin own-cloud/AI); Cassava is a *private pan-African integrator* (Nvidia/Google-backed, opaque capacity). None controls its own cloud stack or frontier models; the replicable elements are institutional (procurement rules, localisation rules, testable sovereignty criteria, telecom anchoring), not technological.

### Inferences
Champion-label verdicts (researcher's judgement):
- **Serpro (Brazil): qualified yes — "sovereign operator", not a tech champion.** Formal mandate, profitable (R$755m 2025), owns sites and operations; but depends on AWS/Google/Huawei/Oracle/Microsoft stacks and cloud is ~2% of revenue. Dataprev is a co-operator rather than a separate champion.
- **Maritaca AI / SoberanIA / Magalu Cloud (Brazil): no (ecosystem players).** Too small or too opaque; state attention is fragmented across them.
- **Turkcell (Turkey): qualified yes — "national data-centre champion and hyperscaler gateway".** Real capacity (54 MW), real capex (~€600m), state anchor shareholder; but own-cloud/AI layer is thin and the flagship project serves Google.
- **Türk Telekom (Turkey): potential, not yet demonstrated.** T3 AI / TÜBİTAK Bilge / Havelsan: national projects, not champions (no public release/benchmarks/commercial traction evidenced).
- **Cassava (pan-Africa/South Africa): qualified yes at regional level; not a South African national champion.** South Africa itself: no national champion.

Replicable elements for other emerging economies (Morocco in particular):
1. *Mandated public demand with price caps and a default-to-sovereign rule* (Brazil's Portaria 5.950/2023 + priority rule) — cheap to legislate; effectiveness depends on enforcement (Brazil's uptake remained modest after 18 months).
2. *Graded data classification* (public data on hyperscalers via a state broker; classified data on state-operated infrastructure) — avoids the false binary of "all sovereign" vs "all hyperscaler".
3. *Testable sovereignty in procurement* (disconnection test, source-code escrow/reversibility, dependency declarations, weighted sovereignty score) — the most novel and portable instrument; as yet untested in an actual award.
4. *Localisation-with-openness* (Turkey): require in-country storage and institutional control rather than banning foreign vendors — this pulls hyperscaler regions in and gives local telecoms a landlord role. Risk: local champion becomes a real-estate/power provider with one dominant tenant.
5. *Telecom-anchored hyperscaler partnership with state shareholder* (Turkcell–Google): replicable where a state-linked telecom has balance sheet and sites (in Morocco, analogous to a Maroc Telecom/inwi-type role); negotiate for capacity not exclusively allocated to the hyperscaler, plus local GPU/own-cloud carve-outs.
6. *Development-bank credit to domestic cloud/data-centre firms* (BNDES → Magalu Cloud R$300m, Scala R$180m) and *tax regimes* (Redata) — replicable but fiscally costly (R$5.2bn waiver estimate for 2026) and contested on water/energy grounds.
7. *Shared regional HPC access* (Turkey via EuroHPC/BSC AI Factory; Brazil via LNCC) for national-language model training — a low-capex path to a sovereign-language LLM before domestic GPU scale exists; Morocco's EU-association and Barcelona proximity make the Turkish route notable.
8. *Language-model niche* (Maritaca's Portuguese, Lelapa's African languages, Turkish LLMs): feasible at low cost with small teams, but none has yet turned linguistic specialisation into durable revenue or state anchor contracts — pair it with guaranteed public procurement.

What failed or stalled (lessons):
- Announcements outrunning power and fiscal reality (Kenya G42–Microsoft: grid share and take-or-pay guarantees).
- State-owned cloud ambitions abandoned for lack of capacity (South Africa 2021 draft → 2024 final policy).
- Policy-process credibility failures (South Africa's hallucinated-citation AI policy, ~1 year delay).
- Timelines slipping by ~12 months for GPU deployments (Cassava) and tax regimes (Redata), and supply-chain shocks (memory/RAM) hitting demand (Turkcell 2026).
- Fragmentation of sovereign-LLM efforts (Brazil: Maritaca vs SoberanIA vs PBIA vs China route; Turkey: T3 vs TÜBİTAK vs Havelsan) diluting scale.
