## 1. Overview: HQ, founding, leadership, ownership, state involvement, footprint

### Takeaway
OVHcloud (Roubaix, 1999) is a listed but tightly family-controlled infrastructure company with no state shareholder; Bleu (commercial launch Jan 2024) is a Capgemini–Orange joint venture reselling Microsoft Azure/M365 from French-controlled infrastructure and still not SecNumCloud-qualified; Mistral AI (Paris, April 2023) is a venture-backed company whose largest shareholders are now foreign strategic corporates (ASML since Sept 2025, Samsung leading the Sept 2026 round), with Bpifrance as a minority investor and lender. None of the three has a state majority owner.

## 2. Strategic mandate and national role

### Takeaway
None of the three has a statutory mandate. The state's leverage is indirect: the "cloud de confiance" doctrine (May 2021) and "Cloud au centre" rules create a protected segment for SecNumCloud-qualified offers, the SREN law (May 2024) and its 2026 implementing texts make that segment mandatory for particularly sensitive public data, and the state acts as anchor customer and co-investor (Bpifrance) for Mistral. Bleu and S3NS exist because the 2021 doctrine explicitly allowed US technology operated by French-controlled entities.

### Cited Findings
- SecNumCloud: created by ANSSI (2015–16); in 2021 the criterion of non-subjection to non-European law was added following the government's May 2021 national cloud strategy statement; v3.2 (2022) requires the operator to be French or European with mainly European capital and data centres in Europe; qualification attaches to an offer, not a company; process milestones J0–J3 — [Wikipedia FR, SecNumCloud](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/SecNumCloud).
- "Cloud au centre" doctrine rules (DINUM): cloud-first for new and substantially modified state systems (R6); deviations must be documented with DINUM for projects ≥ €1m (R7); checked within the review of projects ≥ €9m under decree 2019-1088 (R8); R9 requires SecNumCloud (or European equivalent) plus protection against non-EU authority access for data whose breach could harm public order, security, health or intellectual property, with transitional derogations limited to 12 months after an acceptable offer exists in France; R10 portability/reversibility; R15 supplier diversity — [numerique.gouv.fr, doctrine rules](https://www.numerique.gouv.fr/services/cloud/doctrine/).
- SREN law (21 May 2024) obliges administrations handling sensitive data to use providers not subject to extraterritorial law; a decree of 14 April 2026 sets the details and an order effective 22 Aug 2026 requires SecNumCloud compliance for data of "particular sensitivity" — [Wikipedia FR, SecNumCloud](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/SecNumCloud) (secondary; primary Légifrance texts not retrieved). Since the 2023 doctrine revision, taken up in SREN, the SecNumCloud obligation applies only when two cumulative criteria are met (legally protected secret + disclosure harmful to public order, security, health, life or IP) — [Banque des Territoires, Mar 2026](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/dependances-numeriques-health-data-hub-et-secnumcloud-au-menu-de-la-commission-denquete).
- EU level: SecNumCloud is not recognised EU-wide; EUCS negotiations were described as stalled with France and Germany on opposite sides (Oct 2025) — [Next.ink on Cour des comptes report, 31 Oct 2025](https://next.ink/207027/souverainete-numerique-la-cour-des-comptes-etrille-le-manque-de-cohesion-en-france/); by March 2026 the future EUCS scheme was reported as not including sovereignty criteria despite France's request — [Banque des Territoires](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/dependances-numeriques-health-data-hub-et-secnumcloud-au-menu-de-la-commission-denquete).
- Bleu's role: positioned by its parents as a "cloud de confiance" for the State, agencies, hospitals, local authorities and vital operators, conditional on SecNumCloud 3.2 — [Capgemini, Jan 2024](https://www.capgemini.com/news/press-releases/capgemini-and-orange-are-pleased-to-announce-the-launch-of-commercial-activities-of-bleu-their-future-cloud-de-confiance-platform/).
- OVHcloud's role: no mandate; it has built a defence vertical on its SecNumCloud products "to meet demand from several European defence ministries" (ANNOUNCED, April 2026) — [H1 FY26 results](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/newsroom/news/financial-results-h1-fy26/). Historically it was a partner (with Atos) in the 2013 state cloud plan — [Wikipedia FR](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/OVHcloud).
- Mistral's role: treated de facto as the national AI champion through procurement: Armed Forces framework agreement (notified 16 Dec 2025, announced 8 Jan 2026) steered by AMIAD and open to all armed forces, directorates and services plus CEA, ONERA and SHOM; value and duration not disclosed — [Next.ink, 9 Jan 2026](https://next.ink/brief-article/le-ministere-des-armees-signe-un-accord-cadre-avec-mistral/), [Acteurs Publics](https://acteurspublics.fr/articles/le-ministere-des-armees-signe-un-accord-cadre-avec-le-champion-francais-de-lia-mistral/), [The Star/Reuters, 8 Jan 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/01/08/france039s-armed-forces-ministry-awards-mistral-ai-framework-agreement). A €6m state agreement for 2026–27 (reported 4 Sept 2026, confirmed by minister David Amiel's office) embeds Mistral engineers in administrations, with priorities cybersecurity, fraud and justice backlogs; it followed breaches at DGFiP and the Education ministry in summer 2026 — [Siècle Digital, 4 Sept 2026](https://siecledigital.fr/2026/09/04/apres-le-piratage-des-impots-letat-fait-appel-aux-ingenieurs-de-mistral-pour-renforcer-ses-ministeres/).
- Official unease about concentration: Economy Minister Roland Lescure: "Si l'IA européenne se résume à Mistral, alors nous sommes fichus… on ne peut pas mettre tous nos œufs dans le même panier" — [Siècle Digital, 4 Sept 2026](https://siecledigital.fr/2026/09/04/apres-le-piratage-des-impots-letat-fait-appel-aux-ingenieurs-de-mistral-pour-renforcer-ses-ministeres/).
- AI Action Summit (Paris, 10–11 Feb 2025): Macron announced €109bn of AI investment (ANNOUNCED), 35 sites identified as ready for data centres; projects cited include a Brookfield 1 GW data centre at Cambrai (€20bn, 2030 target) and a 1 GW campus with the UAE "in development" — [Wikipedia FR, Sommet pour l'action sur l'IA](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sommet_pour_l%27action_sur_l%27intelligence_artificielle). ### Inferences
- The French model is "regulated demand + state as customer + minority public capital", not state ownership. The champions are private; the state shapes the market through ANSSI qualification and procurement doctrine.
- The €109bn figure is an aggregate of announced private/foreign intentions; of the pieces checked here, the Mistral-related ones have progressed furthest (44 MW site financed and reported operational; 1.4 GW campus still pre-construction).

## 3. Business model: products, customers, revenue streams, partnerships

### Takeaway
OVHcloud sells mostly bare-metal/private cloud to a long tail of 1.6–1.7m customers, with public cloud the growth engine and large corporates the weak spot. Bleu sells one thing — Microsoft Azure and M365 under French control — to regulated buyers, and its revenue is pre-qualification. Mistral monetises models through enterprise contracts, API/platform usage and, increasingly, compute capacity it owns, with a visible share of marquee customers also being shareholders or lenders.

### Inferences
- Organic vs state-created: OVHcloud (1999), Scaleway/Iliad and Mistral (2023) are market-born; Bleu, S3NS and NumSpot are responses to a regulatory niche the state defined in 2021–22. The state did not create a champion; it created a qualification, and consortia formed around it.
- The state's own behaviour (Health Data Hub on Azure, Education HR system) has undercut the doctrine it imposes, which is the central criticism of both the Cour des comptes and Parliament.

## 8. Critical assessment

### Takeaway
The French model has produced credible companies but not scale: OVHcloud is profitable only at the margin and tiny next to hyperscalers; the "trusted cloud" JVs are late and structurally dependent on US vendors; Mistral is well funded by European standards yet depends on foreign strategic capital and Nvidia hardware, and even ministers warn against relying on it alone.

### Inferences
- "Sovereign" in the French usage covers three different things that the report should keep apart: (i) legal immunity through ownership and control (SecNumCloud: OVHcloud, Outscale, NumSpot, S3NS, soon Bleu); (ii) technological autonomy (only the non-hyperscaler stacks, and even those run on US/Asian chips); (iii) capital control (weakening at Mistral as rounds grow).
- Mistral's move into owned compute raises capital intensity sharply (debt plus multi-billion commitments) at the same time as revenue is still in the hundreds of millions; sustainability depends on the compute-commitment model ("European Compute Units") and on enterprise customers that are in many cases also its investors.
- Bleu's delay has commercial cost: S3NS took the EDF reference, and public bodies bound by the Aug 2026 SREN order cannot place particularly sensitive data on Bleu until qualification.
