## 1. Yotta Data Services (Hiranandani Group) — candidate Indian National Cloud Champion

### Takeaway
Yotta is the largest single GPU supplier to the IndiaAI Mission and the most explicit "sovereign AI cloud" claimant among Indian operators, but it is a privately held, promoter-controlled, heavily leveraged company whose business is pivoting away from India (c.75% of GPU capacity now sold to international customers) and whose headline GPU numbers have repeatedly run far ahead of verified installations. It is better described as India's leading Nvidia-aligned neocloud/GPU lessor with a strong government-supply role than as a state-mandated national cloud champion; its 2024–25 Nasdaq SPAC route has no confirmed completion and has been superseded in company messaging by an India IPO plan (no DRHP confirmed as filed).

### Inferences
- The sequence "4,000 installed (c. early 2025) → 9,216 committed to IndiaAI → 'over 10,000 in production' (2026) → 40,000 'in four months' → 85,000 by March 2027 → one million in 3–5 years" shows a persistent pattern of forward-looking GPU claims; only the low-single-digit-thousands to c.10,000 range has any installed-base support in sources. The report should present every figure above 10,000 as planned/contracted, not deployed.
- The 2026 raise from HNIs rather than institutions, the absence of any SPAC closing announcement, and the switch to an India IPO narrative together suggest the Nasdaq SPAC did not complete as planned (or completed without the expected proceeds); this is an inference, not a confirmed fact.
- The 75/25 partner revenue split means Yotta's economics on partner-funded GPUs are closer to a hosting/operations fee than to owning a GPU cloud; headline "capex" numbers therefore overstate Yotta's own balance-sheet commitment and its own revenue capture.
- The shift to 75–90% international GPU customers at 50% higher prices undermines the "national champion" framing: marginal capacity is being exported as compute, and the IndiaAI subsidised rate (c.₹65–115/GPU-hour) is evidently less attractive than foreign demand.
- Sovereignty is at the data-centre/operator layer only: the stack is Nvidia hardware, Nvidia reference architecture and a Nvidia anchor tenant; Yotta's own IP is the Yntraa/Shakti orchestration layer built on open source.

- Outcome of the Cartica/Nidar merger (closed, terminated or lapsed) could not be confirmed; search budget ran out and DCD returned 403. Check SEC EDGAR for Cartica Acquisition Corp (CITE) 8-K filings after 28 Nov 2025.

The contrast with "4,000 installed" in the SEC filing is the core discrepancy.
- Actual operating IT load (MW) today, utilisation, GPU-hours delivered to IndiaAI, and receivables from government are undisclosed.
- Whether the Blackwell Ultra cluster went live in August 2026 is unconfirmed.
- Identity/credit quality of "Gorilla" (presumably Gorilla Technology Group) and the terms of the US$3.3bn arrangement were not verified against that company's filings.

## 2. Sify Technologies / Sify Infinit Spaces — candidate Indian National Cloud Champion

### Takeaway
Sify is a 25-plus-year-old, Nasdaq-listed, promoter-controlled Indian ICT integrator whose data-centre arm (Sify Infinit Spaces, 14 sites, 188 MW built, 129 MW sold) is a credible mid-sized colocation landlord to hyperscalers, banks and government, but it has no formal national mandate, is loss-making at group level (FY2025-26 loss INR 1,366m on revenue INR 44.9bn), holds no meaningful GPU-cloud or model position, and saw its ₹3,700 crore India IPO stall in September 2026 on a valuation gap. It is a domestic colocation and network player benefiting from localisation and hyperscaler demand, not a national cloud champion in the sense of a sovereign cloud platform.

### Inferences
- At 129 MW sold, SISL would have been billed as India's first listed pure-play data-centre company, but the SEBI approval lapses around 23 Jan 2027; if the IPO is not launched by then a re-filing is needed.
- Group-level losses despite 31% EBITDA growth reflect depreciation and interest on a capex programme (INR 13.3bn in FY26, "significantly higher" in FY27) that exceeds internal cash generation; the stalled IPO removes the planned ₹2,500 crore of fresh equity and raises funding risk.
- Sify's strategic role is as a neutral domestic colocation and network provider to hyperscalers and regulated Indian enterprises (BFSI under RBI localisation); its growth is driven by hyperscaler leasing rather than by a sovereign-cloud mandate. Calling it a "national cloud champion" is not supported: it sells space and power to the foreign clouds that dominate the Indian market.
- The digital-services segment (cloud, managed services) is shrinking (−2% revenue, −67% segment result), which is the part of the business that would have to grow for Sify to be a cloud (rather than colocation) champion.

## 8. Critical assessment: single national champion or distributed model? Hyperscaler dominance and GPU-cloud economics

### Takeaway
India does not have a single national cloud or AI champion; it runs a distributed model — DPI as demand anchor, a subsidised GPU marketplace on private capacity, and a portfolio of twelve model teams — while the bulk of new capacity is being built by conglomerates for US hyperscalers and AI labs. Malaysia and Indonesia each have one clear private, Nvidia-partnered champion (YTL; Indosat/Lintasarta) alongside newer, less mature state vehicles. Across all three, GPU-cloud economics look fragile: utilisation is low, domestic subsidised prices are well below export prices, and operators depend on partner financing or anchor tenants.

### Inferences
- Suggested classification for the report: India = "distributed DPI + subsidised compute marketplace + conglomerate landlords" (no single champion; Yotta is the leading sovereign-GPU supplier, Sarvam the leading model champion among twelve, Reliance/Tata/Adani/Airtel the scale builders for foreign platforms). Malaysia = "private champion (YTL) with state endorsement, plus a hyperscaler-led government cloud panel and an emerging state sovereign cloud". Indonesia = "telco-led private champion (Indosat/Lintasarta) with an SOE/sovereign-fund track (Telkom/Danantara) still at MoU stage".
- Organic vs state-created: all three champions (Yotta, YTL, Indosat/Lintasarta) are organic private groups (real estate, utilities, telecoms) that moved into GPU cloud with Nvidia's partner programme; the state role is endorsement, subsidy and reference demand, not ownership. The state-created vehicles (IndiaAI IBD, AI Malaysia Bhd, MCMC sovereign cloud, Danantara hub) are younger and smaller.
- "Sovereign" in all three countries means domestic location, domestic operator and local-language models; it does not extend to chips, systems or (mostly) base-model lineage — Nvidia is the single common dependency.
- Yotta fails a strict national-champion test on mandate (none exclusive), ownership (private), financial resilience (net debt c.US$1.1bn+, projected losses) and domestic orientation (exporting compute); it passes on scale of IndiaAI supply and sovereign-cloud positioning. Sify fails on platform ambition and AI relevance but passes on longevity, listing transparency and enterprise/hyperscaler colocation. Sarvam passes on mandate, DPI integration and openness, with reservations on model quality and subsidy dependence.
