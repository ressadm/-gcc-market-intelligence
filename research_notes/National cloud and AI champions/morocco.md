# Morocco: cloud and AI landscape, institutions and options for national cloud/AI capabilities (as of November 2026)

Research note conventions: "search summary" after a link means the fact was taken from a search-result digest of that page, not from a full read of the page (several Moroccan outlets returned HTTP 403/404 to direct fetches). These items are lower confidence and are worth a spot check before publication. Items read in full are unmarked.

## 1. Strategy and institutions (Maroc Digital 2030, Maroc IA 2030, DGSSI cloud regime, CNDP, state cloud programme)

### Takeaway
Morocco has a dense strategy stack (Maroc Digital 2030 from Sept 2024; "Maroc IA 2030 / AI Made in Morocco" from Jan 2026; a DGSSI sovereign-cloud decree from Nov 2024) but, as of late 2026, the state's own compute assets (50 MW Jazari-Root sovereign data centre in Rabat, 500 MW Igoudar Dakhla campus) are at land/convention/studies stage, not built. No AI law is in force; a digital law ("Digital X.0") has been stuck at the Secrétariat général du gouvernement, and Law 09-08 (data protection) has not been reformed.

**DGSSI cloud regime (sovereign cloud by regulation)**
- Decree on use of cloud providers by state entities and vital-importance infrastructures (IIV), drafted by DGSSI (under the Administration de la Défense nationale) on the basis of cybersecurity Law 05-20, published in Bulletin Officiel n° 7352 (late Nov 2024). Search results identify it as decree n° 2-24-921. — [Le360, 28 Nov 2024](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/cloud-souverain-le-maroc-franchit-un-pas-decisif-sur-le-terrain-juridique_Y37PYS2R5RDCXL7HS65VPE76Y4/); decree number per [Challenge.ma](https://challenge.ma/cloud-le-maroc-renforce-son-dispositif-292388/) (search summary)
- Two qualification levels. Level 1: required when entities/IIV host sensitive information systems in the cloud; provider must be a Moroccan-law company running all operating and administration systems on national territory. Level 2: additional legal/technical conditions for sensitive data, so that it is handled only by companies subject to Moroccan law without extraterritorial legal exposure. Foreign providers are therefore excluded from direct qualification. — [Le360, 28 Nov 2024](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/cloud-souverain-le-maroc-franchit-un-pas-decisif-sur-le-terrain-juridique_Y37PYS2R5RDCXL7HS65VPE76Y4/)
- Two-year transitional period during which entities may use non-qualified providers if no national offer exists (implies expiry around late Nov 2026, if counted from publication). Scope: state administrations, territorial collectivities, public establishments and enterprises, IIV operators. The cloud decision must be taken by the entity's highest authority with an impact study and risk analysis. — [Le360, 28 Nov 2024](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/cloud-souverain-le-maroc-franchit-un-pas-decisif-sur-le-terrain-juridique_Y37PYS2R5RDCXL7HS65VPE76Y4/)
- DGSSI subsequently adopted a qualification referential for cloud providers (IaaS/PaaS/SaaS) with periodic audits by qualified PASSI auditors. (search summary) — [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/cybersecurite-le-maroc-adopte-un-referentiel-strict-pour-la-qualification-des-prestataires-cloud_MKGUWQ5MTZBBNPAWH6SE5I4HEY/)
- BMI describes a 2021 rule requiring sensitive data to be hosted in Morocco as a demand driver. — [Le360 English (BMI report)](https://en.le360.ma/economy/moroccos-data-center-capacity-set-to-surge-as-ai-and-cloud-investment-accelerates-report-says_MYVV3TMHRJHN7MNV3WDYZSZERQ/)

**CNDP / Law 09-08 / EU adequacy**
- The European Commission has not issued an adequacy decision for Morocco; Law 09-08 (2009) remains in force with no amending legislation enacted as of 2026, though the CNDP has signalled intent to move toward a GDPR-aligned framework. (search summary) — [CMS Expert Guide, Morocco](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-data-protection-and-cyber-security-laws/morocco)
- Morocco is the 55th State Party to Council of Europe Convention 108 (in force for Morocco 1 Sept 2019). — [Council of Europe](https://www.coe.int/en/web/data-protection/-/welcome-to-morocco-55th-state-party-to-convention-108-)

### Inferences
- The regulatory design (Moroccan-law entity, all operations on national soil, protection from extraterritorial law) is close to France's SecNumCloud logic. It creates a reserved public-sector/IIV market for domestic operators (inwi, Maroc Telecom, N+ONE, Medasys, a future state cloud), and pushes hyperscalers toward hosted/partner models (Oracle hosted by N+ONE).
- The transitional period nominally ends around Nov 2026, yet no public list of qualified cloud providers was found. Either qualification is running behind the legal timetable or the list is not easily discoverable; in both cases enforceability of the regime in 2027 is an open execution question.
- The state is building its own sovereign cloud/data centre (Jazari-Root) rather than designating an existing operator as champion. That is a "state cloud" model (closer to Senegal/Rwanda) layered over a regulated private market, not a single-national-champion model.
- Strategy numbers are inconsistent across official communication (100,000 vs 200,000 AI talents; 240,000 jobs by 2026 vs 2030). A report writer should cite targets with their source and date.

### Gaps
- No official DGSSI list of qualified cloud providers was found; I could not confirm which, if any, providers hold Level 1/Level 2 qualification. dgssi.gov.ma was not directly consulted.
- The exact decree text (data classification scheme, exact transition end date) was not read at source (Bulletin Officiel).
- ADD's specific role in cloud, and the content/status of the D4SD (Digital for Sustainable Development) hub with UNDP, were not verified. One secondary source ties Dakhla's power plant to "the D4SD program", which looks like a conflation and is unconfirmed: [We Are Tech](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/tech/morocco-to-build-renewable-powered-data-center-and-ai-institute-in-dakhla).
- Whether "Digital X.0" was adopted after September 2026 is unknown.
- Morocco's status under Convention 108+ (CETS 223) is unconfirmed.
- JAZARI Root: one source says the Rabat hub "launched" in Jan 2026 (the institute), while the 50 MW Jazari-Root data centre is scheduled 2027–2029. These are different things under the same name; sources do not always distinguish them.

## 2. Potential champions and their actual capabilities

### Takeaway
No Moroccan entity currently qualifies as a credible national cloud or AI champion at international scale. The domestic field is made up of telco-hosted colocation/cloud (inwi, Maroc Telecom, Orange Maroc), two specialist operators (N+ONE, Medasys), a strong university compute/research pole (UM6P, backed by OCP), and state financial arms (CDG, Fonds Mohammed VI) that appear as co-sponsors of projects rather than operators. The largest telco, Maroc Telecom, is majority-owned by the UAE's e& and is described by trade press as lacking a significant digital-infrastructure portfolio beyond its networks.

### Inferences
- "Champion" test against the evidence: (a) Maroc Telecom has the balance sheet (≈$1bn annual capex) and the African footprint (Moov Africa in ~10 markets) but minimal cloud assets and foreign majority control (e&), which complicates a "sovereign" role under the DGSSI Level 2 logic (extraterritorial exposure). (b) inwi (Al Mada) has the most developed certified domestic hosting footprint and Moroccan ownership but is small in compute terms. (c) N+ONE is the neutral colocation layer hyperscalers actually use. (d) UM6P/OCP is the only actor with real HPC and research depth, but it is an academic pole, with only 32 data-centre-class GPUs cited in available sources. None combines capital, compute, platform software and customers.
- The most plausible Moroccan "champion" structure is a consortium, not a company: state sovereign cloud (Jazari-Root) + qualified domestic operators (inwi/N+ONE/Medasys/IAM) + UM6P for research compute + CDG/Fonds Mohammed VI as capital. This points to the Indonesia (telco + Nvidia) or state-cloud models rather than the Gulf sovereign-fund model (G42/Humain), since Morocco lacks comparable sovereign capital.
- Maroc Telecom's e& ownership is a channel for Gulf involvement (e& group data-centre/cloud capabilities), but no concrete e&, G42/Core42 or Khazna project in Morocco was found.

### Gaps
- No verifiable MW figures per operator (IAM, inwi, N+ONE, Medasys). Medasys and Atos/Eviden Morocco capabilities were not researched in detail.
- OCP Group digital/data-centre initiatives (OCP Green data centres, InnovX), UM6P College of Computing, UM6P Ventures, Ithmar Capital, Al Mada and Attijariwafa digital arms, and startups (ToumAI, AtlasIA, Sowit): no specific 2026 facts were retrieved; treat as unresearched rather than absent.
- No evidence was found of any 2026 GPU upgrade at Toubkal/ASCC.
- No commercial "MT Cloud" offer details from Maroc Telecom were found; the Google Cloud–Maroc Telecom hub is unconfirmed (one search digest also attached a "$3bn" value to it, which I could not trace to a reliable source).

As of November 2026 the only operational hyperscaler infrastructure is Oracle's Casablanca public cloud region (opened April 2026, hosted by N+ONE). Every large AI/data-centre announcement (Dakhla 500 MW, Nexus/Naver/Nvidia, Iozera 386 MW, Rabat 50 MW sovereign DC) remains at MoU, convention, land or study stage, with delays and scope reductions already visible.

## 4. Enabling conditions (energy, connectivity, water, market size, talent, 5G, data-law fit with EU, African access)

### Takeaway
Morocco's structural assets are real: proximity and cable links to Europe, renewables at roughly 40–46% of installed capacity with a 52% 2030 target, a large French-speaking offshoring workforce, 5G live since Nov 2025, and telco/bank reach into West Africa. Binding constraints are grid capacity around Casablanca, water stress, a very small installed data-centre base, no EU adequacy decision, and talent leakage.

The safe statement is that installed third-party capacity is small (single- to low-double-digit MW by conservative counts, with far higher numbers in commercial market reports) and that reliable installed-MW data do not exist publicly.
- The "hub for Europe" thesis depends on (a) dedicated renewable supply bypassing a constrained grid, (b) low latency via Medusa/EllaLink/Estepona–Tétouan, and (c) European customers' willingness to place workloads outside the EU without an adequacy decision. For AI training/inference of non-personal data, (c) is less binding; for regulated personal-data workloads it is a real barrier.
- Dakhla's location adds legal/reputational risk for EU-facing demand given Western Sahara's contested status and past CJEU litigation over EU–Morocco agreements; Nouaceur/Casablanca or the north (Tétouan/Nador cable landings) do not carry that risk.
- The "hub for Africa" thesis has a stronger demand anchor (Moov Africa, Moroccan banks) but West African data-localisation rules and long latency to sub-Saharan users limit a pure export-of-hosting model; service exports (AI/BPO built on offshoring base, MAD 27bn exports in 2025) look more transferable than raw compute exports.

## 6. Comparators relevant for transfer

### Takeaway
The closest transferable precedents are telco-led sovereign AI clouds with Nvidia (Indonesia) and pan-African private operators (Cassava); the Kenya Microsoft/G42 case is the cautionary tale for hyperscale projects dependent on host-state power and offtake guarantees.

### Cited Findings
- Indonesia: Indosat Ooredoo Hutchison partnered with Nvidia (announced Feb 2024); its subsidiary Lintasarta became an Nvidia Cloud Partner and launched "GPU Merdeka", a sovereign GPU-as-a-service cloud. Operational, not just planned. (search summary) — [DCD](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/indosats-lintasarta-launches-ai-cloud-gpu-merdeka-in-indonesia); [Nvidia blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/indonesia-tech-leaders-sovereign-ai)
- Cassava Technologies: announced March 2025 an Nvidia-powered "AI factory" starting in South Africa; plan of 12,000 GPUs across Africa over three to four years starting with 3,000 in South Africa; live in South Africa per a 2026 company release, with expansion planned to Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt and Morocco. (search summary) — [African Business, Mar 2025](https://african.business/2025/03/technology-information/cassava-and-nvidia-to-launch-africas-first-ai-factory); [Verdict](https://www.verdict.co.uk/cassava-african-ai-factory/); [Cassava Technologies](https://www.cassavatechnologies.com/?p=4627)
- Kenya: the $1bn Microsoft/G42 geothermal data centre at Olkaria (announced 2024) was reported in May 2026 as stalled over power capacity and Microsoft/G42's request for government-guaranteed capacity payments; the Kenyan government says the project is not cancelled and talks continue; a smaller 100 MW first phase may be revived. No later resolution found. (search summary) — [Semafor, 6 May 2026](https://semafor.com/article/05/06/2026/energy-shortfall-problem-scuppers-kenyas-1b-microsoft-data-center); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/microsoft-g42-kenya-data-center-delay-offtake); [The Star (Kenya), 10 May 2026](https://the-star.co.ke/news/2026-05-10-state-microsoft-g42-data-centre-project-remains-on-track-despite-power-concerns)
- Oracle's African footprint for context: Johannesburg region operating, Nairobi announced, Casablanca opened April 2026. — [Hespress, 8 Apr 2026](https://fr.hespress.com/468760-468760.html)

### Inferences
- Indonesia model transfer: requires a domestically controlled telco willing to buy GPUs and a home market large enough to absorb them. inwi (Moroccan-owned) fits the sovereignty test better than Maroc Telecom (e&-controlled), but Morocco's domestic AI demand is far smaller than Indonesia's. A Naver-operated Nexus platform is a foreign-operator variant of this model, which weakens the "sovereign" claim.
- Kenya lesson: hyperscale anchor tenants ask the host state for power and offtake guarantees. Morocco's use of dedicated renewable PPAs (TAQA) and self-generation rules is a partial mitigant, but fiscal guarantees for Dakhla-scale capacity would be a significant contingent liability.
- A Gulf-style model (state capital buying frontier compute at scale) does not transfer without external capital; the realistic Gulf link is as investor/partner (e& via Maroc Telecom; possible sovereign-fund co-investment through Fonds Mohammed VI or Ithmar), and none is confirmed.

### Gaps
- Malaysia (YTL–Nvidia), Senegal and Rwanda state clouds, and Egypt were not researched in this pass.
