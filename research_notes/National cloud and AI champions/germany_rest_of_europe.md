## 1. Deutsche Telekom / T-Systems — telecom-led National Cloud Champion (overview, mandate, business model, technology, financials, achievements)

### Takeaway
Deutsche Telekom (DT) is the closest thing Germany has to a state-anchored cloud champion: the federal state and KfW together hold 28.6% (30 Jun 2026), T-Systems won 70% of the federal "AI cloud" contract in May 2026, and its Munich "Industrial AI Cloud" (up to 10,000 Nvidia Blackwell GPUs, live Feb 2026) is reported sold out. But there is no formal sovereign mandate, T-Systems is a small (€4.1bn revenue, ~10% margin) part of a €119bn group, T Cloud Public revenue is only about €200m, and the "sovereign" portfolio leans on Nvidia hardware and, for one product line, Google Cloud technology.

### Cited Findings
**Ownership and scale**
- ACTUAL: Federal Republic's holding including KfW was 28.6% at 30 Jun 2026 (28.3% at 31 Dec 2025). The often-quoted 27.8% is the 31 Dec 2024 figure; the rise is due to share cancellations after buy-backs, not purchases. [snippet] — [DT interim report Q2 2026, "The T-Share"](https://report.telekom.com/interim-report-q2-2026/to-our-shareholders/the-t-share.html); [DT annual report 2025, The T-Share](https://report.telekom.com/annual-report-2025/to-our-shareholders/the-t-share.html)
- ACTUAL: Split at 30 Jun 2026: federal government 14.22%, KfW 14.37%, free float 71.41%. [snippet] — [DT shareholder structure](https://www.telekom.com/en/investor-relations/company/shareholder-structure)
- ACTUAL FY2025 group: net revenue €119.1bn (+2.9%), adjusted EBITDA AL €44.2bn, free cash flow AL €19.5bn, cash capex €19.3bn (€16.9bn ex-spectrum). 2026 guidance: adjusted EBITDA AL about €47.4bn, FCF AL about €19.8bn. **T-Systems (Systems Solutions segment) financials**

If DT builds, it would be in Bavaria. EU tender launched July 2026; winners expected early 2027. [fetched] — [Light Reading, 9 Nov 2026](https://lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/dt-builds-out-ai-factory-but-still-undecided-on-eu-gigafactory-bid)

### Inferences
- DT's champion status is de facto, not de jure: the state is a minority shareholder, the AI factory was explicitly privately financed, and public work is won by tender. The strongest "state-created" element is procurement (the 70% lot).
- Cloud is financially marginal to DT. T Cloud Public at roughly €200m is about 5% of T-Systems revenue and under 0.2% of group revenue; the US mobile business dominates the group. This limits how much "champion" weight the cloud unit carries inside DT's capital allocation.
- The €1bn Industrial AI Cloud figure is a partnership valuation, not a disclosed DT capex number; with Polarise as data-centre partner and 12 MW initial capacity, DT's own balance-sheet exposure is likely well below €1bn. Tucherpark 2 (about €250m for a further ~8 MW) gives a better sense of unit cost.
- Technology sovereignty is partial: GPUs and AI software are Nvidia; one sovereign line is Google technology under T-Systems control; SAP's layer is German.
- DT's reluctance on the gigafactory, despite being "sold out" at 12 MW, suggests commercial demand for sovereign GPU capacity in Germany is real at tens of MW but unproven at hundreds.

### Gaps
- Open Telekom Cloud's technical basis (OpenStack, historically built with Huawei hardware and software, hosted in Magdeburg/Biere and the Netherlands) is researcher background knowledge; it was not re-verified in this session and no source is cited. The writer should verify before stating it.
- No disclosed revenue for the Industrial AI Cloud, the Google-based sovereign cloud, or public-sector vs private split at T-Systems.
- T-Systems headcount, data-centre count and total MW not retrieved.
- Details and date of the reported data-centre carve-out not verified.
- DT's source count: 14 distinct URLs above, of which 8 are DT/T-Systems/Google primary documents.

## 2. Schwarz Group / Schwarz Digits / STACKIT — private-conglomerate cloud champion

### Takeaway
Schwarz Digits is a privately funded, founder/foundation-controlled attempt at a European hyperscaler: €2.2bn divisional revenue in FY2025, an announced €11bn Lübbenau campus (first phase due end-2027) and a further announced €5.6bn site near Rostock, plus the anchor role in the Cohere–Aleph Alpha deal. The honest caveat is that external cloud revenue is tiny: Manager Magazin, citing internal documents, puts STACKIT's cloud revenue at €46.5m in FY2025/26 with over 90% of Schwarz Digits revenue coming from the group itself.

### Cited Findings
**Overview and ownership**
- STACKIT brand developed in 2018 inside Schwarz IT; used group-internally from 2019; opened to external customers in 2022; IT/digital activities bundled as Schwarz Digits in 2023. Legal entity renamed in 2026 to Schwarz Digits Cloud GmbH & Co. KG (formerly STACKIT GmbH & Co. KG); HQ Am Campus 1, Bad Friedrichshall; parent Schwarz Group, Neckarsulm (Lidl, Kaufland; 600,000+ employees). Company states it is "independent of external investors". Management: Christian Müller, Robin Hermann, Bernd Wagner. [fetched] — [STACKIT facts page, reviewed 20 Aug 2026](https://stackit.com/en/facts); [industriemagazin.at](https://industriemagazin.at/industrie/lidl-cloud-stackit-schwarz-gruppe-ki-chips/)
- Schwarz Digits co-CEOs: Christian Müller and Rolf Schumann. [fetched] — [Schwarz Digits press release, 17 Nov 2025](https://schwarz-digits.de/en/presse/archive/2025/ground-breaking-ceremony-in-luebbenau-schwarz-digits-invests-11-billion-euros-in-europe-s-digital-sovereignty)

**Financials**
- ACTUAL FY2025: Schwarz Group revenue €185.6bn (+5.8%); group investment about €9bn in 2025, more than €10bn planned for the current year. Schwarz Digits revenue €2.2bn (+15.8%), implying about €1.9bn in FY2024. This is the whole digital division, mostly intra-group IT, not external cloud revenue. [snippet] — [Schwarz Group press release 2026](https://gruppe.schwarz/en/press/archive/2026/companies-of-schwarz-group-generate-185.6-billion-euros-in-revenue-and-drive-growth-with-investments-in-excess-of-10-billion-euros); [onlinemarktplatz.de](https://onlinemarktplatz.de/268009/schwarz-gruppe-umsatz/)
- REPORTED (not confirmed by Schwarz): Manager Magazin, from internal documents: STACKIT cloud revenue €46.5m in FY2025/26; plan of €177.3m for the current year; over 90% of Schwarz Digits revenue is intra-group; parts of core group applications, including Lidl Plus, still run on Azure or Google Cloud. [fetched, secondary] — [industriemagazin.at summarising Manager Magazin](https://industriemagazin.at/industrie/lidl-cloud-stackit-schwarz-gruppe-ki-chips/)

**Infrastructure**

- ANNOUNCED end-Aug 2026: Dummerstorf (near Rostock) investment of up to €5.6bn by 2033. ACTUAL July 2026: Bad Friedrichshall campus opened (15.5 ha, five buildings). [fetched, secondary] — [industriemagazin.at](https://industriemagazin.at/industrie/lidl-cloud-stackit-schwarz-gruppe-ki-chips/)

- AI: Schwarz held more than 20% of Aleph Alpha and is providing €500m of "structured financing" to lead Cohere's Series E, in return for the combined company running on STACKIT (see section 3). [fetched] — [TechCrunch, 25 Apr 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/25/why-cohere-is-merging-with-aleph-alpha/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/cohere-takes-over-aleph-alpha-shortly-after-the-german-startup-ousted-its-original-founder/)
- Cybersecurity: Schwarz acquired XM Cyber in 2021; in July 2026 CrowdStrike agreed to buy XM Cyber IP (45+ patents, source code) for $145m in cash and stock, with XM Cyber remaining independent. [fetched, secondary] — [industriemagazin.at](https://industriemagazin.at/industrie/lidl-cloud-stackit-schwarz-gruppe-ki-chips/)

### Inferences
- Schwarz is the most "organic" (non-state) champion in this set: self-funded from retail cash flow, no subsidy named, state role limited to procurement and political endorsement.
- The €11bn and €5.6bn figures are multi-year announced envelopes; nothing confirms how much has been spent. Against roughly €46.5m of reported external cloud revenue, the build is a very long-dated bet that the anchor tenant will be the group itself plus Cohere and public-sector frameworks.
- STACKIT is the most technologically independent of the German offerings at the IaaS layer (own open-source stack, own data centres), but GPUs will be US-designed and its AI layer is now Canadian (Cohere).
- Internal workloads still on Azure/Google (reported) undercut the "we eat our own cooking" narrative.

### Gaps
- Schwarz Group ownership detail (Dieter Schwarz Stiftung gGmbH holding the large majority of economic interest, with voting control in a separate Schwarz Unternehmenstreuhand KG) is researcher background knowledge; not verified in this session, no source cited.
- No audited external STACKIT revenue, customer count, headcount, or profitability. The €46.5m figure rests on one magazine report relayed by a trade outlet.
- Existing operational MW and GPU counts for STACKIT not found. "100,000 GPUs" is a ceiling for a future phase, not an order.
- Whether the Lübbenau timeline is on track as of Nov 2026 was not checked.
- Source count: 11 distinct URLs, 5 primary (Schwarz Digits, Schwarz Group, STACKIT, OpenInfra).

## 3. Aleph Alpha — contested National AI Champion (overview, pivot, cap table, financials, 2025–26 restructuring)

### Takeaway
Aleph Alpha no longer functions as an independent national AI champion. It abandoned frontier-model development in September 2024, its founder was pushed out in 2025–26, and on 24 April 2026 Canada's Cohere agreed to take it over at a 90/10 ownership split, with a definitive agreement signed 16 September 2026; closing had not been confirmed as of mid-November 2026. Its headline "$500m" 2023 round reportedly contained only about €110m of equity.

### Cited Findings
**Overview**
- Founded 2019 in Heidelberg by Jonas Andrulis and Samuel Weinbach; about 250 staff at the time of the Cohere deal; products centre on the PhariaAI suite; earlier Luminous model family. [fetched] — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/cohere-takes-over-aleph-alpha-shortly-after-the-german-startup-ousted-its-original-founder/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/25/why-cohere-is-merging-with-aleph-alpha/)

**2023 Series B: announced vs real**
- ANNOUNCED 6 Nov 2023: Series B of "over $500m" from seven investors led by Innovation Park Artificial Intelligence (Ipai, Heilbronn, Dieter Schwarz Foundation-backed), Bosch Ventures and Schwarz Group companies, with SAP, HPE and others. [snippet] — [CNBC, 6 Nov 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/06/aleph-alpha-raises-500-million-to-build-a-european-rival-to-openai.html); [Bloomberg, 6 Nov 2023](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-11-06/german-giants-sap-bosch-invest-over-500-million-aleph-alpha-an-openai-rival)
- The total included applied research, joint business development and pre-committed licence agreements with consortium members, not only cash equity. [snippet] — [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2023/11/06/aleph-alpha-raises-over-half-a-billion-in-series-b-funding-for-ai-development); [The Decoder, 2023](https://the-decoder.com/aleph-alpha-raises-more-than-500-million-in-series-b-financing-round/)
- REPORTED: only about €110m arrived as equity; the rest was revenue guarantees and commitments from investors. The search summary did not attribute this cleanly to one outlet; it appears in secondary analyses of the Cohere deal and was originally a German press finding. Treat as reported, not disclosed. [snippet] — [heise](https://heise.de/-11253050); [High-Tech Investing (Substack)](https://hightechinvesting.substack.com/p/aleph-alpha-and-cohere-the-quiet)
- Cap table: Schwarz held "more than 20%" / "about 20%" via Schwarz Digits before the deal and gained growing influence over strategy and personnel during 2025. Full cap table undisclosed. [fetched] — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/cohere-takes-over-aleph-alpha-shortly-after-the-german-startup-ousted-its-original-founder/)

**Financials**
- REPORTED: 2023 revenue €945k (double 2022, against an internal target of €5.5m); net loss rose from €8.3m (2022) to €18.9m (2023). 2024 target of $20m revenue reportedly missed. [snippet; attribution to outlet not verified] — [Fortune, 7 Sep 2024](https://fortune.com/europe/2024/09/07/aleph-alpha-germany-ai-startup-tech-silicon-valley/); [xpert.digital (weaker source, for the 2024 miss)](https://xpert.digital/en/cohere-swallows-aleph-alpha/)
- TechCrunch characterises Aleph Alpha as having "generated little revenue and significant losses". For comparison Cohere reported $240m ARR in 2025 and was last valued at $6.8bn. [fetched] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/25/why-cohere-is-merging-with-aleph-alpha/)

**Pivot and leadership timeline**
- Sep 2024: pivot from building large models to the PhariaAI platform ("operating system" for generative AI in enterprises and government); Andrulis said a purely European LLM was no longer a viable business model. Aug 2025: Reto Spoerri (ex-head of Lidl's online business) became co-CEO. Oct 2025: Andrulis gave up the CEO role, offered an advisory-board seat; Ilhan Scheer became second co-CEO. Early 2026: about 50 jobs cut; Andrulis told NZZ he was "out", NZZ reported he had been pushed out; he then announced a new AI start-up with Roland Berger. [fetched] — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/cohere-takes-over-aleph-alpha-shortly-after-the-german-startup-ousted-its-original-founder/)

**Cohere transaction**
- ANNOUNCED 24 Apr 2026: Cohere to take over Aleph Alpha; combined value about $20bn per the term sheet (Handelsblatt); Aleph Alpha shareholders to receive one Cohere share per nine held (CNBC), i.e. roughly 10% of the combined company; Cohere keeps its name and CEO Aidan Gomez; dual HQ Canada/Germany. Schwarz Group leads Cohere's Series E with €500m (about $600m) described as "structured financing", and expects the combined company to run on STACKIT. Germany said it plans to be an anchor customer and prioritise sovereign AI in procurement; Aleph Alpha brings contracts with BMDS and the state of Baden-Württemberg. Both governments back the deal; context is a new Canada–Germany "Sovereign Technology Alliance". [fetched] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/25/why-cohere-is-merging-with-aleph-alpha/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/cohere-takes-over-aleph-alpha-shortly-after-the-german-startup-ousted-its-original-founder/); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/127656-cohere-acquires-aleph-alpha-and-raises-600-million-from-its-top-backer/)

## 10. Finland — is there a credible champion?

### Takeaway
Finland's strength is public supercomputing, not companies: CSC hosts LUMI and, from 2027, LUMI-AI (€387.8m contract signed 31 Aug 2026, AMD-based, built by Bull), but its best-known AI company Silo AI was sold to AMD in 2024 and no Finnish cloud or model champion exists.

### Cited Findings
- LUMI-AI: EuroHPC JU signed a €387.8m contract with Bull on 31 Aug 2026 (acquisition, installation, maintenance); AMD Instinct MI430X GPUs and 256-core 6th-gen EPYC CPUs; hosted at CSC's Kajaani data centre; funded 50% EuroHPC (Digital Europe) and 50% LUMI AI Factory consortium (Finland lead, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Norway, Poland); about ten times the AI capacity of current LUMI; available to users in 2027; described as the largest European procurement of a dedicated AI system. [fetched] — [EuroHPC JU, 31 Aug 2026](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/eurohpc-ju-signs-contract-deploy-lumi-ai-supercomputer-2026-08-31_en); [Techzine](https://techzine.eu/news/infrastructure/143930/bull-builds-e387-8-m-lumi-ai-supercomputer-in-finland)

[snippet] — [SRV](https://www.srv.fi/en/srv-as-a-company/srv-articles/business-premises/building-a-data-center-starts-at-the-drawing-board/)
- Nebius (Amsterdam-HQ) owns its main data centre in Mäntsälä, Finland. [fetched, tertiary] — [Wikipedia: Nebius Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebius_Group)

## 11. EU-level programmes (AI Factories and Gigafactories, CADA, IPCEI-CIS, Gaia-X, EuroStack)

### Takeaway
EU instruments now fund public compute at meaningful scale (19 AI Factories, a gigafactory tender launched July 2026) and a sovereignty-in-procurement law is proposed (Cloud and AI Development Act, June 2026), but none has yet produced a commercial champion; Gaia-X stands as the cautionary example of a standards body mistaken for a cloud.

### Cited Findings
- AI Factories: 19 selected in three waves (Dec 2024: Finland, Germany, Greece, Italy, Luxembourg, Spain, Sweden; Mar 2025: Austria, Bulgaria, France, Germany, Poland, Slovenia; Oct 2025: Czechia, Lithuania, Netherlands, Romania, Spain, Poland), plus 13 Antennas (incl. Switzerland and the UK). €10bn EU plus national investment in supercomputing and AI Factories over 2021–27; at least 9 new AI-optimised systems. [fetched] — [European Commission, updated 20 Oct 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-factories)
- AI Gigafactories: call for tenders launched late July 2026 for up to seven, each with over 100,000 advanced AI processors; industry-led with up to €10bn EU and national funding, expected to unlock at least €20bn private investment; winners expected early 2027. Nothing has been awarded. [fetched] — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-factories); [EuroHPC JU](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/eurohpc-ju-signs-contract-deploy-lumi-ai-supercomputer-2026-08-31_en); [Light Reading](https://lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/dt-builds-out-ai-factory-but-still-undecided-on-eu-gigafactory-bid)
- Industry criticism of gigafactory terms: DT cites a 60% public-reservation requirement and expected 10–15% effective utilisation. [fetched] — [Light Reading, 9 Nov 2026](https://lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/dt-builds-out-ai-factory-but-still-undecided-on-eu-gigafactory-bid)
- Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA): Commission proposal COM(2026) 502, listed as published 3 June 2026; procedure 2026/0138(COD); three pillars (capacity build-out with a target of tripling EU data-centre capacity, research support, and a cloud/AI sovereignty framework that sets requirements for providers and governs access to public procurement). It is a proposal, not law. Sources conflict on dates (one law-firm blog gives 2 July 2026), and one low-quality site claims Official Journal publication, which contradicts all others and should be ignored. [snippet] — [European Commission library](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/proposal-cloud-and-ai-development-act-cada); [EP Legislative Observatory](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1903654); [Covington, Global Policy Watch](https://globalpolicywatch.com/2026/06/the-eu-cloud-and-ai-development-act-in-depth)
- Gaia-X: initiated 2019 by ministers Altmaier and Le Maire; AISBL formed by 22 Franco-German founders (incl. DT, SAP, Siemens, Bosch, OVHcloud, Orange, Atos); explicitly not intended to be a cloud provider or to compete with hyperscalers; founding member Scaleway quit in Nov 2021; open-source advocates warned of capture by large firms ("Trojan horse" for big tech, Euractiv 2020); Politico (Oct 2021) reported infighting and delays. Outputs are specifications, labels and a catalogue (600+ services from 15 providers at Nov 2025; 180+ data spaces claimed). The Wikipedia article carries tags for promotional tone, so status claims are the organisation's own. [fetched, tertiary] — [Wikipedia: Gaia-X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaia-X)

### Inferences
- Gaia-X lessons: (1) a federation and labelling scheme does not create supply-side capacity; (2) admitting incumbents and US hyperscalers into governance diluted the purpose and drove out challengers; (3) without committed public demand, compliance labels did not shift procurement. CADA's procurement pillar and Germany's EU-anchored tender design are direct responses to lesson (3).
- EU compute policy is effective at building public research systems (LUMI-AI) but the gigafactory design shows a tension between public-access obligations and private bankability.

### Gaps
- IPCEI-CIS (researcher background: approved Dec 2023, up to €1.2bn state aid from seven member states, 19 companies incl. DT and SAP; "8ra" initiative) could not be verified; Commission press page did not render.
- EuroStack debate and Bertelsmann Stiftung report (researcher background: Feb 2025 report estimating about €300bn over ten years) not retrieved.

## 12. Critical assessment across the set (dependence on US technology; organic vs state-created; who is a real champion)

### Takeaway
Germany has two credible cloud champions with different models (DT: part-state, integrator-led; Schwarz: private, self-funded, infrastructure-led) and no independent AI-model champion; in the other five countries there is one emerging infrastructure champion (Nscale, UK), one promising consortium (Sferical AI, Sweden), and otherwise public research assets, not companies.

### Cited Findings
- Every "sovereign" German offering depends on US technology at some layer: DT's AI factory on Nvidia GPUs and software, with a Perplexity inference layer [DT press release](https://www.telekom.com/de/newsroom/aktuelles/medieninformationen/2025/11/fuer-ein-souveraenes-deutschland); T-Systems Sovereign Cloud on Google Cloud [Google docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/t-systems-sovereign-cloud/docs/product-overview); Delos on Microsoft Azure with OpenAI models [SAP News](https://news.sap.com/2025/09/sap-openai-partner-launch-sovereign-openai-germany/); Schwarz's internal apps partly on Azure/Google and its security and AI partners US/Canadian [industriemagazin.at](https://industriemagazin.at/industrie/lidl-cloud-stackit-schwarz-gruppe-ki-chips/).
- The German state chose a foreign-controlled combination for its AI layer: Cohere shareholders get about 90% of the combined company with Aleph Alpha. [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/cohere-takes-over-aleph-alpha-shortly-after-the-german-startup-ousted-its-original-founder/)
- Commercial traction beyond government is thin where measurable: T Cloud Public about €200m target for 2026 [Yahoo Finance call summary](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/deutsche-telekom-q4-earnings-call-155923050.html); STACKIT external cloud €46.5m reported [industriemagazin.at](https://industriemagazin.at/industrie/lidl-cloud-stackit-schwarz-gruppe-ki-chips/); Aleph Alpha under €1m in 2023 [Fortune](https://fortune.com/europe/2024/09/07/aleph-alpha-germany-ai-startup-tech-silicon-valley/). The exception is GPU capacity: DT's first Munich phase is reported sold out [Light Reading](https://lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/dt-builds-out-ai-factory-but-still-undecided-on-eu-gigafactory-bid).
- UK policy explicitly rejects self-sufficiency as the definition of sovereignty. [UK Compute Roadmap](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68791dc82bad77c3dae4dddb/uk_compute_roadmap_print-version.pdf)
- Swiss public model builders disclaim frontier competition. [CSCS](https://www.cscs.ch/science/computer-science-hpc/2026/apertus-15-building-the-next-generation-of-open-ai-infrastructure)

### Inferences
- Suggested classification for the report writer:
  - Germany: DT/T-Systems = National Cloud Champion (telecom-led, part-state-owned, procurement-anchored). Schwarz Digits/STACKIT = National Cloud Champion (private conglomerate, organic). Aleph Alpha = failed/absorbed National AI Champion; successor is Cohere (Canadian) on STACKIT. Helsing = sector (defence) AI champion. SAP = integrator, not infrastructure champion.
  - UK: Nscale = emerging infrastructure champion, privately and US-financed, with status of flagship Stargate UK uncertain. No model champion (DeepMind is US-owned).
  - Sweden: Sferical AI/Wallenberg consortium = credible but pre-operational; Telia = sovereign services layer on foreign-owned infrastructure.
  - Switzerland, Netherlands, Finland: no corporate champion; public research infrastructure and open models (Apertus, GPT-NL, LUMI-AI) fill the role. Swisscom is a sovereign hosting utility.
- Organic vs state-created: Schwarz, Sferical/Wallenberg, Nscale and Lovable are organic/private; DT and Swisscom are legacy state-linked incumbents; Apertus, GPT-NL, LUMI-AI and the AI Factories are state-created; Aleph Alpha was privately founded but politically elevated beyond its capital base.
- Why Aleph Alpha failed to keep pace (synthesis of section 3): under-capitalised by one to two orders of magnitude; headline funding was largely commitments; corporate backers steered it to integration work; state demand arrived late and in small tickets; it conceded the model race publicly in Sept 2024 and lost its founder in 2025–26.
- Announced capacity far exceeds operating capacity across the region: operating sovereign AI capacity that could be confirmed is DT's 12 MW (rising to 20 MW); Lübbenau (200 MW first phase, end-2027), Strängnäs (up to 750 MW), gigafactories and UK AI Growth Zones are all plans.

### Gaps
- No independent market-share data (e.g. Synergy Research) was retrieved to quantify European providers' share against US hyperscalers.
- The Stargate UK pause, the closing of Cohere–Aleph Alpha, and DT's December 2026 gigafactory decision are live items that may change before publication.
- Items flagged "researcher background" in Gaps sections throughout are unverified recollections and must not be cited without a source.
