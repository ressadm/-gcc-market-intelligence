## 1. Third-party comparisons: who is judged ahead on what

### Takeaway
Independent sources converge on one picture: the UAE is ahead on what exists today (operating capacity, US regulatory standing, adoption, institutional maturity, capital already deployed into frontier labs), while Saudi Arabia is ahead on latent scale (land, power system, domestic market, home-grown talent share, distance from Iran) but later in delivery. No retrieved source ranks HUMAIN ahead of G42 on delivered capability; the pro-Saudi argument is about 2028–2034, not 2026.

## 2. National fundamentals side by side

### Takeaway
Saudi Arabia has roughly three times the population, about twice the installed power capacity and a far larger citizen workforce, but a weaker fiscal position (deficits of 3–6% of GDP, breakeven oil price estimates of US$80–96+) and a smaller live data-centre base than the UAE. The UAE has more operating capacity, nuclear baseload, a lower fiscal breakeven and higher adoption, but a tiny citizen talent pool and greater physical exposure to Iran.

### Inferences
- PIF's own strategy number (3,000MW national data-centre target, PIF share 1,800MW by 2030) is materially below HUMAIN's long-run rhetoric (6GW+ by 2034) and roughly consistent with HUMAIN's 3GW-by-2030 target; it is the most authoritative Saudi planning figure found.
- On the best available numbers, live capacity in the two countries is the same order of magnitude (roughly 300–470MW each); the UAE's lead is in near-term AI-grade capacity under construction (200MW Stargate + Microsoft 200MW + Khazna pipeline), not in a large installed-base gap.
- Fiscal asymmetry: Saudi Arabia runs deficits even at wartime oil prices and is being told by the IMF to make PIF more selective; Abu Dhabi's funds are still accumulating (Mubadala +17%). This supports HUMAIN's push for 70% third-party financing and Aramco's entry as shareholder.
- Saudi Arabia's higher locally educated talent share suggests a more sustainable base; the UAE's model depends on continued inward migration, which the war puts at risk.

### Inferences

## 8. Outlook 2027–2030 and milestones

### Takeaway
Analysts do not give quantified probability scenarios; they give conditional ones. The bullish case rests on cheap, fast power and inference demand for the under-served Middle East–Africa–South Asia region; the bearish case combines an AI valuation correction (2027–28), thin non-captive demand, US policy reversal and persistent attack risk. Saudi capacity is expected to arrive later than the UAE's in every forecast found.
