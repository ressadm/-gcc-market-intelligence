## What exactly are the archetypes/models (verbatim names) and how is each defined?

### Takeaway
None of the covered firms publishes a country-archetype taxonomy comparable to McKinsey/BCG/WEF; what they publish instead are (a) provider typologies (Accenture's four provider types incl. "Frontliners"), (b) government posture menus by AI-stack layer (Strategy&'s "Assure / Control / Partner / Influence"), (c) enterprise control-level scales (Capgemini's five levels; IBM's three tiers × build/partner/buy; ADL's four tiers), and (d) loose regional groupings of countries (Deloitte Asia Pacific). The single most-repeated message across all of them is that full-stack sovereignty is unrealistic and "selective", "pragmatic" or "hybrid" sovereignty is the target.

**Accenture — "Sovereign AI: Own your AI future — From managing risk to accelerating growth" (global flagship; read in full text)**
- Publication: copyright 2025; survey fielded July–August 2025; the web landing page is dated November 2025 (search summary for the month). Authors: David Wood (Global Sovereign AI Lead), Mauro Macchi (CEO EMEA), Kunal Shah (Sovereign AI Lead APAC), Bryan Rich, Mauro Capo (Digital Sovereignty Lead EMEA), Surya Mukherjee (Sovereign AI Global Research Lead) — [ACN-Global](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-4/Sovereign-AI-Report.pdf); landing page [accenture.com](https://www.accenture.com/us-en/insights/technology/sovereign-ai)
- Definition: "Sovereign AI refers to a country's ability to independently develop and deploy AI by relying on its own infrastructure, data, models and talent." It "is not about isolationism or every nation/company building the entire AI stack from scratch—very few can. It's about interoperability on one's own terms." — [ACN-Global](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-4/Sovereign-AI-Report.pdf)
- Provider typology (unit of analysis = provider; four types, verbatim): 
  - "Global cloud providers offer unmatched scale, service breadth and pace of innovation. They also provide strong legal, operational and technical sovereign capabilities: e.g., AWS's European Sovereign Cloud, Microsoft's EU Data Boundary and Google's sovereign offerings with EU partners."
  - "Frontliners are country-endorsed national champions (often local incumbents such as telcos or high-tech companies) with local brand recognition that can meet public sector trust requirements and run government-endorsed industry or cross-industry solutions (data and application spaces for AI)."
  - "Neoclouds are AI-native providers built for the new era of compute... Players such as Nebius, Nscale, CoreWeave and Lambda exemplify this model."
  - "Federated consortia are alliances of governments, industries and technology providers that co-develop shared sovereign infrastructure solutions and governance frameworks." Examples: Catena-X; "AI Gigafactory-style consortia emerging across Europe, and national programs such as Saudi Arabia's HUMAIN initiative and Indonesia's Sovereign AI TechCo."
  - Summary of strengths: "cloud providers with global scale and innovation cadence, frontliners with policy-grade assurance and sector convening power, neoclouds with nimble, locally governed AI buildouts, and consortiums with the power of pooled resources and collaboration. The most resilient strategies combine all four as needed." — [ACN-Global](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-4/Sovereign-AI-Report.pdf)
- "Sovereignty continuum" (unit = workload/use case): "Sovereignty is not binary—it's a continuum." "Some use cases need only local data residency and oversight of global infrastructure, while others call for full-stack sovereignty—local data and infrastructure, multi-cloud and multi-model access, confidential computing, advanced encryption or even air-gapped systems when necessary." Headline: "Only one-third of an organization's AI workloads need to be sovereign." — [ACN-Global](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-4/Sovereign-AI-Report.pdf)
- Four imperatives (verbatim): "1. Make sovereignty a CEO-led priority...; 2. Reframe sovereignty as a source of value creation, not just risk mitigation or compliance; 3. Build hybrid ecosystems that combine local trust with global innovation and scale; 4. Architect AI ensuring flexibility across infrastructure, data and models." — [ACN-Global](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-4/Sovereign-AI-Report.pdf)
- "Sovereignty Maturity Index" (unit = organisation, averaged by country and industry; 0–100): composite of four components — "Current and target sovereignty levels" (weight 0.5); "Sovereignty achieved across five layers of the digital and AI stack—Applications, Data, AI Models, Infrastructure, and Security & Governance" (0.3); "Investment in sovereignty initiatives" (0.1); "Control over data" (0.1). "0 indicates no sovereignty and 100 represents full sovereignty across all dimensions." — [ACN-Global](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-4/Sovereign-AI-Report.pdf)

**Strategy& Middle East (PwC network) — "Pragmatic AI sovereignty: Architecting AI value chains for control and continuity in the Gulf countries" (Viewpoint; read in full text)**
- Publication: © 2026; web page release metadata 31 August 2026; latest endnote dated 11 May 2026. Authors: Chady Smayra (Partner, Riyadh), Ramzi Khoury (Partner, Dubai), Sreedhar Gopalakrishnan Nair (Principal, Dubai); contributor Shahmeer Naeem — [S&-PDF](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1/en/industries/technology/pragmatic-ai-sovereignty/strategyand-pragmatic-ai-sovereignty.pdf); [S&-Page](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1/en/industries/technology/pragmatic-ai-sovereignty.html); press release [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/research-studies/gcc-must-secure-critical-ai-chokepoints-to-protect-gulfs-260-billion-economic-opportunity-strategy-472497)
- Definition: "Pragmatic AI sovereignty is the ability of a government to retain strategic control over critical AI capabilities while ensuring the continuity of AI's value chain under disruption." It "mitigates the risks of both external dependence and internal fragility." — [S&-PDF](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1/en/industries/technology/pragmatic-ai-sovereignty/strategyand-pragmatic-ai-sovereignty.pdf)
- AI value chain (Exhibit 1), five layers: "Semiconductors and Hardware"; "Physical Infrastructure (Data Centers, Connectivity)"; "Cloud Platforms"; "Models and Intelligence"; "AI Systems and Sector Deployment"; three "Cross-cutting Enablers": "Talent and Human Capital"; "Cybersecurity and Digital Resilience"; "Data Sovereignty and Privacy". Supply-side actors named per layer; telecom operators are placed in the physical infrastructure layer ("Data center operators, colocation providers, telecom operators, energy providers") and "Hyperscalers, sovereign cloud operators, managed service providers (MSPs)" in cloud platforms. — [S&-PDF](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1/en/industries/technology/pragmatic-ai-sovereignty/strategyand-pragmatic-ai-sovereignty.pdf)
- Four postures (unit = government, applied per value-chain layer), verbatim:
  - "Assure: This posture secures a value layer via agreements, allocation, diversification or contractual safeguards. Typically, it is most effective for globally concentrated AI capabilities, such as advanced chips, critical hardware and frontier models." Example: "the UAE has secured GPU allocations as part of the U.S.–UAE AI Acceleration Partnership."
  - "Control: This posture secures a value layer by owning, operating or governing it through government entities or national champions. ...most effective in such areas as critical data, digital identity, sensitive workloads, national platforms and AI use cases in sectors involving vital national infrastructure." Example: India's Aadhaar.
  - "Partner: This posture secures a value layer via partnership with a global player, while maintaining local governance, localization, resilience and exit rights. Typically, it works well for layers that involve cloud platforms, foundation models and advanced AI services." Example: "France... established Bleu, a sovereign cloud platform that combines Microsoft Azure technology with French operational control and governance."
  - "Influence: This posture secures a value layer using capital, regulation, procurement or market access to shape the layer's standards, capital flows, supplier priorities or policy dialogues." Example: the EU AI Act. — [S&-PDF](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1/en/industries/technology/pragmatic-ai-sovereignty/strategyand-pragmatic-ai-sovereignty.pdf)
- Exhibit 3 is a 5-layer × 4-posture matrix rated "HIGH APPLICABILITY / MEDIUM APPLICABILITY / LOW APPLICABILITY / NOT APPLICABLE". Cell text (verbatim highlights): Cloud Platforms/Control — "Refrain from competing with hyperscalers across the full platform stack; their scale, economics, service breadth and innovation cycle are difficult to replicate"; Cloud/Partner — "Codevelop sovereign cloud capabilities with hyperscalers and local providers, while localizing operations, support, compliance and platform know-how"; Physical Infrastructure/Control — "Own and operate in-country data centers, power, cooling, interconnection, hardened sites and distributed edge infrastructure through national players"; Semiconductors/Control — "Refrain from full upstream self-sufficiency"; Semiconductors/Influence — "Use sovereign capital and strategic relationships to shape supplier priorities"; Models/Control — "Build local capability in fine-tuning, evaluation, Arabic adaptation, inference and sector-specific models"; AI Systems/Control — "Build and operate critical AI systems for government and strategic sectors". (The colour rating of each cell could not be recovered from text extraction.) — [S&-PDF](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1/en/industries/technology/pragmatic-ai-sovereignty/strategyand-pragmatic-ai-sovereignty.pdf)
- Seven "Idea Spotlights" (verbatim names): "National AI Exchange", "Hardened Sovereign Nodes", "Federated Cloud Framework", "National Model Escrow", "National AI Apprenticeship Program", "National AI Assurance Labs", "Sovereign Data Embassy". — [S&-PDF](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1/en/industries/technology/pragmatic-ai-sovereignty/strategyand-pragmatic-ai-sovereignty.pdf)

## What classification logic is used?

### Takeaway
Classification is almost always by layer of the AI stack crossed with a degree-of-control choice, driven by criticality of the workload or capability; only Accenture (survey index) and IBM (cluster analysis) use quantitative methods, and neither classifies countries by strategic model.

### Cited Findings
- Strategy&: governments should assess each value-chain layer on four criteria — "concentration, capital intensity, strategic sensitivity and value creation potential" — and then choose a posture or combination of postures per layer. — [S&-PDF](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1/en/industries/technology/pragmatic-ai-sovereignty/strategyand-pragmatic-ai-sovereignty.pdf)
- Accenture: classification of use cases via a "sovereignty audit for each use case, considering factors such as country risk, industry regulation and business or use-case criticality"; provider choice by complementary strengths; maturity index by weighted survey components (weights 0.5/0.3/0.1/0.1). Survey: 1,928 organisations, 28 countries, 18 industries, July–August 2025; respondents were limited to organisations "that consider sovereignty highly or critically important to their technology stack". — [ACN-Global](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-4/Sovereign-AI-Report.pdf)
- Accenture Middle East: sample N = 185 (UAE 85, Saudi Arabia 85, Qatar 15), drawn from the 2025 global survey. — [ACN-ME](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-fy26/q4/Architecting-Sovereign-AI-In-The-Middle-East-Accenture-Report.pdf)
- Deloitte APAC: country pathways are sorted by "economic scale, strategic priorities, and domestic capacity"; the eight pillars are a checklist, not a scoring model. — [DEL-APAC](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone1/apac/en/docs/collections/2026/sovereign-ai-realising-strategic-opportunities-across-asia-pacific.pdf)
- IBM IBV: tiers by business criticality and differentiation; "Regulatory classifications, such as the EU AI Act's high-risk categories, should inform enterprise AI tiering, but they do not replace it." Segmentation method: four control measures (data, models, compute infrastructure, applications) analysed with "multiple correspondence analysis (MCA)" then "hierarchical cluster analysis (Ward's method)", validated with ANOVA; MCA was used "rather than imposing a single linear maturity scale". — [IBM-IBV](https://www-api.ibm.com/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:57477381-e34f-4f94-871c-d08682c27397/original/as/the-calculus-of-ai-sovereignty-report.pdf)
- Capgemini: five control levels reported per stack dimension from self-reported "desired level of digital sovereignty". — [CAP-2026](https://www.capgemini.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CRI_Digital-Sovereignty_Final-V05_Web.pdf)
- ADL: 2×2 of "sovereignty criticality" (value-at-risk) vs "implementation feasibility" (technical complexity, cost, time, ecosystem maturity). — [ADL-Tradeoffs](https://www.adlittle.com/en/insights/viewpoints/trade-offs-digital-sovereignty)
- KPMG India: contrasts countries by the primary instrument of control — private companies (US), state ownership (China), regulation (EU), public digital infrastructure (India). — [KPMG-IN](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2026/02/sovereign-ai-and-national-security.pdf)
- Deloitte Luxembourg: score the estate against the 4+1 dimensions "to find gaps beyond your risk appetite"; segment workloads by risk. — [DEL-LU](https://www.deloitte.com/lu/en/our-thinking/future-of-advice/achieving-digital-sovereignty.html)

### Inferences
- Because Accenture's sample is restricted to organisations that already rate sovereignty as highly or critically important, its headline percentages overstate concern in the general enterprise population; the country index scores are averages of self-assessments, not assessments of national capability.
- A Qatar sub-sample of 15 respondents is too small to support country-level conclusions about Qatar.
- No firm in this group applies a transparent, replicable scoring of national strategies; country placements are narrative judgements.

### Gaps
- Capgemini's Digital Sovereignty Index methodology and Accenture's Middle East country-level index breakdown were not extracted.

## What do the firms recommend, and what do they say about national champions, ownership, telecom operators, sovereign wealth funds and public-private partnerships?

### Takeaway
The consensus recommendation is hybrid: keep control of data, identity, critical workloads and sector applications; partner with hyperscalers for cloud platforms and frontier models; do not try to replicate chips or hyperscale platforms. Strategy& and Accenture explicitly endorse national champions (often telcos) as builders and operators, and Strategy& assigns sovereign wealth funds the role of securing access and influence.

### Cited Findings
- Strategy& role allocation (verbatim): "Governments will need to set the rules and aggregate demand. Sovereign wealth funds will need to secure access and wield influence. National champions will need to build and operate critical infrastructure. Government entities and national champions will have to create data and use cases, too." GCC governments "must forge robust strategic technology partnerships; empower national champions; wield sovereign capital to secure access and influence; and build and nurture local AI production and deployment ecosystems." — [S&-PDF](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1/en/industries/technology/pragmatic-ai-sovereignty/strategyand-pragmatic-ai-sovereignty.pdf)
- Strategy& on national champions and ownership: "The GCC countries can create national AI champions or mandate that existing players design, integrate and operate AI systems in critical domestic environments and scale sector-specific AI solutions in government, energy, finance, health, logistics and defense." Governments "can provide national champions with the mandates to scale AI-ready infrastructure capable of supporting strategic national workloads." — [S&-PDF](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1/en/industries/technology/pragmatic-ai-sovereignty/strategyand-pragmatic-ai-sovereignty.pdf)
- Strategy& on what not to own: do not compete with hyperscalers across the full platform stack; refrain from full upstream chip self-sufficiency; take "a measured approach to developing frontier models, while investing assertively in sovereign hosting, Arabic adaptation and sector-specific models"; host global foundation models locally "through strategic hosting agreements with major model providers, such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind". Closing line: "The GCC countries that succeed and lead in the AI era will not be those that attempt to own the entire AI value chain." — [S&-PDF](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1/en/industries/technology/pragmatic-ai-sovereignty/strategyand-pragmatic-ai-sovereignty.pdf)
- Strategy& on over-localisation risk: "Taken too far, localized systems can become fragile. During the recent Middle East conflict, cloud infrastructure in the UAE and Bahrain was affected" (citing a Reuters report of 2 March 2026 on AWS data centres damaged in drone strikes); hence the "Sovereign Data Embassy" and multi-cloud "Federated Cloud Framework" proposals. — [S&-PDF](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1/en/industries/technology/pragmatic-ai-sovereignty/strategyand-pragmatic-ai-sovereignty.pdf)

Recommendations include "Foster innovation ecosystems through managed interdependence". — [CAP-2026](https://www.capgemini.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CRI_Digital-Sovereignty_Final-V05_Web.pdf); [press release](https://www.capgemini.com/news/press-releases/most-organizations-say-full-digital-sovereignty-is-an-unrealistic-goal-as-businesses-prioritize-resilience-over-total-independence/)
- ADL: "A rigid 'sovereign-first' approach can increase cost, slow innovation, and reduce competitiveness"; "Open models lower the barrier to entry, but they don't deliver sovereignty. Nations need to distinguish between AI access and AI independence"; test — "whether you could maintain AI operations if your primary foreign technology provider withdrew access tomorrow." — [ADL-Tradeoffs](https://www.adlittle.com/en/insights/viewpoints/trade-offs-digital-sovereignty); [ADL-ArmsRace](https://www.adlittle.com/en/insights/viewpoints/global-ai-arms-race)
- KPMG India frames trade-offs rather than recommendations ("Openness versus security"; "Global integration versus autonomy"; "Public infrastructure versus private innovation") and asks "What role should private companies play on sovereign digital rails?" — [KPMG-IN](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2026/02/sovereign-ai-and-national-security.pdf)

### Inferences
- Three firms (Accenture, IBM, EY) independently single out telecom operators as the natural local anchor for sovereign AI; Strategy& places telcos in the physical-infrastructure layer and speaks of "national champions" more generally (its authors' bios mention advising "national ICT champions").
- None of the firms recommends a government-owned cloud as the default; ownership is recommended only for critical data, identity, hardened sites and critical-sector systems, with national champions (state-backed or private) as operators.
- Public-private partnership is discussed mainly as hyperscaler–local joint operation (Bleu, Delos, Thales–Google) and as pooled demand or consortia (National AI Exchange, AI Gigafactories), rather than as PPP financing structures.

### Gaps
- No firm in scope quantifies the cost or performance trade-off between a hyperscaler-licensed partner cloud and an independent local cloud; Capgemini's 23% premium is a willingness-to-pay figure only.
- No detailed guidance on ownership structures (state vs listed vs JV shareholding) for national champions was found.
- Kearney's view on sovereign wealth funds rests on a single press quote (search summary).

## What commercial interest does each firm have that a reader should weigh?

### Takeaway
Every framework here comes from a firm that sells sovereignty advisory or integration services, and most have declared alliances with hyperscalers, NVIDIA or local sovereign providers; the uniform "hybrid, don't build everything" conclusion is consistent with those commercial positions. No report was found to be explicitly sponsored by an outside vendor, but several were co-produced with one.

### Cited Findings
- Accenture: the global report itself lists Accenture as delivery partner to the providers it showcases — Nebius ("Through its partnership with Accenture"), Cassava Technologies ("Partnering with Accenture to launch a full stack of secure GPU offerings"), Telus ("Accenture is developing and deploying industry-specific solutions on this sovereign platform"), Telia Cygate, and Indosat Ooredoo Hutchison "with Accenture and NVIDIA". ### Inferences
- Accenture's typology gives every provider class a role and Accenture is a named partner in at least five of its showcased examples, so the "combine all four" recommendation aligns with its integrator business model.
- Strategy&'s advice against competing with hyperscalers while empowering national champions is consistent with a client base of Gulf governments, sovereign funds and national ICT champions; the report discloses no sponsor.
- Firms with a proprietary product or vendor co-author (EY, IBM, Roland Berger, Deloitte–STACKIT) should be read as partly promotional; the more analytically independent pieces in this set are Strategy&'s Viewpoint, Deloitte's APAC policy paper and TMT Predictions, and Capgemini's survey.

### Gaps
- No explicit sponsorship statements were found in the Accenture, Strategy&, Deloitte APAC or Capgemini reports; undisclosed commercial relationships cannot be ruled out.
- PwC alliance pages could not be opened (HTTP 403), so the exact wording of the Microsoft and AWS relationships is unverified beyond the URL structure and search summaries.
