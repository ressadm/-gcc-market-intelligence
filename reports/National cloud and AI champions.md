# National champions rent their sovereignty from Washington

*Global benchmarking study of National Cloud and AI Champions outside the United States, China and Japan — evidence as of 15 November 2026*

## 1. Executive Summary

**No country outside the United States and China has built a national cloud or AI champion that is sovereign in the full sense: every champion profiled here runs on imported accelerators (overwhelmingly Nvidia), most run on licensed US cloud software, and the largest are subject to a US regulatory licence that can be widened or withdrawn within months.** What countries have built are narrower things, and the difference between them is the main finding of this study. Abu Dhabi has built the only state-linked group with real operating scale (Khazna reported ~30 live data centres and nearly 650 MW in late 2025, and G42's listed subsidiary Presight earned AED 3.03bn in 2025 with 38.5% from abroad), yet its licence-free chip access lapses on 6 April 2027 unless G42 becomes a US company or re-applies, and the first 200 MW of Stargate UAE had no published commissioning notice by mid-November 2026 ([AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/ai/2025/12/uaes-khazna-enters-saudi-arabia-with-first-data-centre/); [Presight FY2025 release](https://www.presight.ai/news/presight-reports-36-9-annual-revenue-growth-in-2025-surpassing-aed-3-billion); [The Gulf Tape timeline](https://thegulftape.com/articles/stargate-uae)). Saudi Arabia's HUMAIN has gigawatt-scale targets (about 3 GW by 2030) but the only disclosed live capacity is **1.1 MW**, and it publishes no revenue ([The National, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2026/10/22/saudi-arabias-humain-expands-nvidia-deal-with-84mw-ai-computing-plan/); [FWDstart, 7 Sept 2026](https://www.fwdstart.me/p/humain-starts-ipo-preparations-16-months-after-launch-with-revenues-still-undisclosed)). France shows the opposite route: no state ownership, but a regulated demand segment (SecNumCloud) and state-as-customer that helped a private model company, Mistral AI, reach a valuation above €21bn and company-stated annualised revenue above US$400m ([Mistral Series D](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-makes-sovereign-open-weight-ai-to-frontier/); [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/mistral-ai-830m-debt-data-centre-paris)). Across all regions, announced capacity exceeds verified operating capacity by roughly an order of magnitude, MoUs are routinely reported as projects, and commercial revenue outside government and related parties is the least-evidenced part of almost every profile. For Saudi Arabia the implication is to convert announcements into disclosed, contracted, energised capacity and to fill the platform and application layers; for Morocco it is to build sovereignty through regulation, procurement and a modest state-plus-operator consortium rather than through a Gulf-style capital-led champion that its economics cannot support.

### What the evidence supports

This study profiles **ten National Cloud Champions or qualified cloud cases and seven National AI Champions**, plus five cautionary or regional cases (Bleu, Yotta, Sify, Aleph Alpha and Cassava), across 20 countries. The research notes apply a strict test: an entity counts as a champion only if there is evidence of (i) real cloud or AI capability, (ii) a strategic national role conferred by ownership, mandate, procurement or regulation, and (iii) delivery beyond announcements. On that test, four of the candidates named in the brief fail or pass only in qualified form, and the report says so: **Aleph Alpha** abandoned frontier-model development in 2024 and is being absorbed by Canada's Cohere at a roughly 90/10 split ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/cohere-takes-over-aleph-alpha-shortly-after-the-german-startup-ousted-its-original-founder/)); **Bleu** (Orange–Capgemini) was still unqualified under SecNumCloud in October 2026, nearly three years after commercial launch ([Next.ink](https://next.ink/260765/secnumcloud-bleu-vise-desormais-une-qualification-pour-la-fin-dannee/)); **Sify** is a loss-making colocation landlord whose IPO stalled ([Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/ipos/sify-infinit-spaces-ipo-valuation-gap/)); and **Naver Cloud** was eliminated from Korea's sovereign foundation-model project in January 2026 for using another model's frozen weights ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/20098/lg-ai-tops-koreas-sovereign-ai-project-as-naver-cloud-fails-to-advance-msit-confirms)).

### The five most instructive national champion models

**1. The Abu Dhabi integrated state-capital model (G42 / Core42 / Khazna / MGX).** One patron, Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed, chairs the operating company, the investment vehicle and the policy council; the state is customer (an AED 13bn government digital strategy with a 100% sovereign-cloud pillar), financier and regulator-coordinator at once ([Abu Dhabi Media Office](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/government-affairs/abu-dhabi-government-launches-digital-strategy-2025-2027/)). It is strategically relevant because it is the fastest route to scale and the only one that has produced audited, internationalising revenue (Presight) and a closed US$49bn third-party fund (MGX) ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/01/mgx-ai-fund-uae-49-billion.html)). It is also the most contingent: its "sovereign" cloud is a US-technology cloud under Emirati operation, and G42 has reportedly weighed US majority ownership to keep chip access ([Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/ai-cloud/g42-weighs-us-ownership-to-secure-chip-access/)).

**2. The French regulated-demand model (Mistral AI, OVHcloud, the SecNumCloud vehicles).** The state did not create a champion; it created a qualification and a procurement doctrine, and private firms and consortia formed around it. The model matters because it is affordable and has produced the only non-US, non-Chinese general-purpose model company with broad private-sector customers. Its limits are equally clear: European providers have been stuck at about 15% of their home cloud market since 2022, and Mistral's two anchor shareholders are now Dutch and Korean corporates ([Synergy Research](https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/european-cloud-providers-local-market-share-now-holds-steady-at-15); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/08/mistral-ai-raises-record-3-billion-in-samsung-led-funding-round)).

**3. The Indian distributed marketplace model (IndiaAI Mission, Sarvam AI, Yotta).** The state buys subsidised GPU-hours on privately owned capacity (38,231 GPUs onboarded by March 2026), funds twelve model teams rather than one, and uses digital public infrastructure such as Aadhaar as reference demand ([Communications Today](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/38000-gpus-one-big-problem-indias-ai-mission-cant-find-enough-users/)). It is the lowest-fiscal-risk design and the most replicable in middle-income economies, but it leaves the country fully dependent on US chips, suffers low utilisation, and has seen its leading GPU supplier redirect about 75% of capacity to foreign customers at higher prices ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/how-yotta-is-building-its-ai-infra-playbook-to-serve-global-enterprises/)).

**4. The telecom-anchored gateway model (Turkcell–Google, Singtel/Nxera–KKR, stc/center3–Oracle, Deutsche Telekom/T-Systems).** A state-linked telecom supplies sites, power, connectivity and regulatory cover; a foreign partner supplies the cloud stack or the capital. It is strategically relevant because most emerging economies have such a telecom and little else. Its risk is that the champion becomes a landlord: Turkcell says its Ankara hyperscale capacity will be "fully allocated to Google" ([Turkcell Q3 2026 call](https://www.earningswhispers.com/transcript/TKC/Q32026)).

**5. The Korean tournament model (LG AI Research, SK Telecom, Upstage) and Brazil's testable-sovereignty procurement.** Korea confers champion status project by project and withdraws it, enforcing a "from scratch" test that eliminated Naver; Brazil's planned "Nuvem Brasileira" converts sovereignty into procurement tests (a disconnection test, source-code reversibility, a weighted sovereignty score) ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/tech/2026/08/18/2026081812375747016); [Convergência Digital](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/governo/nuvem-brasileira-vai-funcionar-em-infraestrutura-publica-localizada-no-pais-e-sera-operada-por-empresa-publica/)). Both matter because they make champion status contestable and measurable rather than declared. Neither has yet produced a frontier-competitive model or an awarded contract.

### Headline lessons

Sovereignty is layered, and countries buy different layers. Jurisdictional and operational control (who can compel access, who runs and patches the system) is achievable by law and procurement at modest cost. Technological control (chips, cloud software, frontier models) is not currently achievable by anyone outside the US and China; the European Commission's own sovereignty framework concedes its top level is "not today relevant" because of chip dependence ([European Commission, Cloud Sovereignty Framework](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/2ad80a48-166f-4c77-a513-80c53ca2a128_en)). The economics reinforce this: all-in AI capacity costs roughly US$35–60bn per gigawatt, with 40–70% of it paid to chip suppliers on an annual product cadence ([Investing.com on Bernstein](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/how-much-does-a-gw-of-data-center-capacity-actually-cost-4314046); [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/15/its-all-about-the-gpus-70-of-nvidias-ai-supercomputer-cost-goes-to-vera-rubin-chips?.tsrc=rss)). The 2026 Iran war added a physical dimension that no 2025 announcement priced in: AWS declared its Bahrain region unrecoverable on 15 September 2026 ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/17/aws-middle-east-outage-permanent-data-loss-bahrain-uae/)).

## 2. Definition and Taxonomy of National Cloud and AI Champions

### Sovereignty is a ladder with five rungs, and nobody outside the US and China stands on the top one

No authoritative definition of "national champion" specific to cloud or AI was found in the literature reviewed; the term is used loosely. The 2025–2026 policy literature has instead converged on treating sovereignty as a multi-dimensional spectrum. The most operational scheme is the **EU Cloud Sovereignty Framework**, which scores eight objectives (strategic/ownership, legal and jurisdictional, data and AI, operational, supply chain, technology, security, environmental) and assigns a Sovereignty Effectiveness Assurance Level from SEAL-0 (no sovereignty) through SEAL-1 (jurisdictional), SEAL-2 (data), SEAL-3 (technological, with marginal non-EU control) to SEAL-4 (full digital sovereignty). The overall level is the lowest achieved on any objective; the first EU tender set SEAL-2 as the pass mark; and the Commission's own lessons-learnt state that SEAL-4 "is not today relevant" given dependence on chip and hardware supply chains ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/2ad80a48-166f-4c77-a513-80c53ca2a128_en)). The think-tank literature reaches the same place by another route: the Tony Blair Institute frames the choice as "Control, Steer, Depend" per layer, WEF/Bain as "strategic interdependence", and Carnegie as "managed interdependence" ([TBI](https://institute.global/insights/tech-and-digitalisation/sovereignty-in-the-age-of-ai-strategic-choices-structural-dependencies); [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/publications/rethinking-ai-sovereignty/); [Carnegie, June 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/06/early-lessons-in-the-pursuit-of-sovereign-ai)). The TBI and WEF pages were read only as search summaries, so their detailed lever lists are not reproduced here.

This report uses a five-rung working taxonomy synthesised from those sources: **(a) strategic anchoring** (who owns and can take over the entity); **(b) jurisdictional sovereignty** (who can legally compel access); **(c) data sovereignty** (where data sit and who holds the keys); **(d) operational sovereignty** (who runs, staffs and patches the system, and whether it survives disconnection from the foreign vendor); and **(e) technological and supply-chain sovereignty** (who owns the chips, cloud software and model weights). Most "sovereign" offerings in this study reach (b) to (d). None reaches (e) in full. The accurate description of almost every champion is therefore "sovereign-operated on foreign technology", and the report uses that language.

### What counts as a National Cloud Champion

A National Cloud Champion is an entity that (1) operates actual cloud capability — IaaS/PaaS, a government or sovereign cloud platform, or AI compute sold as a service — or large-scale data-centre capacity on which such services demonstrably run, and (2) holds a strategically important national role evidenced by state ownership, a formal mandate, a qualification regime, anchor procurement or a designated role in a national programme. A telecom operator or data-centre company is not classified as a cloud champion on the strength of buildings and fibre alone. Where the evidence shows a landlord whose tenants are foreign clouds, the report classifies it as a **digital-infrastructure champion** or **hyperscaler gateway** and says so (stc/center3, Turkcell, Sify).

### What counts as a National AI Champion

A National AI Champion is an entity with a strategic national role in AI — foundation models, sovereign AI platforms, GPU compute dedicated to AI, research, or applied AI at national scale — again evidenced by mandate, state capital, anchor demand or designation. Prominence is not enough: Lovable (Sweden), DeepL and Black Forest Labs (Germany) are commercially successful AI companies without a national role, and Google DeepMind is London-based but US-owned.

### Four ownership categories and seven champion models

The report distinguishes **government-owned companies** (HUMAIN, Serpro, TII), **government-backed or state-linked companies** (G42/Core42, stc, Deutsche Telekom, Singtel, Turkcell), **private national champions** (OVHcloud, Schwarz Digits, Mistral AI, Sarvam AI, LG AI Research, Naver, YTL, Yotta) and **research institutions or programmes** (AI Singapore, TII, MBZUAI, the Swiss AI Initiative, GPT-NL). Combining ownership with strategy yields seven models, used throughout: (1) state-created and state-backed vehicles; (2) sovereign-wealth-fund-backed technology groups; (3) telecom-led digital infrastructure champions; (4) private-sector national champions; (5) research-led AI champions; (6) public-private partnership and marketplace models; and (7) hyperscaler–local "trusted cloud" joint ventures and imported turnkey stacks. Model (7) is added because the evidence shows it is the fastest-growing form: Bleu and S3NS in France, Delos in Germany, KT's Secure Public Cloud in Korea, Oracle Alloy at stc, and the US government's American AI Exports Program, which packages chips, cloud, models and applications for foreign governments ([trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/press-release/department-commerce-begins-inaugural-call-proposals-american-ai-exports-program)).

### Status labels used in this report

Every financial and capacity figure carries one of four labels. **ACTUAL** means reported in filings, audited, or confirmed operational. **COMMITTED** means a signed binding contract, closed financing or legally budgeted amount. **ANNOUNCED** means a target, plan, pledge, "up to" figure, MoU or framework agreement. **SPECULATIVE/ESTIMATE** means a third-party estimate, unconfirmed media report or derived figure. MoUs and non-binding term sheets are named as such and never described as partnerships in operation. The tag **[verify]** marks facts the researchers saw only in a search-result summary or a single lower-reliability source.

## 3. Global Landscape and Selection Methodology

### The scale gap that frames every national strategy

Three US firms hold about 61% of a cloud infrastructure market running at roughly US$168bn per quarter (Q3 2026: AWS 26%, Microsoft 19%, Google 16%) ([DCD on Synergy Q3 2026](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/synergy-enterprise-cloud-infrastructure-spending-jumped-55-yoy-in-q3/)) [verify]. Four US firms spent roughly US$410bn of capex in 2025 and guide to US$700–750bn in 2026 ([Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/m-four-us-tech-giants-spent-roughly-410-billion-on-capital-expenditure-last-year-much-of-it-tied-to-ai-and-are-steering-toward-roughly-725-billion-in-2026/); [Walnut AI capex tracker](https://walnutinvest.com/stats/ai-capex-statistics)) [verify — secondary trackers]. European providers' share of the European cloud market fell from 29% in 2017 to 15% in 2022 and has stayed there even though their revenues more than tripled; the two largest, SAP and Deutsche Telekom, hold about 2% each ([Synergy Research](https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/european-cloud-providers-local-market-share-now-holds-steady-at-15)). Private AI investment in 2024 was US$109.1bn in the US against US$4.5bn in the UK ([Stanford HAI AI Index 2025](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report?sf223786129=1)). A national champion outside the US draws on a capital pool one to two orders of magnitude smaller.

Demand for sovereignty is nonetheless real and growing. Gartner forecasts sovereign-cloud IaaS spending of US$80bn in 2026 (+35.6%), with China about US$47bn of it and the fastest growth in the Middle East and Africa (+89%), mature Asia-Pacific (+87%) and Europe (+83%); it expects about 80% of sovereign spend to come from new or not-yet-migrated workloads rather than repatriation ([Gartner, 9 Feb 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-09-gartner-says-worldwide-sovereign-cloud-iaas-spending-will-total-us-dollars-80-billion-in-2026)) [verify]. Nvidia reported sovereign AI revenue of more than US$30bn in its fiscal 2026 ([Motley Fool, 28 Feb 2026](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/02/28/nvda-earnings-call-stock-q4-ceo-cfo/?.tsrc=rss)) [verify]. The uncomfortable reading is that sovereign programmes are a growth market for the dominant US supplier, and the hyperscalers are moving to serve the segment themselves: the AWS European Sovereign Cloud went live in Brandenburg on 15 January 2026 with a €7.8bn commitment to 2040 ([Amazon](https://press.aboutamazon.com/aws/2026/1/aws-launches-aws-european-sovereign-cloud-and-announces-expansion-across-europe)).

Two events define the evidence base for sovereignty demand. On 18 June 2025 a Microsoft France executive told the French Senate under oath, "No, I cannot guarantee" that French citizens' data would never be transferred to US authorities without French consent ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/25/microsoft_admits_it_cannot_guarantee/?td=rt-3a)). And withdrawal has now been documented at three layers: chips (the 2023 US restriction on the UAE), cloud services (the ICC prosecutor's email in 2025; Nayara Energy in July 2025) and frontier models (US-ordered cut-off of foreign access to an Anthropic model, as reported by Carnegie) ([Carnegie, June 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/06/early-lessons-in-the-pursuit-of-sovereign-ai); [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/tech-s--sovereignty-washing--in-europe-will-ripple-in-the-global-south); [Carnegie, Oct 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/10/ai-frontier-technology-middle-power-countries-four-scenarios)).

### Research limitations and items to verify

The underlying research was completed on 15 November 2026. The researchers' web-search budget ran out before every gap was closed, and several primary sites returned access errors. This report never fills a gap from memory; where the notes hold no sourced fact, the report says the information was not found. The main caveats follow, written as prose because each needs its qualification.

**Disclosure gaps that are real, not search failures.** HUMAIN publishes no revenue, profit, valuation, energised capacity or GPU delivery count. G42, Core42, Khazna, AI71, TII, Qai and Bleu publish no consolidated financials. center3's standalone revenue and energised megawatts were not found. Exact shareholdings are undisclosed for G42, Mistral (current cap table), Aleph Alpha, Cassava and Yotta.

**Live items that may have changed.** Whether the first 200 MW of Stargate UAE has been commissioned; whether G42's reported US-majority restructuring or capital raise progressed after September 2026; whether the Cohere–Aleph Alpha deal closed; Deutsche Telekom's December 2026 decision on an EU AI gigafactory bid; Bleu's SecNumCloud qualification (targeted December 2026); whether OpenAI paused Stargate UK (seen only as a headline); whether the Microsoft Saudi region went live in Q4 2026; AWS's early-2027 decision on Bahrain; whether Brazil's Nuvem Brasileira tender was issued; whether Morocco's "Digital X.0" law was adopted and whether any provider holds DGSSI cloud qualification.

## 4. Profiles of National Cloud Champions

Ten entities are profiled as National Cloud Champions or qualified cloud cases (4.1–4.10), followed by three cautionary cases named in the brief that fail the champion test (4.11–4.13) and one regional case (4.14). Each profile follows the A–G framework and ends with its own source list. Capacity and money figures carry the ACTUAL / COMMITTED / ANNOUNCED / ESTIMATE labels defined in Section 2.

### 4.1 Core42 and Khazna Data Centers (UAE) — the Gulf's only state-linked cloud champion with operating scale

**A. Company overview.** Core42 is the cloud and compute operator of Abu Dhabi's G42 group, formed in October 2023 by merging G42 Cloud, Injazat and Inception. Khazna Data Centers, acquired by G42 in November 2020, builds and owns the facilities; its CEO is Hassan Alnaqbi ([Wikipedia: G42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G42_%28company%29); [MEED](https://www.meed.com/khazna-confirms-mgx-and-silver-lake-as-shareholders)). On 27 March 2025 Khazna confirmed MGX and Silver Lake as minority shareholders with G42 remaining the majority owner; telecom operator e& sold its 40% stake for US$2.2bn and remains a tenant (ACTUAL) ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/khazna-data-centers-confirms-fresh-funding-from-mgx-and-silver-lake/)). Exact percentages are not published. The parent G42 is chaired and controlled by Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed, the UAE National Security Adviser, with Mubadala, Silver Lake and Microsoft as minority investors (see 5.2). Khazna has acquired a site in Dammam, Saudi Arabia, for a facility of up to 200 MW and agreed with Eni to build a 500 MW campus in Italy (both ANNOUNCED) ([AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/ai/2025/12/uaes-khazna-enters-saudi-arabia-with-first-data-centre/)). Core42's leadership could not be confirmed from primary pages.

**B. Strategic mandate and national role.** No statute names Core42 or Khazna as the national cloud. The mandate is expressed through ownership, demand and regulation. The Abu Dhabi Government Digital Strategy 2025–2027 commits AED 13bn (US$3.5bn) and sets "100% sovereign cloud adoption" as a pillar, led by the Department of Government Enablement and reviewed by the AI and Advanced Technology Council that Sheikh Tahnoon chairs ([Abu Dhabi Media Office](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/government-affairs/abu-dhabi-government-launches-digital-strategy-2025-2027/); [The National, 1 Oct 2025](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/2025/10/01/abu-dhabi-reviews-plans-to-make-emirate-worlds-first-ai-enabled-government-by-2027/)). In February 2026 the UAE Central Bank announced a partnership with Core42 on sovereign financial cloud infrastructure ([Wikipedia: G42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G42_%28company%29)). Most tellingly, the US export rule of July 2026 lists G42 Holding Ltd, including G42 Cloud Technology LLC, by name as approved for advanced computing items ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/14/2026-14132/enhanced-favorable-treatment-for-the-united-arab-emirates-under-the-export-administration)). That is a sovereign mandate of an unusual kind: conferred jointly by Abu Dhabi and Washington.

**C. Business model and value proposition.** Khazna is a wholesale builder-owner whose anchor tenants are the Abu Dhabi state, e&, Microsoft and, at Stargate UAE, OpenAI and Oracle. Core42 sells sovereign cloud and AI compute to government and regulated sectors (B2G and B2B). A secondary profile describes its AI cloud as silicon-agnostic (Nvidia, AMD, Cerebras, Qualcomm) and its "Insight" layer as a sovereign-control layer built with Microsoft Azure ([Gulf Times Now](https://gulftimesnow.com/g42-abu-dhabi-ai-company-explained-profile/)) [verify — low-authority source]. Customer mix and revenue are undisclosed.

**D. Technology and capabilities.** Khazna reported about 30 live data centres with nearly 650 MW in December 2025 (ACTUAL per company, via secondary press), up from 40 MW in 2020 ([AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/ai/2025/12/uaes-khazna-enters-saudi-arabia-with-first-data-centre/)). The pipeline is far larger and is all ANNOUNCED: the 5 GW UAE–US AI campus unveiled on 15 May 2025; Stargate UAE, a 1 GW cluster inside it with OpenAI, Oracle, Nvidia, SoftBank and Cisco, whose first 200 MW was due in 2026; and a further 200 MW for Microsoft ([CNBC, 22 May 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/22/stargate-uae-openai-nvidia-oracle.html); [Microsoft Source EMEA](https://news.microsoft.com/source/emea/2025/11/microsoft-and-g42-accelerate-uaes-digital-future-with-major-data-centre-expansion/)). On 13 August 2026 Khazna said two 100 MW buildings were enclosed and handover was still expected in 2026; a 14 November 2026 write-up found **no commissioning announcement** ([The Gulf Tape](https://thegulftape.com/articles/stargate-uae); [VLSI Korea](https://www.vlsi.kr/en/stargate-uae-datacenter-en/)). One low-authority site claimed the site "switched on" in September; it cites no notice and is treated here as unreliable. On chips, US Commerce authorised up to the equivalent of 35,000 Nvidia GB300 chips for G42 on 19 November 2025, and Microsoft holds licences for about 81,900 A100-equivalents in the UAE; these are licences, not confirmed installations ([The Gulf Tape](https://thegulftape.com/articles/stargate-uae); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-and-g42-expand-data-center-capacity-plans-in-uae-by-200mw/)). The stack is American: Azure, OpenAI models, Nvidia systems.

**E. Financial and investment profile.** ACTUAL: e&'s US$2.2bn exit valued its 40% stake; Core42 secured US$550m of financing in July 2026 for build-out in Europe and the US ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/core42-secures-550-million-financing-to-fuel-ai-build-out-in-europe-us/)). COMMITTED/ANNOUNCED: Microsoft's US$15.2bn UAE investment for 2023–2029, of which about US$7.3bn by end-2025 including its US$1.5bn G42 stake. ANNOUNCED/ESTIMATE: a cost of "more than US$30bn" has been attached both to the whole campus (by the UAE AI minister) and to the 1 GW Stargate cluster (by Reuters); the two scopes must not be summed ([EnterpriseAM](https://enterpriseam.com/ai/2026/09/15/g42-rethinks-stargate-plans-amid-iran-fears/)). Core42 and Khazna revenue, debt and capex are not disclosed. No source states who finances Stargate UAE or the lease terms.

**F. Ecosystem and partnerships.** Operational: Microsoft (equity, Azure, tenancy), e& (tenant), the Abu Dhabi government. Under construction: OpenAI and Oracle as operators of Stargate UAE. Agreements without confirmed construction: Eni (Italy), DataOne (France). Research partners MBZUAI and Cerebras sit on the model side (5.2).

**G. Achievements and impact.** Khazna grew sixteen-fold in five years and is the largest verified operating data-centre platform among the entities in this study. The ratio of announced to operating capacity is nonetheless about 8–10 to 1. Two 2026 shocks define the risk profile. First, Iran's IRGC named Stargate UAE as a target in April 2026, and Reuters reported in September that G42 is considering redrawing the 5 GW plan into dispersed, hardened clusters ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/iran-threatens-to-attack-openais-stargate-data-center-in-uae-war/); [EnterpriseAM](https://enterpriseam.com/ai/2026/09/15/g42-rethinks-stargate-plans-amid-iran-fears/)). Second, the licence-free chip authorisation expires on 6 April 2027 unless the entities become US companies or re-apply (wording from secondary sources). The sovereign cloud of the UAE is, structurally, a US-technology cloud under Emirati operation.

### 4.2 stc Group / center3 (Saudi Arabia) — a state-controlled infrastructure champion whose cloud layer is foreign

**A. Company overview.** stc Group is Saudi Arabia's incumbent telecom operator, listed on the Saudi Exchange and **62% owned by PIF** after PIF sold 100 million shares (about 2%) in November 2024 ([PIF](https://pif.gov.sa/en/news-and-insights/press-releases/2024/pif-announces-sale-of-100-million-shares-in-stc)) [verify]. Founding dates and current executive names were not verified in the research notes and are omitted.

**B. Strategic mandate and national role.** No formal sovereign-cloud mandate for stc or center3 was found. The role arises from incumbency, state ownership and control of landing stations and data centres. center3 states alignment with Vision 2030 and the aim of making the Kingdom a regional digital hub ([center3](https://center3.com/)). Saudi rules require government data to be held by a locally registered provider and never moved abroad, which structurally favours local hosts (Section 7).

**C. Business model and value proposition.** center3 is a wholesale infrastructure business: carrier-neutral colocation, international capacity, internet exchange, cable landing and "cloud enablement" for telecoms, cloud providers, government, banks and hyperscalers. stc's cloud exposure comes through partners: a partnership with Oracle worth more than SAR 2bn (May 2025) for sovereign cloud services delivered through **Oracle Alloy** hosted in center3 sites (COMMITTED per source) ([Saudi Press Agency](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2317972)) [verify]; the Alibaba-linked SCCC, which advertises Nvidia L20/H20 GPU clusters and Alibaba's Qwen model in Riyadh ([SCCC](https://sccc.sa/en/capabilities/ai)) [verify]; and an AWS collaboration announced at LEAP 2026 whose binding status is unknown ([Telecom Review](https://www.telecomreview.com/articles/telecom-operators/30130-stc-group-puts-saudi-arabias-ai-ambitions-into-action-at-leap-2026/)) [verify]. **D. Technology and capabilities.** center3 participates in 16 subsea cables with five more under construction ([center3 subsea capacity](https://center3.com/services/subsea-capacity)) [verify]. Its data-centre count is reported inconsistently (18, about 20, or 25), and no audited figure for energised megawatts in 2026 was found. Targets are 300 MW by 2027 and 1 GW by 2030 under a US$10bn programme (ANNOUNCED) ([Total Telecom](https://totaltele.com/saudis-center3-unveils-plans-for-1-gigawatt-of-data-centre-capacity-by-2030/)) [verify]. The planned AI joint venture with HUMAIN (HUMAIN 51%, center3 49%; up to 1 GW, initial 250 MW "subject to customer commitments") is still an **MoU**: signed December 2025, it expired and was extended by six months ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/saudi-telecom-company-signs-mou-with-humain-to-develop-1gw-of-data-center-capacity/); [Fast Company Middle East](https://fastcompanyme.com/news/stc-and-humain-extend-deal-to-build-up-to-1gw-ai-data-centers-in-saudi-arabia/)). **E. Financial and investment profile.** ACTUAL (FY2025): revenue SAR 77.82bn (+2.5%), operating profit SAR 14.44bn (+0.1%), net profit attributable to shareholders SAR 14.83bn (down 39.9% only because 2024 included a SAR 13.97bn discontinued-operations gain), operating cash flow SAR 18.28bn, year-end cash SAR 13.38bn ([stc Annual Report 2025](https://www.stc.com/content/dam/stc/stc-annual-report-2025/financial-performance)). center3's revenue, EBITDA and capex are not separately disclosed in the sources retrieved. ANNOUNCED: the US$10bn center3 programme. stc is the only Saudi champion with audited, material cash flow, but its operating profit is flat, so the data-centre build is a growth narrative rather than a current earnings driver.

**F. Ecosystem and partnerships.** Oracle (contracted), SCCC/Alibaba Cloud, AWS (announced), HUMAIN (MoU), Saudi Electricity Project Development Company (September 2026 partnership; binding status not stated) and Epsilon (internet-exchange access) ([center3](https://center3.com/)).

**G. Achievements and impact.** Operating carrier-neutral sites in Riyadh, Jeddah and Dammam; a 9.6 MW hyperscaler-grade expansion at Khurais in 2023; an IDC MarketScape "Leader" rating for Gulf colocation ([Converge Digest](https://convergedigest.com/stc-expands-its-hyperscale-data-center-in-riyadh/); [center3](https://center3.com/)). Assessment: stc/center3 is a genuine national digital-infrastructure champion and the natural host for sovereign workloads, but it does not own a cloud stack, and its flagship AI venture has not progressed beyond an MoU in almost a year, which suggests anchor demand for the first 250 MW was not yet contractually secured.

### 4.3 OVHcloud (France) — a private champion by identity and qualification, not by market share

**A. Company overview.** OVHcloud was founded on 15 October 1999 by Octave Klaba in Roubaix, where it remains headquartered, and listed on Euronext Paris in 2021 ([Wikipedia FR](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/OVHcloud)). It is listed but family-controlled: at 23 October 2026 the Klaba family held **74.27% of capital**, employees and directors 1.86%, treasury 0.37% and the free float 23.50% ([OVHcloud shareholders](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/investor-relations/shareholders-and-governance/)). No state body appears in the shareholder table. Octave Klaba returned as Chairman-CEO on 20 October 2025. The company reports more than 3,200 employees, 46 data centres in 18 locations on four continents and about 500,000 servers ([OVHcloud company page](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/company/)). In FY2026 France was 48% of revenue, other Europe 29% and the rest of the world 23% ([OVHcloud FY26 results](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/newsroom/news/financial-results-fy26/)).

**B. Strategic mandate and national role.** OVHcloud has no mandate. Its national role comes from the French "cloud de confiance" doctrine: ANSSI's SecNumCloud qualification, which since version 3.2 requires a European operator with mainly European capital, and the SREN law of May 2024, whose implementing order effective 22 August 2026 requires SecNumCloud for data of "particular sensitivity" ([Wikipedia FR: SecNumCloud](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/SecNumCloud)) [secondary; primary legal texts not retrieved]. OVHcloud has built a defence vertical "to meet demand from several European defence ministries" (ANNOUNCED) ([H1 FY26 results](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/newsroom/news/financial-results-h1-fy26/)).

**C. Business model and value proposition.** It sells mainly bare-metal and private cloud to a long tail of about 1.7 million customers in more than 140 countries. FY2026 mix (ACTUAL): Private Cloud €690.4m (60.3%), Public Cloud €255.1m (22.3%, growing 18.9% like-for-like), Webcloud €199.7m (17.4%). Large corporates are the weak spot (19% of revenue, +2.6%). The proposition is price, an own-designed vertically integrated stack and legal immunity from non-European law. Public-sector revenue share is not disclosed.

**D. Technology and capabilities.** OVHcloud has 250 MW of power connected, of which 140 MW is in operation (ACTUAL) ([FY26 results](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/newsroom/news/financial-results-fy26/)). Its stack is its own, built on open-source standards. It holds three SecNumCloud-qualified offers: Bare Metal Pod, VMware on OVHcloud, and, since 1 September 2026, "SNC Cloud Platform", a pay-as-you-go public cloud ([Solutions Numériques](https://www.solutions-numeriques.com/secnumcloud-avec-ovhcloud-numspot-rejoint-le-cercle-restreint-des-clouds-qualifies/)). AI capability is modest: an AI Lab created after acquiring Dragon LLM (March 2026) to build "sovereign LLMs" and agentic services, plus Gladia voice AI (July 2026); no GPU counts are disclosed, and the redefined AI strategy deploys from 2027 (ANNOUNCED). There is no hyperscaler dependence in the stack; chips are imported.

**E. Financial and investment profile.** ACTUAL (FY2026, year to 31 August; audit reports pending at release): revenue €1,145.1m (+7.2% like-for-like), adjusted EBITDA €480.5m (42.0% margin), net income €9.4m, net debt €1,157m excluding leases, leverage 2.7x, first positive levered free cash flow since the IPO, ratings BB-/Ba3 ([FY26 results](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/newsroom/news/financial-results-fy26/)). Capex runs at about a third of revenue. ANNOUNCED (FY2027 guidance): organic growth above 10% and margin above 42.5% ([TradingSat](https://www.tradingsat.com/ovhcloud-FR0014005HJ9/actualites/ovhcloud-ovh-ameliore-sa-rentabilite-sur-son-exercice-2026-1172029.html)) [verify]. There is no government funding in the capital structure.

**F. Ecosystem and partnerships.** Acquisitions (Seald, Dragon LLM, Gladia); VMware; reference customer Alchemy (more than 2,000 bare-metal servers). OVHcloud was a founding member of Gaia-X ([Wikipedia: Gaia-X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaia-X)).

**G. Achievements and impact.** OVHcloud is among the five largest European providers, but Synergy places it behind SAP and Deutsche Telekom, each at about 2% of a European market worth €61bn in 2024 ([Synergy Research](https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/european-cloud-providers-local-market-share-now-holds-steady-at-15)). It grew 7.2% against a market Synergy expected to grow about 24%. It is the clearest case of commercial viability without state capital — and of the ceiling that viability hits: a 42% EBITDA margin yields only €9.4m of net income because of capital intensity. A 2021 fire destroyed its Strasbourg SBG2 facility, a reminder that resilience is part of sovereignty.

### 4.4 Deutsche Telekom / T-Systems (Germany) — a part-state telecom anchored by procurement

**A. Company overview.** Deutsche Telekom (DT) is a listed group in which the Federal Republic (14.22%) and state bank KfW (14.37%) together held **28.6%** at 30 June 2026; the free float is 71.41% ([DT shareholder structure](https://www.telekom.com/en/investor-relations/company/shareholder-structure)) [verify]. Group CEO is Tim Höttges; T-Systems, the Systems Solutions segment, is led by Ferri Abolhassan and has a Chief Sovereignty Officer, Christine Knackfuss ([DT, 2 Feb 2026](https://www.telekom.com/en/newsroom/latest-updates/media-information/2026/2/t-cloud-public-sovereign-power)). T-Systems' headcount and data-centre count were not retrieved.

**B. Strategic mandate and national role.** There is no formal sovereign mandate. The state is a minority shareholder and public work is won by tender. The strongest state-conferred role is procurement: on 21 May 2026 the digital ministry (BMDS) awarded **70% of the €250m federal AI cloud contract** to a consortium led by T-Systems and SAP, with the remaining 30% to SVA, Schwarz Digits/STACKIT and Codesphere; no lot went to a US hyperscaler, and the tender required operator, infrastructure, key management and platform software to be anchored in the EU (COMMITTED) ([cloudmagazin](https://www.cloudmagazin.com/en/2026/06/19/germanys-stack-goes-live-250-million-euros-for-the-federal-ai-cloud/)). DT and SAP also said they would build a "Deutschland-Stack" for public institutions.

**C. Business model and value proposition.** T-Systems is an IT-services integrator selling to enterprise and public customers. Its cloud lines are T Cloud Public (the rebranded Open Telekom Cloud; more than 4,000 enterprise customers by company claim), the "T-Systems Sovereign Cloud powered by Google Cloud", in which T-Systems operates sovereignty controls over Google Cloud workloads, and since 2026 the Industrial AI Cloud ([DT, 2 Feb 2026](https://www.telekom.com/en/newsroom/latest-updates/media-information/2026/2/t-cloud-public-sovereign-power); [Google Cloud docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/t-systems-sovereign-cloud/docs/product-overview)) [verify for Google docs]. The public–private revenue split is not disclosed.

**D. Technology and capabilities.** The Industrial AI Cloud in Munich was announced on 4 November 2025 as a partnership "valued at €1bn" with Nvidia and SAP, with up to 10,000 Blackwell GPUs, and described by Deutsche Bank's CEO as "a purely private-sector initiative, not a state measure" ([DT, 4 Nov 2025](https://www.telekom.com/de/newsroom/aktuelles/medieninformationen/2025/11/fuer-ein-souveraenes-deutschland)). It opened on 4 February 2026 with 12 MW of initial guaranteed capacity and data-centre partner Polarise (ACTUAL) ([TelecomTV](https://www.telecomtv.com/content/ai/dt-opens-the-doors-to-its-ai-factory-54783/)) [verify]. In November 2026 Abolhassan said the first phase is "sold out" (company claim, no customer list) and announced a second phase of close to €250m taking the site to 20 MW (ANNOUNCED) ([Light Reading, 9 Nov 2026](https://lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/dt-builds-out-ai-factory-but-still-undecided-on-eu-gigafactory-bid)). T Cloud Public is certified to BSI C5 and offers about 80% of hyperscaler core functionality by the company's own estimate. Sovereignty is partial: GPUs and AI software are Nvidia's, one sovereign line is Google technology, and SAP supplies the German software layer. The technical basis of Open Telekom Cloud was not verified in the notes.

**E. Financial and investment profile.** ACTUAL: group FY2025 revenue €119.1bn and cash capex €19.3bn; T-Systems revenue €4,103m (+2.5%) and adjusted EBITDA AL €427m; H1 2026 T-Systems revenue €2,102m with an 8.7% margin ([DT, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.telekom.com/en/newsroom/latest-updates/media-information/2026/2/deutsche-telekom-outperforms-targets-for-2025); [DT Q2 2026 report](https://report.telekom.com/interim-report-q2-2026/management-report/development-of-business-in-the-operating-segments/systems-solutions.html)). ANNOUNCED: T Cloud Public revenue of "over €200m" in 2026 ([Yahoo Finance call summary](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/deutsche-telekom-q4-earnings-call-155923050.html)) [verify]. That is about 5% of T-Systems and under 0.2% of the group. The €1bn figure is a partnership valuation, not disclosed DT capex. No cloud or AI capex line is published. A reported plan to carve out DT's data centres is unverified.

**F. Ecosystem and partnerships.** Nvidia, SAP, Polarise, Google Cloud; ecosystem users named at launch include Siemens, Agile Robots, Wandelbots, PhysicsX and Perplexity. DT was part of a joint German EU-gigafactory bid that collapsed in 2025; as of 9 November 2026 it had not decided whether to bid alone, citing a requirement to reserve 60% of capacity for public use and expected "effective utilisation" of only 10–15% ([Light Reading](https://lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/dt-builds-out-ai-factory-but-still-undecided-on-eu-gigafactory-bid)).

**G. Achievements and impact.** DT won the largest share of Germany's flagship sovereign AI procurement and operates the only confirmed operating sovereign AI capacity in Germany. Critics argue the federal award creates "new dependencies on IT corporations" ([netzpolitik.org](https://netzpolitik.org/?p=520710)) [verify].

### 4.6 Singtel / Nxera (Singapore) — a state-owned telecom recycling capital into regional AI infrastructure

**G. Achievements and impact.** Nxera filled its new Singapore capacity and reached Singtel's return-on-invested-capital target (11.1%). Control of the largest platform (STT GDC) now sits with a US private-equity firm. The model is a disciplined one — state-linked operator, private-equity capital, Nvidia technology, neighbouring countries' land and power — and carries less sovereignty rhetoric, and therefore less narrative risk, than its peers.

### 4.7 Turkcell (Turkey) — a state-anchored data-centre champion and hyperscaler gateway

Technology and capabilities.** ACTUAL: 54 MW of active IT capacity across four locations (Q3 2026); TARGET: 100 MW by 2032 ([Turkcell Q3 2026 call](https://www.earningswhispers.com/transcript/TKC/Q32026); [Fintables](https://fintables.com/arastirma/yazilar/sirket-notlari/tcell-analist-toplantisi-notlari-2026-3f.md)) [verify]. The cloud stack of the flagship project is Google's.

**E. Financial and investment profile.** ACTUAL (FY2025, inflation-adjusted): revenue TRY 241.5bn (+10.7%), EBITDA TRY 104.0bn (43.1% margin) ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260305283495/en)). Cumulative data-centre investment was about €600m by Q1 2026 ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/turkcell-iletisim-hizmetleri-as-q1-earnings-call-highlights-2026-05-15/)) [verify]. ANNOUNCED: Google US$2bn over ten years; Turkcell about US$1bn by 2032; a target of data-centre and cloud reaching 8–10% of revenue by 2032 ([Converge Digest](https://convergedigest.com/oogle-cloud-to-build-new-turkiye-region-as-part-of-2b-10-year-investment/); [MarketBeat, 6 Mar 2026](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/turkcell-iletisim-hizmetleri-as-q4-earnings-call-highlights-2026-03-06/?.tsrc=rss)).

The 2026 slowdown shows that even a well-capitalised telecom is exposed to global hardware supply cycles.

### 4.8 Serpro, with Dataprev (Brazil) — a state sovereign-cloud operator and broker, not a technology champion

**A. Company overview.** Serpro (Serviço Federal de Processamento de Dados) is a federal state-owned IT company. **C. Business model and value proposition.** Serpro is a B2G provider and a multicloud broker. The government cloud is available to more than 250 federal bodies (an addressable base), but a November 2025 report counted only 11 that had migrated ([DCD Brasil](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/br/not%C3%ADcias/governo-pretende-migrar-dados-estrat%C3%A9gicos-para-infraestrutura-soberana-brasileira/)) [verify].

**D. Technology and capabilities.** The Nuvem de Governo runs in two Serpro-owned data centres (Brasília and São Paulo) operated entirely by Serpro staff, on foreign stacks: AWS Outposts, Google Distributed Cloud and Huawei Cloud Stack, with Oracle and Microsoft in homologation as of August 2026. Serpro's CEO said that when it sought a fully isolated cloud, Huawei was the only vendor that initially responded, and stated plainly that sovereignty has not yet been achieved ([Mobile Time](https://www.mobiletime.com.br/noticias/11/05/2026/serpro-nuvem-soberana/)). The next step, the "Nuvem Brasileira" (a cooperation of the ministry, ABDI, Serpro and BNDES), would procure a licensable cloud software platform with irrevocable source-code access, shared IP, a "disconnection test" of nearly 500 checks and a weighted "sovereignty gradient". It was at a non-binding market-sounding stage on 3 September 2026, with no budget or deadline ([Convergência Digital, 3 Sep 2026](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/governo/nuvem-brasileira-vai-funcionar-em-infraestrutura-publica-localizada-no-pais-e-sera-operada-por-empresa-publica/)).

**E. Financial and investment profile.** ACTUAL (2025): net revenue R$4.7bn, net profit R$755.1m, year-end cash R$2.5bn ([Agência Gov, Apr 2026](https://agenciagov.ebc.com.br/noticias/202604/serpro-impulsiona-transformacao-digital-do-brasil-e-registra-lucro-recorde-em-2025)) [verify]. Government-cloud revenue rose from R$1.26m in 2024 to more than **R$89m** in 2025, about 2% of revenue ([Serpro strategy report Q4 2025](https://www.transparencia.serpro.gov.br/governanca/estrategia-institucional/desempenho-estrategia-4tri.pdf)) [verify]. The ministry's statement of "more than R$1bn invested" in the government cloud is self-reported with no breakdown. Dataprev earned R$899.7m net profit on R$2.5bn revenue in 2025, with 60.6% of revenue from financial institutions' payroll-loan services ([Convergência Digital, Apr 2026](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/governo/com-consignado-dataprev-tem-lucro-de-r-900-milhoes-e-crescimento-de-77-em-2025/)) [verify].

**F. Ecosystem and partnerships.** AWS, Google, Huawei, Oracle, Microsoft (vendors); MGI, ABDI and BNDES (state partners); an MoU with the Piauí-led SoberanIA model initiative. The Federal Court of Accounts reportedly ordered a geographic-redundancy plan and a review of Serpro's AWS contract ([Convergência Digital](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/?p=52736)) [verify].

**G. Achievements and impact.** Serpro is profitable, owns its sites and operations and holds a legal priority in federal procurement. Uptake is small relative to the narrative. Its lasting contribution may be institutional: the graded data-classification model and the Nuvem Brasileira design convert sovereignty from a marketing claim into testable procurement criteria. That design is unproven, since no tender had been issued in the latest sources.

Technology.** An initial Nvidia-based AI factory was declared deployed in South Africa on 19 March 2026; on 4 May 2026 the local CEO said it "goes live in Cape Town soon" ([ITWeb, 4 May 2026](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/cassava-plans-ai-factory-in-joburg-as-cape-town-goes-live/6GxRKMYQbWnMb3Wj)). The original target was June 2025. Installed GPU counts are undisclosed; 3,000 GPUs in South Africa and 12,000 across Africa are ANNOUNCED aspirations.

**E. Financials.** ACTUAL (December 2024): a US$310m package of US$90m equity (DFC, Finnfund, Google) and US$220m debt refinancing ([US DFC](https://www.dfc.gov/media/press-releases/cassava-technologies-receives-90-million-equity-investment-expanding-digital)). A US$720m AI-factory investment figure appears in one outlet only and is unconfirmed.

**F. Ecosystem.** Nvidia (Cloud Partner status and equity), Google, Anthropic, CSIR. Planned follow-on markets are Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt and **Morocco**.

**G. Assessment.** Cassava is the only African-owned entity found that plausibly qualifies as a regional AI-infrastructure champion, but delivery lags announcements by about a year and disclosure is poor. Absent a domestic alternative, a foreign-owned pan-African operator of this kind could become Morocco's de facto GPU-cloud supplier.

## 5. Profiles of National AI Champions

Seven entities are profiled as National AI Champions (5.1–5.7), followed by one cautionary case (5.8). Naver's model business is treated within its cloud profile (4.9) because the Korean state itself withdrew its model-champion status.

### 5.1 HUMAIN (Saudi Arabia) — a state-created full-stack vehicle whose delivery is far behind its announcements

**A. Company overview.** HUMAIN is a Riyadh-based AI company launched on 12 May 2025 as a PIF subsidiary, reported as chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and led by CEO Tareq Amin, formerly head of Aramco's technology arm ([The National, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2026/10/22/saudi-arabias-humain-expands-nvidia-deal-with-84mw-ai-computing-plan/); [Semafor, 7 Sept 2026](https://www.semafor.com/article/09/04/2026/the-switzerland-of-ai-saudi-arabias-big-ai-bet)). Since a transaction that closed on 1 November 2026 it is owned **51% by PIF and 49% by Saudi Aramco** (through a wholly owned subsidiary), Aramco having contributed about SAR 9.9bn (US$2.6bn) in cash during 2026 plus AI, compute and digital assets (ACTUAL; the PIF release was seen only in summary, corroborated by secondary outlets) ([PIF, 1 Nov 2026](https://www.pif.gov.sa/en/news-and-insights/press-releases/2026/pif-and-aramco-close-humain-transaction-to-accelerate-humain-global-growth/); [W.Media](https://w.media/?p=45021)). Both shareholders are state-controlled, so HUMAIN is in effect wholly state-owned. The Saudi Company for Artificial Intelligence (SCAI), previously wholly owned by PIF, was transferred to HUMAIN ([Aramco, 28 Oct 2025](https://www.aramco.com/en/news-media/news/2025/pif-and-aramco-agree-for-aramco-to-acquire-a-significant-minority-stake-in-humain)). Which Aramco assets moved at closing is not itemised publicly. Board composition, headcount and subsidiaries were not found. HUMAIN plans offices in the US, France and the UK and has spoken of a dual listing in Saudi Arabia and on Nasdaq around 2029 (ANNOUNCED) ([FWDstart](https://www.fwdstart.me/p/humain-starts-ipo-preparations-16-months-after-launch-with-revenues-still-undisclosed)).

**B. Strategic mandate and national role.** HUMAIN's stated purpose is to make the Kingdom "an international AI powerhouse" across four areas: data centres, infrastructure and cloud platforms, AI models including an Arabic multimodal LLM, and AI solutions ([Nvidia, 13 May 2025](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/humain-and-nvidia-announce-strategic-partnership-to-build-ai-factories-of-the-future-in-saudi-arabia)). PIF and Aramco tie it to PIF's mandate "to develop strategic sectors and localize modern technologies". No royal decree, cabinet decision or published charter granting an exclusive sovereign mandate was found, and no source shows that government entities are required to buy from it. The mandate is political and ownership-based. Secondary sources describe a division of labour in which HUMAIN commercialises the ALLaM model while the Saudi Data and AI Authority (SDAIA) keeps regulatory authority; this is not confirmed in a primary document.

**C. Business model and value proposition.** Four lines: build-to-suit data-centre capacity for hyperscalers and AI labs that buy their own GPUs; its own GPU cloud and an inference API marketplace; Arabic-first models and applications (ALLaM, HUMAIN Chat, HUMAIN ONE); and investing. Named customers are few and several are investees. The Saudi Ministry of Commerce is among the first cloud customers (ACTUAL). Luma AI, whose US$900m Series C HUMAIN led, contracted for the initial 100 MW of the AMD–Cisco–HUMAIN venture (COMMITTED per source). Together AI agreed in September 2026 to use 250 MW, with the companies "expecting" more than US$5bn of gross annualised revenue in the first operating year (ANNOUNCED projection). A 50 MW Vera Rubin tranche for an unnamed US customer remains "subject to final agreements" (MoU-level). HUMAIN ONE is to be bundled with Microsoft 365 Copilot with a target of one million users (ANNOUNCED) ([FWDstart](https://www.fwdstart.me/p/humain-starts-ipo-preparations-16-months-after-launch-with-revenues-still-undisclosed); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2026/10/22/saudi-arabias-humain-expands-nvidia-deal-with-84mw-ai-computing-plan/)). A December 2026 roll-out of compute, models and software to developers and businesses is planned ([The Circuit, 13 Nov 2026](http://circuit.news/2026/11/13/pifs-humain-sets-up-december-rollout-for-its-saudi-ai-tools/)).

**D. Technology and capabilities.** *Models.* ALLaM, the Arabic-first LLM, was trained on about 4,000 GPUs, and the CEO acknowledges that no Middle Eastern entity has built a state-of-the-art frontier model. He wants HUMAIN to be a "Switzerland" for open-weight models; Bloomberg reported a HUMAIN platform built on a Chinese open-source model (MiniMax), alongside partnerships with Reflection AI and Mistral ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/09/04/2026/the-switzerland-of-ai-saudi-arabias-big-ai-bet)). *Infrastructure.* Targets are about 3 GW by 2030 and more than 6 GW by 2034 (ANNOUNCED). The only disclosed live figure is **1.1 MW** at launch of the Nvidia-powered cloud, at utilisation above 90% (ACTUAL); AMD and Cisco say jointly built infrastructure is live but give no megawatts. The Nvidia track illustrates the gap: May 2025 headline of up to 500 MW and "several hundred thousand" GPUs; October 2026 dated pipeline of up to 84 MW and about 27,000 GPUs, of which 14 MW deploys from Q4 2026 and 70 MW remains subject to final agreements. Other programmes are ANNOUNCED and overlap in unknown ways, so they must not be summed: the AMD–Cisco joint venture for 1 GW by 2030 ([DCD](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/amd-cisco-and-humain-set-up-jv-for-1gw-of-ai-infrastructure-in-saudi-arabia/)) [verify], an xAI 500 MW data centre ([Middle East AI News](https://www.middleeastainews.com/p/xai-to-build-500mw-saudi-ai-data)) [verify], an AWS "AI Zone", a Qualcomm allocation reported only by an aggregator, and 100 MW at DataVolt's Oxagon campus, where ground was broken on 31 August 2026 with first capacity expected in 2028 ([NEOM](https://www.neom.com/en-us/newsroom/HUMAIN-and-DataVolt-to-develop-AI-data-center-in-NEOM)). Two 100 MW sites at Riyadh and Dammam due in Q2 2026 had no public confirmation of operation by September 2026 per an aggregator tracker ([vision2030.ai tracker](https://vision2030.ai/analysis/humain-data-centers-riyadh-dammam-status/)) [verify]. All chips are third-party and depend on US export approval; Saudi Arabia was not covered by the July 2026 rule that upgraded the UAE ([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/150631/the-trump-administration-throws-gulf-ai-a-lifeline/)).

**E. Financial and investment profile.** HUMAIN "has not disclosed revenue, EBITDA, or profit" ([FWDstart](https://www.fwdstart.me/p/humain-starts-ipo-preparations-16-months-after-launch-with-revenues-still-undisclosed)). ACTUAL: Aramco's US$2.6bn cash; a US$3bn investment in xAI; the lead of Luma AI's Series C. COMMITTED per source: US$3bn from Blackstone (instrument unspecified). MoU-level or facility ceiling: up to US$1.2bn of financing for up to 250 MW (January 2026). ANNOUNCED: a 30% shareholder / 70% third-party funding mix ("not there yet"), a US$2.5bn data-centre vehicle, a US$10bn venture fund. ESTIMATE: build cost of US$11–12m per MW (the CEO), implying more than US$30bn for 3 GW excluding tenant GPUs ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/09/04/2026/the-switzerland-of-ai-saudi-arabias-big-ai-bet)). Headline aggregates such as "US$77bn" appear only in aggregator titles and are unverified. PIF's cumulative equity, capex to date, debt and valuation are undisclosed.

**F. Ecosystem and partnerships.** Chips and systems: Nvidia, AMD, Cisco, Qualcomm, Groq. Cloud: AWS, Microsoft. Developers: DataVolt (NEOM), stc/center3 (MoU). Models: Mistral, Reflection AI. Prospective tenants: xAI, Together AI, Luma AI, Nebius (preliminary framework). Government: PIF, Aramco, the ICT ministry, SDAIA.

**G. Achievements and impact.** In 18 months HUMAIN consolidated state AI assets under one roof, attracted every major chip vendor and launched an Arabic chat product. Against a 3 GW target, publicly evidenced delivery is well under 1%. The credibility issues are specific: no disclosed revenue; demand that is partly circular (investees as customers); dependence on case-by-case US licences; and pace tied to offtake — the CEO says he could build faster but needs predictable revenue first. It is a designated national champion whose champion status rests, so far, on mandate and capital rather than output.

### 5.2 G42 group, including Inception, Presight and MGX (UAE) — the clearest state-linked AI champion, and the most geopolitically exposed

**A. Company overview.** Group 42 Holding Ltd was founded in 2018 in Abu Dhabi. Its chairman and controlling shareholder is Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan, the UAE National Security Adviser; Group CEO is Peng Xiao; the board includes Mubadala's Khaldoon Al Mubarak, Microsoft's Brad Smith and Silver Lake's Egon Durban ([Wikipedia: G42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G42_%28company%29)) [secondary; g42.ai was not reachable]. Mubadala invested in 2020, Silver Lake about US$800m in 2021 and Microsoft US$1.5bn in 2024 (ACTUAL); percentages are not published. Subsidiaries and affiliates include Core42 and Khazna (4.1), Inception (models), Presight (ADX-listed analytics), Space42 (ADX-listed), M42 (health), CPX (cybersecurity) and AIQ (energy AI with ADNOC). MGX, founded in March 2024 by Mubadala and G42 and also chaired by Sheikh Tahnoon, is the investment vehicle ([Wikipedia: MGX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGX_Fund_Management_Limited)). In September 2026 Bloomberg reported that G42 had weighed bringing in a US majority shareholder or creating a US vehicle to secure chip access, and was exploring raising "billions"; nothing is final (SPECULATIVE/REPORTED) ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/microsoft-backed-ai-firm-g42-weighs-raising-billions-of-dollars); [Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/ai-cloud/g42-weighs-us-ownership-to-secure-chip-access/)).

**B. Strategic mandate and national role.** G42 is a government-created champion in substance. Its chair also chairs MGX and the emirate's AI and Advanced Technology Council; a state fund is an investor; and its main customers are state entities. Carnegie describes it as "a single state-linked vehicle" and notes that hosting US compute functions as "geopolitical insurance" ([Carnegie, June 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/06/early-lessons-in-the-pursuit-of-sovereign-ai)). No statute confers the role.

**C. Business model and value proposition.** A holding group selling sovereign cloud and compute (Core42/Khazna), big-data and AI systems to governments (Presight), satellite and geospatial services (Space42), health (M42) and model development (Inception), while investing globally through MGX. It now exports a build-own-operate model of AI facilities to other governments. Status by project: Kenya (with Microsoft, about US$1bn) is **stalled or suspended**; Italy (Khazna–Eni, 500 MW) and France (DataOne) are agreements without confirmed construction; Vietnam is a framework of up to US$1bn (MoU-level); India is an announced national-scale supercomputer using Cerebras chips ([DCD on Kenya](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-and-g42-data-center-in-kenya-stalled-due-to-lack-of-power-capacity/); [Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/06/early-lessons-in-the-pursuit-of-sovereign-ai)).

**D. Technology and capabilities.** *Models.* Jais (Arabic–English, 2023) was built by Inception with MBZUAI and Cerebras; Jais 2 is a 70bn-parameter open-weight model; K2 Think (2025) and K2 Think V2 (70B, January 2026) are open reasoning systems from MBZUAI and G42; Jais 3 (15 October 2026) is a family of six open models from 0.9bn to 375bn parameters plus speech, built on MBZUAI's "K2 Horizon" base models and published on Hugging Face ([PR Newswire, Jais 3](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/institute-of-foundation-models-and-inception42-launch-jais-3-the-worlds-leading-arabic-native-ai-for-speech-writing-and-agents-302909754.html); [PR Newswire UK, K2 Think V2](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/mbzuai-launches-k2-think-v2-uaes-fully-sovereign-next-generation-reasoning-system-302671477.html)). Benchmark claims are vendor-reported. The strategy is open-weight and Arabic-specialised, not frontier-scale. *Infrastructure.* See 4.1. *Governance history.* In 2023–24 US concerns about Huawei equipment and Chinese ties led G42 to phase out Huawei and divest China holdings; the Microsoft deal carries added oversight ([NYT, 2023](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/11/27/us/politics/ai-us-uae-china-security-g42.html); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/microsoft-g42-deal-positive-because-it-cut-huawei-ties-white-house-official-says-2024-06-24/)).

**E. Financial and investment profile.** G42's consolidated revenue, profit and valuation are not disclosed. The only audited figures are the listed subsidiaries. **Presight** (ACTUAL, FY2025): revenue AED 3.03bn (+36.9%), EBITDA AED 785m, profit after tax AED 665.5m, international revenue AED 1.17bn (38.5% of total, up from 23%), backlog AED 3.4bn, no debt ([Presight](https://www.presight.ai/news/presight-reports-36-9-annual-revenue-growth-in-2025-surpassing-aed-3-billion)). **Space42** (ACTUAL, FY2025): revenue US$577m (−8%) with US$129m of impairments ([Space42](https://space42.ai/en/press-release/2026/fy-2025-press-release/)) [verify]. **MGX**: COMMITTED — a first third-party fund closed at about US$49bn on 1 July 2026, not yet deployed; ACTUAL participations (cheque sizes undisclosed) in funding rounds of Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI; COMMITTED — a consortium acquisition of Aligned Data Centers at a US$40bn enterprise value (the consortium's, not MGX's share; closing not verified); ANNOUNCED — €30–50bn for a French AI campus and a US$100bn assets target ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/01/mgx-ai-fund-uae-49-billion.html); [Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/mgx-aip-gip-to-acquire-aligned-data-centers/)). Carnegie cites reported UAE AI spending of US$148bn since the start of 2024, at home and abroad.

**F. Ecosystem and partnerships.** Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle, Nvidia, Cerebras, AMD; MBZUAI (research); Mubadala and MGX (capital); ADNOC (AIQ); the Abu Dhabi government (demand). G42 published an assurance framework for US-origin chips in February 2026.

**G. Achievements and impact.** G42 has operating assets, listed subsidiaries with audited and internationalising revenue, an open Arabic model family and a closed US$49bn fund. Three qualifications matter. Presight's foreign customers are overwhelmingly other governments (Kazakhstan, Jordan, Albania), so "international" does not mean "private-sector". Governance is concentrated in one individual across operator, investor and policy council. And the reported consideration of US majority ownership shows the tension at the core of the model: the "sovereign national champion" depends on US chips, US platforms and a US licence with a hard review date. Carnegie's judgement is that the UAE's sovereignty efforts "have yet to fully take off" and have deepened reliance on Washington.

### 5.3 Technology Innovation Institute and AI71 (UAE) — a research-led champion that has stepped back from the frontier

**A. Company overview.** TII was founded in May 2020 as an "Abu Dhabi government funded research institution" within the Advanced Technology Research Council (ATRC). Its president is Faisal Al Bannai and its CEO Najwa Aaraj; it has ten research centres ([Wikipedia: TII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_Innovation_Institute)). AI71 was launched on 28 November 2023 by ATRC and is taken to market by VentureOne, ATRC's commercialisation arm ([ATRC](https://www.atrc.gov.ae/news/abu-dhabis-advanced-technology-research-council-launches-ai71-new-ai-company-pioneering)). Ownership percentages, legal form, budget and headcount are not disclosed. One guide says AI71 is "backed by G42"; official ATRC material names only ATRC and VentureOne, and the official source prevails.

**B. Strategic mandate and national role.** TII is a state research lab; AI71 is its go-to-market arm, launched by the Crown Prince of Abu Dhabi with the proposition of self-hosted, "decentralised data control" AI for companies and countries. It is a separate institutional lineage from G42, so Abu Dhabi runs two parallel model families under different patrons.

**C. Business model and value proposition.** TII releases open-weight models; AI71 sells applications built on them in enterprise search, health, legal, government services and agriculture. An agreement with the Abu Dhabi Department of Health covers its Falcon-based "RAZI71" system ([dxbi.ae](https://www.dxbi.ae/companies/ai71)) [verify — directory source]. Named customers are mainly Abu Dhabi government bodies; no revenue is disclosed.

**D. Technology and capabilities.** The Falcon family began with 7B, 40B and 180B models in 2023. The 2026 releases are deliberately small and specialised: Falcon H1R 7B, an open-weight hybrid reasoning model (5 January 2026); Falcon-H1 Arabic; Falcon Perception, a 600M-parameter multimodal model (3 May 2026); and Falcon-ASR for Arabic and Emirati speech (7 October 2026) ([TII, Falcon Reasoning](https://www.tii.ae/news/tii-launches-falcon-reasoning-best-7b-ai-model-globally-also-outperforms-larger-models); [TII, Falcon Perception](https://www.tii.ae/news/tii-launches-falcon-perception-new-multimodal-ai-model-helps-machines-see-and-understand-world); [TII, Falcon-H1 Arabic](https://www.tii.ae/news/abu-dhabis-tii-launches-falcon-h1-arabic-establishing-worlds-leading-arabic-ai-model)). Claims such as "best 7B model globally" are vendor claims. Training-compute provenance and licence terms for the 2026 models were not verified.

**E. Financial and investment profile.** Undisclosed. TII is government-funded; no budget, AI71 revenue or funding figure was found.

**F. Ecosystem and partnerships.** AI71's launch partners were the Abu Dhabi Department of Government Enablement, the UAE Office of AI, World Wide Technology, Hub71, PwC Middle East and AWS ([TII](https://www.tii.ae/news/abu-dhabis-advanced-technology-research-council-launches-ai71-new-ai-company-pioneering)).

**G. Achievements and impact.** Falcon put a Gulf research lab on the open-model map in 2023. The 2026 pattern shows TII has stopped competing on frontier scale and targets efficient, edge-deployable and Arabic-dialect niches — realistic for a state lab, but it means Falcon is not a frontier competitor. Overlap with G42's Jais is evident: both claimed Arabic benchmark leadership in 2026. AI71's commercial traction is not evidenced.

### 5.4 Mistral AI (France) — a private champion made by venture capital and anchored by state demand

**A. Company overview.** Mistral AI was founded on 28 April 2023 in Paris by Arthur Mensch, Guillaume Lample and Timothée Lacroix. It has offices in the UK, Palo Alto and Singapore, opened a Munich hub in September 2026 and reports more than 1,000 employees ([Wikipedia FR: Mistral AI](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_AI); [Mistral news](https://mistral.ai/news)). It has no state majority owner. ASML led the September 2025 Series C with €1.3bn and became the largest shareholder at roughly 11% (press-reported); Samsung Electronics led the September 2026 Series D. Bpifrance is a minority shareholder and a lender. In 2024 the CEO said 65% of capital remained French; that is almost certainly out of date, and no current cap table is public ([Mistral Series C](https://mistral.ai/fr/news/mistral-ai-raises-1-7-b-to-accelerate-technological-progress-with-ai); [Mistral Series D](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-makes-sovereign-open-weight-ai-to-frontier/)).

**B. Strategic mandate and national role.** No statutory mandate. Mistral is treated as the national AI champion through procurement: an Armed Forces framework agreement (notified 16 December 2025), open to all forces and several defence research bodies, with value undisclosed; an assistant rolled out to about two million civil servants in June 2026; and a €6m state agreement for 2026–27 embedding Mistral engineers in ministries ([Next.ink, 9 Jan 2026](https://next.ink/brief-article/le-ministere-des-armees-signe-un-accord-cadre-avec-mistral/); [Siècle Digital, 4 Sept 2026](https://siecledigital.fr/2026/09/04/apres-le-piratage-des-impots-letat-fait-appel-aux-ingenieurs-de-mistral-pour-renforcer-ses-ministeres/)). The economy minister has warned against concentration: "Si l'IA européenne se résume à Mistral, alors nous sommes fichus."

**C. Business model and value proposition.** Mistral monetises models through enterprise contracts, API and platform usage and, increasingly, owned compute sold as capacity. It claims more than 125 enterprise customers in 20 countries; named deals include CMA CGM (€100m partnership), ASML, HSBC, Airbus, EDF, BMW, BNP Paribas, Stellantis and SAP ([Mistral customers](https://mistral.ai/en/customers)). Circularity is visible: BNP Paribas is customer, shareholder and lender; HSBC is customer and lender; ASML is customer and lead investor. Its 2026 pivot is toward a vertically integrated offer of models, compute and forward-deployed engineers, including "European Compute Units" pre-sold to an anchor group of enterprises ([Mistral, 11 Aug 2026](https://mistral.ai/news/regional-inference-open-models-new-compute/)). Public versus private revenue split is undisclosed.

**D. Technology and capabilities.** Mistral combines open-weight small and medium models with frontier models under more restrictive licences; 2026 releases include Small 4, Medium 3.5 and Large 4 (weights published 29 October 2026), plus speech, OCR and physical-AI models. It now also serves third-party open models, beginning with a Chinese one (Z.ai's GLM-5.2), which drew criticism. Before 2026 it relied on Azure, Google Cloud and CoreWeave. It now owns a 44 MW site at Bruyères-le-Châtel, financed by debt; sources conflict on its contents (about 13,800 Nvidia GB300 GPUs per one report; "18,000 GB200 systems" already operational per Nvidia in June 2026) ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/mistral-ai-830m-debt-data-centre-paris); [Nvidia blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/france-advances-europes-ai-future/)). ANNOUNCED: €1.2bn in Sweden for 2027; 200 MW across Europe by 2027; up to 1 GW by 2030; and a separate 1.4 GW campus with MGX, Bpifrance and Nvidia whose construction was "expected" to start in the second half of 2026.

**E. Financial and investment profile.** ACTUAL (closed equity): about €5.8bn across five rounds, the latest €3bn at a post-money valuation above €21bn. COMMITTED: US$830m of debt from seven banks including Bpifrance (March 2026). COMPANY-STATED, unaudited: annualised revenue above US$400m in February 2026, from about US$20m a year earlier; ANNOUNCED: US$1bn by end-2026 ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/mistral-ai-830m-debt-data-centre-paris); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/08/mistral-ai-raises-record-3-billion-in-samsung-led-funding-round)). A press statement that Mistral "has spent €4 billion on data centres" exceeds cash plausibly deployed and should be read as announced commitments. No profit figures are public. State money is modest: a minority Bpifrance stake, a share of the loan and a €6m services contract.

**F. Ecosystem and partnerships.** Investors ASML, Samsung, Nvidia, the EU-backed Scaleup Europe Fund and Luxembourg; partners Nvidia, HUMAIN (August 2026), Cloudera and Mozilla; a planned joint lab with Morocco's digital ministry. Microsoft took a small stake in 2024, which triggered a sovereignty debate.

**G. Achievements and impact.** Mistral is the only non-US, non-Chinese general-purpose model company in this study with broad private-sector traction across shipping, banking, aerospace and automotive. The cautions are scale and independence: its valuation is "far below" those of the US labs, its hardware is entirely Nvidia, its anchor shareholders are foreign corporates, and Carnegie notes that close ties to one government can deter other governments from co-investing ([Carnegie, Sept 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/09/artificial-intelligence-ai-development-breakout-germany-india-japan-uk-middle-power)). Its move into owned compute raises capital intensity sharply while revenue is still in the hundreds of millions.

### 5.5 LG AI Research (South Korea) — the best-benchmarked Korean model developer, with no evidenced external business

**A. Company overview.** LG AI Research is described as "the conglomerate's AI think tank", a group-level lab under the LG holding structure, co-headed by Lim Woo-hyung; it built EXAONE 1.0 in 2021 ([Korea Herald, 2 Mar 2026](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10685061)). Its founding date, headcount and precise legal status were not verified. It works through "One LG": LG Uplus, LG Electronics, LG Energy Solution and LG CNS are its deployment and infrastructure arms. There is no state ownership.

**B. Strategic mandate and national role.** Status comes from the state's Sovereign AI Foundation Model Project. LG AI Research ranked first of five consortia in Stage 1 (January 2026) and passed Stage 2 (August 2026) with Upstage and SK Telecom; support runs through 2027 ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/20098/lg-ai-tops-koreas-sovereign-ai-project-as-naver-cloud-fails-to-advance-msit-confirms); [Money Today, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.mt.co.kr/tech/2026/08/18/2026081812375747016)). The designation is contestable and time-limited: the ministry is weighing concentrating GPUs and funding on a single model.

**C. Business model and value proposition.** No external revenue is disclosed. The lab serves group affiliates; LG Uplus plans "end-to-end customized AI services built on Exaone and the Paju AIDC". Models are distributed as open weights for research and education, with commercial licensing restricted ([LG AI Research blog](https://www.lgresearch.ai/blog/view?seq=641)) [verify for licence terms].

**D. Technology and capabilities.** K-EXAONE (January 2026) is a 236bn-parameter mixture-of-experts model with about 23bn active parameters; it topped 10 of 13 Stage 1 benchmarks and scored 32 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, first in Korea and seventh among open-weight models worldwide ([Korea Times, 11 Jan 2026](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/business/tech-science/20260111/lgs-k-exaone-delivers-top-benchmark-results)). EXAONE 4.5 (April 2026) is its first open-weight vision-language model; its benchmark results are self-reported. A 750bn-parameter K-EXAONE 2.0 was reported by a single uncorroborated source [verify]. Group compute is ANNOUNCED: LG Uplus's Paju data centre, 200 MW and up to 120,000 GPUs by 2027. The models are proprietary in origin and trained from scratch, which is exactly what the state test rewards.

**E. Financial and investment profile.** No stand-alone financials were located, and the government project's budget and GPU allocation per team were not quantified in the sources.

**F. Ecosystem and partnerships.** Nvidia (cited for the EXAONE Path healthcare model and physical-AI cooperation) ([Nvidia, 30 Oct 2025](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/south-korea-ai-infrastructure)); LG affiliates; the science ministry (MSIT) and its agency NIPA.

**G. Achievements and impact.** LG AI Research is the Korean entity whose champion label is best supported by independent benchmarks. The limits are two. Korean models still trail: in August 2026 the best Korean score on the same index was 47 against 61–63 for leading US models. And a commercial footprint outside LG affiliates is not evidenced.

### 5.6 AI Singapore (Singapore) — a national programme office that adapts foreign models and trains people

**A. Company overview.** AI Singapore (AISG) is not a company. It was launched in May 2017 as a national programme, supported by the National Research Foundation and hosted by the National University of Singapore, with backing from Smart Nation, IMDA, Enterprise Singapore and EDB, and research partners across the country's universities and A*STAR ([AI Singapore](https://aisingapore.org/); [SEA-LION](https://sea-lion.ai/)). Its current executive leadership was not verified.

**B. Strategic mandate and national role.** The mandate is explicit: build deep national AI capabilities, grow local talent and develop the ecosystem. It co-leads the National Multimodal LLM Programme, launched in December 2023 with S$70m ([TechNode](https://technode.com/2025/11/25/singapores-national-ai-program-drops-meta-model-and-switches-to-alibabas-qwen/)). Singapore does not pursue a from-scratch frontier model.

**C. Business model and value proposition.** A publicly funded programme with three instruments. "100 Experiments" co-funds industry AI projects up to S$150,000, matched by the company; more than 300 projects are completed ([AISG 100E](https://aisingapore.org/?p=17086)). The AI Apprenticeship Programme is a nine-month, stipend-paid scheme for citizens, with more than 200 graduates and over 90% placement on a dated count ([AISG AIAP](https://aisingapore.org/the-ai-apprenticeship-programme/)). SEA-LION, a family of Southeast Asian language models, is distributed free for research and commercial use.

**D. Technology and capabilities.** SEA-LION is a fine-tune whose base has changed repeatedly: Meta's model family, then Alibaba's Qwen3-32B for version 4 (late 2025; Alibaba supplied technical support and about 100bn tokens), then Nvidia's Nemotron for version 4.8 in 2026, with Google's Gemma under its safety models. It covers more than eleven regional languages, and the 2026 line adds agentic, speech and guard models ([SEA-LION](https://sea-lion.ai/); [TechNode](https://technode.com/2025/11/25/singapores-national-ai-program-drops-meta-model-and-switches-to-alibabas-qwen/)). AISG owns no significant compute of its own in the sources.

**E. Financial and investment profile.** COMMITTED public funding: S$70m for the LLM programme; more than S$500m for AISG AI R&D (2024); S$500m for national high-performance compute; more than S$1bn for public AI research through 2030 (January 2026) ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/investment/40061698)). These figures partly overlap and should not be summed. AISG's annual budget is not disclosed.

**F. Ecosystem and partnerships.** NUS (host), the universities, A*STAR, IMDA; Alibaba Cloud, Nvidia and Google (model bases); industry partners in 100 Experiments such as GIC, Sompo and IBM.

**G. Achievements and impact.** Measurable impact is strongest in talent and applied projects. SEA-LION's impact is at ecosystem level: it has been adopted by Indonesia's GoTo, and version 4 topped a regional benchmark among open models under 200bn parameters at release. The model shows low lock-in — and that a "national" model built this way has its capabilities set upstream.

### 5.7 Sarvam AI (India) — a state-nurtured start-up, first among twelve

**A. Company overview.** Sarvam AI was founded in August 2023 in Bengaluru by Vivek Raghavan and Pratyush Kumar. It builds LLM, speech and multimodal platforms for governments, enterprises and developers and plans a San Francisco office ([ThePrint, 22 Jun 2026](https://theprint.in/science/sarvam-ai-unicorn-anthropic-ban/2966656/)). There is no government shareholding.

**B. Strategic mandate and national role.** Sarvam was the first start-up selected under the IndiaAI Mission's foundation-model pillar (April 2025) and is one of twelve selected organisations, with support of "up to ₹246.74 crore in financial and computing resources". The state has not given it exclusivity: the public-academic BharatGen consortium received roughly four times as much ([Analytics India Magazine](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-features/how-bharatgen-took-the-biggest-slice-of-indiaais-gpu-cake)).

Technology and capabilities.** Sarvam 105B and 30B are open-weight (Apache 2.0) mixture-of-experts models, announced in February 2026 and "trained entirely in India using compute provided under the IndiaAI Mission" — 4,096 Nvidia H100s for six months, supplied by Yotta. Independent benchmarking is sobering: Sarvam 105B scores 18 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index against 33 for a comparable US open model and 27 for Mistral Small 4, with high hallucination indicators ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/sarvam-105b-Sarvam-30b-everything-you-need-to-know)). COMMITTED public support: up to ₹246.74 crore, of which nearly ₹99 crore was phase-one compute subsidy. The compute access is time-bound; afterwards Sarvam must fund its own infrastructure, and 30–50% of the new round is earmarked for compute. Burn and headcount are undisclosed.

**F. Ecosystem and partnerships.** UIDAI (Aadhaar), the states of Odisha and Tamil Nadu (announced compute partnerships; terms not retrieved), HCLTech, Microsoft, Swiggy, Yotta.

**G. Achievements and impact.** The reference deployment is Aadhaar: voice interaction and fraud-alert services hosted on-premises in air-gapped UIDAI infrastructure, which the company says have served about 40 million residents (company-reported) ([Sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/stories/aadhaar); [UIDAI](https://uidai.gov.in/images/UIDAI_Partners_with_indigenous_GenAI_Company.pdf)). The work began under a volunteer policy, so it evidences adoption more than revenue. Sarvam is a credible national model champion in a deliberately plural field: strongest on mandate, openness and integration with public infrastructure; weakest on model quality, hardware and subsidy dependence.

### 5.8 Cautionary case: Aleph Alpha (Germany) — a champion elevated beyond its capital base, now being absorbed

**A. Company overview.** Aleph Alpha was founded in 2019 in Heidelberg by Jonas Andrulis and Samuel Weinbach and had about 250 staff at the time of the Cohere deal. Schwarz held more than 20% through Schwarz Digits; the full cap table is undisclosed ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/cohere-takes-over-aleph-alpha-shortly-after-the-german-startup-ousted-its-original-founder/)). No primary company page was retrieved; the profile relies on press and adviser releases.

**B. National role.** Described in German media as Europe's answer to OpenAI, it retreated to serving public authorities that cannot simply use US AI, with contracts at the federal digital ministry and the state of Baden-Württemberg.

**C. Business model.** In September 2024 it pivoted from building large models to PhariaAI, an "operating system" for generative AI in enterprises and government; the founder said a purely European LLM was no longer a viable business model.

**D. Technology.** The earlier Luminous model family and the Pharia suite; technical details were not re-verified.

**E. Financials.** The November 2023 Series B was ANNOUNCED as "over US$500m" from investors led by the Schwarz-backed Ipai, Bosch and SAP, but it included research commitments and pre-committed licences; only about **€110m** reportedly arrived as equity (REPORTED, not disclosed) ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/06/aleph-alpha-raises-500-million-to-build-a-european-rival-to-openai.html); [heise](https://heise.de/-11253050)) [verify]. Revenue in 2023 was reportedly €945,000 against a loss of €18.9m ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/europe/2024/09/07/aleph-alpha-germany-ai-startup-tech-silicon-valley/)) [verify].

**F. The Cohere transaction.** Announced 24 April 2026: Cohere takes over Aleph Alpha, whose shareholders receive roughly 10% of the combined company; Schwarz leads Cohere's Series E with €500m of structured financing, and the combined company is to run on STACKIT; Germany said it plans to be an anchor customer. A definitive agreement was signed on 16 September 2026 (COMMITTED); no report of closing was found by 15 November 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/25/why-cohere-is-merging-with-aleph-alpha/); [Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/09/16/cohere-aleph-alpha-combine-to-target-enterprise-ai-market)) [verify for the September signing].

**G. Assessment.** Aleph Alpha no longer functions as an independent national AI champion. The causes are documented: under-capitalisation by one to two orders of magnitude; headline funding that was largely commitments; corporate backers who steered it to integration work; state demand that arrived late and in small tickets. Germany's national-AI slot is now filled by a Canadian-controlled company hosted on a German cloud — sovereignty at the infrastructure and jurisdiction layer, not at the model-ownership layer.

## 7. National Strategies and Institutional Ecosystems

Each country note covers, as far as the research notes allow, the national digital, AI and cloud strategies; implementing institutions; sovereign capital; telecoms and national technology groups; research; procurement and demand creation; regulation and data sovereignty; and incentives — and then states how the champion fits and whether it emerged organically or was created or transformed by government. Where a dimension is not addressed, the notes held no sourced fact; the list of uncovered sub-topics is in Section 3.

### Middle East

#### Saudi Arabia — one owner behind every executor

**Strategy.** The National Strategy for Data and AI (NSDAI) was unveiled in October 2020 by SDAIA, with targets including 20,000 data and AI specialists and 300 active start-ups by 2030; a target of SAR 75bn of investment is cited by a secondary report ([Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/home/article/2577621/saudi-arabia-outlines-strategy-become-global-leader-ai-2030); [Qoyod](https://www.qoyod.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/qoyod-ai-adoption-en.pdf)) [verify]. Whether it has been refreshed since 2020 was not found. **Cloud policy and regulation.** A Cloud First Policy directs government and semi-government entities to consider cloud for new IT investments ([MCIT](https://www.mcit.gov.sa/sites/default/files/ksa_cloud_first_policy_en.pdf)) [verify]. The communications regulator's framework requires anyone controlling cloud infrastructure in the Kingdom to register, obliges holders of Saudi government data to use a registered provider, and bars moving that data abroad even temporarily ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2021/04/saudi-arabia-releases-version-3-of-its-cloud-computing-regulatory-framework)) [verify]. The data-protection law has been enforced since September 2024 with SDAIA as regulator. **Institutions.** SDAIA (strategy, data regulation, originator of ALLaM), the communications regulator CST, the Digital Government Authority and the ICT ministry ([Chambers TMT 2026](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/tmt-2026/saudi-arabia)) [verify]. **Sovereign capital.** PIF owns 51% of HUMAIN, 62% of stc and 51% of tower company TAWAL; Aramco owns 49% of HUMAIN. **Incentives.** A Cloud Computing Special Economic Zone, launched in April 2023 and supervised by CST, offers a hosting regime for hyperscalers ([CST](https://www.cst.gov.sa/en/services/licensing/Pages/Cloud-Computing-Special-Economic-Zone.aspx)) [verify]. Power-tariff or GPU subsidies were not retrieved. **Market.** A secondary guide counts five public-cloud providers with live in-Kingdom regions as of October 2026 (Google, Oracle, Alibaba via SCCC, Huawei, Tencent), with Microsoft due in Q4 2026 and AWS in December 2026 ([alskyline guide](https://alskyline.com/kb/cloud-regions-saudi-arabia-2026-guide)) [verify — aggregator]. **Champion fit and origin.** HUMAIN was deliberately created in 2025 and consolidated by the state. stc is a legacy state incumbent, partly floated and deliberately repositioned through the center3 carve-out and the TAWAL sale. Neither is organic. Strategy and regulation (SDAIA, CST, the ministry) are separated from commercial execution (HUMAIN, stc), with PIF owning both executors.

#### United Arab Emirates — customer, financier, coordinator and supplier under overlapping leadership

**Strategy and demand.** The Abu Dhabi Government Digital Strategy 2025–2027 commits AED 13bn to become "the world's first fully AI-native government by 2027", with 100% sovereign-cloud adoption and more than 200 AI solutions; the government reported in September 2025 more than 100 AI use cases across 40 entities ([Abu Dhabi Media Office](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/technology/abu-dhabi-government-digital-strategy-2025-2027-accelerates-ai-native-government-journey/)). The federal AI Strategy 2031 was not sourced. **Institutions.** The AI and Advanced Technology Council (chaired by Sheikh Tahnoon), the Department of Government Enablement, and ATRC. **Sovereign capital.** Mubadala and MGX; details of other Abu Dhabi funds were not sourced. **Telecoms.** They have moved to supporting roles: e& sold its Khazna stake, and du is building an AED 2bn hyperscale data centre with Microsoft as anchor tenant ([AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/tech/2025/04/uaes-du-and-microsoft-to-build-544m-hyperscale-data-centre/)). **Research.** MBZUAI co-develops Jais and K2 Think; TII produces Falcon. Only about 15% of UAE AI talent was educated in-country ([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/150631/the-trump-administration-throws-gulf-ai-a-lifeline/)). **Regulation and geopolitics.** The defining instrument is American: on 14 July 2026 the US moved the UAE to Country Group A:5, lifting licence requirements, with G42 named ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/14/2026-14132/enhanced-favorable-treatment-for-the-united-arab-emirates-under-the-export-administration)). **Champion fit and origin.** Government-created and tightly coordinated, yielding speed and scale but weak independent price discipline and limited evidence of private-sector pull.

#### Qatar — a designated champion without delivered capacity

Qatar's vehicle is Qai, an AI company owned by the Qatar Investment Authority and announced in December 2025; its chairman said it will not independently build large language models. Its main asset is an ANNOUNCED US$20bn joint venture with Brookfield, an investment envelope with no megawatts, sites or timeline disclosed ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/qatar-launches-ai-infrastructure-investment-venture-following-ai-company-launch/); [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/2025/12/09/brookfield-signs-ai-deal-with-qatari-company-qai-to-form-us20b-joint-venture/)). QIA's AI strategy is mainly outward financial investment (Anthropic, xAI, a Blue Owl data-centre platform) ([Reuters via Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/business/investment/qatars-wealth-fund-teams-up-with-blue-owl-for-3bln-data-centre-push-vij2uedy)). The only operating sovereign GPU cloud is Ooredoo's, launched in mid-2025 as an Nvidia Cloud Partner ([RCR Wireless](https://rcrwireless.com/20250704/ai-infrastructure/ooredoo-launches-ai)) on data centres run by Syntys, which operates four campuses totalling 26 MW and is building 20 MW more ([DCD on Syntys](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/syntys-breaks-ground-on-20mw-data-center-in-qatar/)). The research asset is Fanar, an Arabic model from QCRI at Hamad Bin Khalifa University, sponsored by the ICT ministry; Fanar 2.0 was trained on 256 Nvidia H100 GPUs ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16397)). A QAR 9bn digital incentive package was cited by the minister in 2024 ([The Peninsula](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/17/05/2024/data-centre-capacity-to-grow-three-times-in-five-to-six-years)). Qatar needs US export licences for advanced chips. **Assessment:** investor first, operator second; Qai is a champion in mandate, not yet in capacity. The national AI strategy was not sourced.

#### Bahrain — a policy pioneer whose hyperscaler model was physically destroyed

Bahrain never had a national champion. Its Cloud First Policy (2017) was the first in the region to mandate cloud for government, and AWS opened a region in 2019; AWS reports 72 government entities migrated and more than 85% of government workload in the cloud ([AWS Public Sector Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/publicsector/bahrains-cloud-first-success-story/)). Iranian strikes from March 2026 damaged the facilities, and on 15 September 2026 AWS said it is "unable to restore access to the resources and data hosted exclusively in this Region", deferring next steps to early 2027 ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/17/aws-middle-east-outage-permanent-data-loss-bahrain-uae/)). Telecom Beyon/Batelco commissioned a modest solar-powered facility at its "Data Oasis" campus, and the labour fund Tamkeen aims to train 50,000 Bahrainis in AI by 2030 ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/events-conferences/batelco-by-beyon-announces-commissioning-of-bahrains-first-white-space-data-centre-during-gateway-gulf-2025-508462); [Tamkeen](https://tamkeen.bh/en/ai-skills-training-tamkeen/)). Where government workloads now run was not found. Bahrain is the strongest empirical counter-argument to the idea that in-country hosting equals resilience.

### Europe

#### France — the state built a qualification, and consortia formed around it

**Strategy and regulation.** The "cloud de confiance" doctrine (May 2021) and the "Cloud au centre" rules make cloud the default for new state systems and require SecNumCloud-qualified hosting, immune from non-EU authority access, for data whose breach could harm public order, security, health or intellectual property ([numerique.gouv.fr](https://www.numerique.gouv.fr/services/cloud/doctrine/)). The SREN law of May 2024 and its 2026 implementing texts make that mandatory for particularly sensitive data. SecNumCloud is not recognised EU-wide, carries a 25–40% cost premium, and does not cover the AI layer ([Banque des Territoires](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/dependances-numeriques-health-data-hub-et-secnumcloud-au-menu-de-la-commission-denquete)). **AI strategy and capital.** At the February 2025 AI Action Summit the president announced €109bn of AI investment, an aggregate of announced private and foreign intentions ([Wikipedia FR: Sommet](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sommet_pour_l%27action_sur_l%27intelligence_artificielle)). Bpifrance is a shareholder of and lender to Mistral and a partner in the 1.4 GW campus joint venture with MGX and Nvidia. France 2030 allocations were not sourced. **Institutions.** ANSSI (qualification), DINUM (doctrine), UGAP (central purchasing), AMIAD (defence AI), GENCI/CNRS/CEA (public compute). **Research and compute.** The Jean Zay supercomputer (1,456 H100 GPUs among others) and Alice Recoque, an exascale system costing €554m over five years with production targeted for 2027 ([The Star/Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/11/19/france039s-eviden-to-build-new-supercomputer-with-amd-in-european-ai-push)). **Telecoms and groups.** Orange (Bleu), Thales (S3NS, on Google technology), Iliad (Scaleway), Dassault Systèmes (Outscale), and the NumSpot consortium. **Critique.** The Cour des comptes (October 2025) judged the strategy late and uncosted, and noted that the state's own Health Data Hub runs on Microsoft ([Next.ink](https://next.ink/207027/souverainete-numerique-la-cour-des-comptes-etrille-le-manque-de-cohesion-en-france/)). **Champion fit and origin.** OVHcloud, Scaleway and Mistral are market-born; Bleu, S3NS and NumSpot are responses to a regulatory niche the state defined. The precedent to avoid is France's own: the Andromède programme of 2009–2012 co-financed two rival telecom-led clouds (Cloudwatt, Numergy) that earned single-digit millions and were absorbed or shut ([Wikipedia: Cloudwatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloudwatt)).

#### Germany — procurement and standards as industrial policy, private capital for the build

Since the 2025 coalition a new digital ministry (BMDS) owns the "Deutschland-Stack", a common technology and standards base for government by 2028, and the IT Planning Council has made Sovereign Cloud Stack standards binding from 2028 ([Bundesdruckerei](https://www.bundesdruckerei.de/en/innovation-hub/what-deutschland-stack); [heise](https://heise.de/-11222752)) [verify]. The €250m federal AI cloud tender excluded US hyperscalers by design. A 2026 roadmap, "Deutschlands Weg zur KI-Nation 2030", and a data-centre strategy aim to double data-centre capacity and quadruple AI capacity by 2030 ([CIO.de](https://www.cio.de/article/4244636/digital-gipfel-2026-kanzler-merz-blaest-zur-ki-offensive.html)) [verify]. The state holds 28.6% of DT but funds little directly; the big builds (DT, Schwarz) are privately financed. Germany hosts two EU AI Factories. SAP is the indispensable integrator in every sovereign construct (DT's AI cloud, Delos, STACKIT, the federal AI cloud) but declines to own infrastructure; its Delos Cloud runs Microsoft Azure technology under German operation and is the vehicle for "OpenAI for Germany" ([SAP News](https://news.sap.com/2025/09/sap-openai-partner-launch-sovereign-openai-germany/)) [verify]. **Champion fit and origin.** Schwarz is organically private; DT is a legacy part-state incumbent; the exception to the state's hands-off stance is its political brokering of the Cohere–Aleph Alpha deal. Germany's stronger AI companies are vertical specialists: Helsing (defence AI, valued at US$18bn in July 2026) has the clearest state-demand link ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/138452-helsing-raises-1-8b-at-18b-valuation-a-record-for-german-defence/)) [verify].

#### United Kingdom — sovereignty defined as assured access, not ownership

The UK Compute Roadmap (July 2025) commits up to £2bn to 2030, including more than £1bn to expand the public AI Research Resource twentyfold, and defines sovereignty pragmatically, explicitly not as self-sufficiency ([UK Compute Roadmap](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68791dc82bad77c3dae4dddb/uk_compute_roadmap_print-version.pdf)). A Sovereign AI Unit, launched as a state-backed venture fund in April 2026 with up to £500m, caps equity tickets at £20m ([UKTN](https://www.uktech.news/government-policy/uk-government-launches-500m-sovereign-ai-unit-20260416)) [verify; a conflicting £700m/£140m figure is unresolved]. The one credible infrastructure contender is Nscale, valued at US$14.6bn in March 2026 after raising about US$4.9bn in a year, with a US$14bn Microsoft agreement for about 200,000 GPUs across the US, Portugal and Norway ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366639801/AI-factory-builder-Nscale-announces-another-2bn-of-funding)). It is a GPU landlord for US customers more than a sovereign cloud. A headline suggests OpenAI paused Stargate UK in spring 2026; this could not be verified. BT plays a minor role, a partnership under which Nscale builds up to 14 MW at three BT sites ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/bt-nscale-nvidia-14mw-sovereign-ai-data-centres-uk)). **Assessment:** no domestic frontier-model champion and no sovereign cloud champion; one emerging, privately and largely US-financed infrastructure company.

#### Switzerland — sovereign capability is public and academic

There is no commercial champion of scale. The Swiss AI Initiative (EPFL, ETH Zurich, the national supercomputing centre CSCS) released the open Apertus model in 2025, trained on the "Alps" supercomputer, and Apertus 1.5 in July 2026; CSCS states "Apertus is not about competing with frontier models from private companies" ([CSCS](https://www.cscs.ch/science/computer-science-hpc/2026/apertus-15-building-the-next-generation-of-open-ai-infrastructure)). Swisscom, 51% owned by the Confederation, offers Apertus to business customers on Nvidia systems ([Wikipedia: Swisscom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swisscom); [Blick](https://www.blick.ch/digital/sprachmodell-apertus-jetzt-kommt-das-chatgpt-aus-der-schweiz-id21191706.html)) [verify for the platform]. **Assessment:** a state-created, research-led model; Swisscom is a sovereign hosting utility, not an AI champion. The Swiss Government Cloud programme was not verified.

#### Netherlands — a small public model project and no champion

GPT-NL, built by TNO, SURF and the Netherlands Forensic Institute with €13.5m from the economics ministry, entered government pilots in February 2026 ([NL Times](https://nltimes.nl/2026/02/26/dutch-government-agencies-pilot-homegrown-ai-model-gpt-nl)). EuroHPC approved €70m for an AI factory in Groningen, due to operate in early 2028 ([Techzine](https://www.techzine.eu/news/infrastructure/135362/the-netherlands-to-get-ai-factory-thanks-to-e70-million-from-europe)) [verify]. KPN's sovereign cloud is partly built on Schwarz's STACKIT. Nebius is Amsterdam-headquartered but is the restructured former Yandex holding with capacity in Finland, France and the US; it is not a Dutch champion ([Wikipedia: Nebius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebius_Group)).

#### Sweden — the most organic consortium route, still pre-operational

Sferical AI, launched in September 2025 and backed by AstraZeneca, Ericsson, Saab, SEB and Wallenberg Investments, aims to provide sovereign compute to Swedish industry ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ericsson-joins-astrazeneca-saab-in-swedish-ai-infrastructure-jv/)) [verify]. Brookfield ANNOUNCED up to SEK 95bn for AI infrastructure centred on Strängnäs, and Telia became its exclusive sovereign AI services partner for Sweden in March 2026, with facilities "dependent on timely access to power" ([TelecomTV](https://telecomtv.com/content/ai/telia-brookfield-plot-sovereign-ai-push-55095)). Sweden hosts an EU AI Factory. Lovable, valued at US$13.3bn in August 2026, is an application company, not a sovereign capability ([Wikipedia: Lovable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovable_%28company%29)). **Assessment:** no established champion, but a credible pathway because the demand-side anchors co-own the compute vehicle and a family foundation supplies patient capital.

#### Finland — a host nation for European public compute

Finland's strength is public supercomputing: the national centre CSC hosts LUMI and, from 2027, LUMI-AI, for which the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking signed a €387.8m contract on 31 August 2026, half EU-funded and half funded by a six-country consortium ([EuroHPC JU](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/eurohpc-ju-signs-contract-deploy-lumi-ai-supercomputer-2026-08-31_en)). No Finnish cloud or model champion exists.

#### The EU layer

The EU has selected 19 AI Factories and launched a tender in July 2026 for AI gigafactories of more than 100,000 advanced processors each, with nothing yet awarded ([European Commission, AI Factories](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-factories)). A Cloud and AI Development Act was proposed in mid-2026 with a sovereignty framework governing access to public procurement; it is a proposal, not law ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/proposal-cloud-and-ai-development-act-cada)) [verify]. Gaia-X stands as the cautionary example of a standards body mistaken for a cloud ([Wikipedia: Gaia-X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaia-X)).

### Asia-Pacific

#### South Korea — a tournament state steering private conglomerates

The AI Basic Act took effect on 22 January 2026, creating a National AI Committee chaired by the president ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways)). A National Growth Fund of KRW 150tn is being built, with KRW 6tn for AI in 2026 ([Asia Economy](https://cm.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026010911075380438)) [verify]. The state acts as tournament organiser (the sovereign model project), anchor GPU buyer (up to 50,000 GPUs for the National AI Computing Center and three domestic cloud providers) and co-investor. The National AI Computing Center, a KRW 2.4tn project for 15,000 GPUs by 2028, needed three tenders before a Samsung SDS-led private consortium took it on; ground was broken in August 2026 ([Asia Economy, 3 Aug 2026](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026080313080357696)). Nvidia's "over 260,000 GPUs" for Korea are plans ([Nvidia, 30 Oct 2025](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/south-korea-ai-infrastructure)). Research institutions (KISTI, ETRI) and domestic AI-chip firms (FuriosaAI, Rebellions) participate. The cloud-security certification regime and public-cloud market shares were not sourced. **Champion fit and origin.** Champions are organically grown private firms that the state steers and co-finances; status is contestable, and the state has had to concede control to private consortia to get projects built.

#### Singapore — state capital orchestrating foreign technology

Budget 2024 allocated S$1bn over five years for AI, partly to secure GPU access; Budget 2026 reportedly created a National AI Council chaired by the prime minister ([Techgoondu](https://www.techgoondu.com/2024/02/19/singapores-new-s1-billion-gambit-to-be-a-pre-eminent-ai-hub/); [EDB](https://www.edb.gov.sg/en/news-and-insights/latest-in-singapores-ai-scene-that-businesses-should-know-a-round-up-from-january-to-march-2026)) [verify for 2026]. State capital runs through Temasek (Singtel; the seller of STT GDC) and the National Research Foundation (AISG). The compute mandate is about access and in-country hosting, not ownership of the stack. Power is the binding constraint, pushing growth to Johor and Batam. **Champion fit and origin.** State-created or state-transformed (a privatised telecom still majority state-owned; a programme office), orchestrating KKR, Nvidia, Alibaba and Google around state-linked assets.

#### India — a distributed model with no single champion

The IndiaAI Mission (March 2024; ₹10,372 crore over five years) subsidises GPU-hours on privately owned capacity pooled through a government portal, funds twelve foundation-model teams and relies on digital public infrastructure as reference demand. By March 2026 it had onboarded 38,231 GPUs at a subsidised rate around ₹65 per GPU-hour ([TelecomTalk](https://telecomtalk.info/over-38000-gpus-onboarded-under-indiaai-mission/1005751/)) [verify]. Execution is the weak point: only about ₹400 crore had reportedly been released by mid-2026, the portal is paperwork-heavy, and a think tank warned capacity is "at risk of being underused" ([Communications Today](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/38000-gpus-one-big-problem-indias-ai-mission-cant-find-enough-users/)). Scale is shifting to conglomerates acting as landlords for US firms: Reliance at Jamnagar for Google and Meta; Tata's HyperVault with OpenAI as first customer (100 MW initially); Airtel's Nxtra and AdaniConneX for Google at Visakhapatnam ([Google Cloud press corner](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-04-28-Google-Breaks-Ground-on-India-AI-Hub,-Launching-a-National-Industrial-Ecosystem-Alongside-Indias-Digital-Infrastructure-Milestone); [The Core](https://www.thecore.in/business/reliance-commits-rs-10-lakh-crore-to-build-ai-data-centres-in-india-858773); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/openai-becomes-first-customer-of-tcs-data-centre-business-aims-1-gw-capacity-building)). A 2026 budget proposal for a tax holiday for foreign cloud providers using Indian data centres favours that landlord model. Independent GPU clouds lack demand density: E2E Networks ran at 35–40% utilisation, and its 21% shareholder L&T chose a US neocloud for a 10,000-GPU cluster ([The Ken](https://the-ken.com/story/lts-rs-15000-cr-bet-with-nvidia-is-a-gut-punch-to-a-struggling-e2e/)). Data-protection and localisation rules were not sourced. **Champion fit and origin.** All private and organic; the state is market-maker and subsidiser, not owner.

#### Malaysia — a private champion and a state track that are not joined up

YTL Power, a private conglomerate, built with Nvidia a Blackwell-based facility in its 600 MW Kulai, Johor data-centre park, operates "YTL AI Cloud" and produced the ILMU Malay model, later re-based on Nvidia's Nemotron open models; the prime minister endorsed it as "our own sovereign AI" ([YTL Power](https://www.ytlpowerinternational.com/press-releases/malaysia-accelerates-sovereign-ai-ambition-nvidia-and-ytl-brief-prime-minister-anwar-ibrahim-at-apec-summit/); [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/03/17/ytl-ai-labs-partners-nvidia-to-boost-malaysias-ai-capabilities-with-ilmu-nemo-30b-model)). GPU counts, customers and AI revenue are undisclosed; the RM10bn programme value is ANNOUNCED. The state's instruments are newer: a 2021 panel that licensed three US hyperscalers plus Telekom Malaysia for government cloud ([The Edge](http://theedgemalaysia.com/article/microsoft-google-amazon-tm-given-conditional-approval-build-hyperscale-data-centres)); a National Cloud Computing Policy (August 2025) mandating public-sector cloud adoption; an RM2bn "Sovereign AI Cloud" assigned to the telecom regulator in Budget 2026, not yet shown operational ([Sinar Daily](https://www.sinardaily.my/ampArticle/730888)); and AI Malaysia Bhd, launched in July 2026 with a National AI Action Plan 2026–2030 ([The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/812301)). A Malaysian think tank warns that the ecosystem "still depends heavily on foreign engineering and imported hardware" ([ISIS Malaysia](https://www.isis.org.my/2025/10/27/owning-the-future-malaysias-sovereign-ai-cloud/)). The sovereign wealth fund's role was not found. **Origin:** organic private champion with state endorsement; state vehicles are younger and smaller.

#### Indonesia — a telecom-led private champion and a state track at MoU stage

Indosat Ooredoo Hutchison's subsidiary Lintasarta launched "GPU Merdeka" in August 2024 as Indonesia's first Nvidia Cloud Partner, in a third-party (BDx) facility, and co-developed the open Sahabat-AI models with GoTo, initially a fine-tune of Meta's Llama and now moving to Nvidia's Nemotron ([Lintasarta](https://www.lintasarta.net/en/news/lintasarta-introduced-ai-merdeka-to-empowering-indonesias-ai-sovereignty); [Nvidia blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/indonesia-tech-leaders-sovereign-ai)). GPU counts and investment are undisclosed. The state track is the sovereign fund Danantara with state-owned Telkom: an October 2026 MoU for a "National AI Data Center Hub" and plans for up to 1 GW, all at MoU or planning stage ([Katadata](https://katadata.co.id/digital/teknologi/6ae46777acc34/telkom-danantara-pln-kembangkan-hub-pusat-data-ai-berbasis-kawasan-industri)). The national AI roadmap was still awaiting presidential regulation in mid-2026 ([Tech For Good Institute](https://techforgoodinstitute.org/insights/country-spotlights/consolidation-without-completion-indonesias-ai-developments-in-2026/)). The June 2024 ransomware attack on the temporary national data centre, where most agencies had no backups, weakened the case for a government-run cloud ([Fulcrum](https://fulcrum.sg/indonesias-national-data-centre-ransomware-attack-a-digital-governance-failure/)). **Origin:** organic, privately controlled and foreign-co-owned telecom champion; the state is re-entering through its fund.

### Africa, Latin America and Turkey

#### Morocco — a dense strategy stack and no champion yet

Morocco has Maroc Digital 2030 (September 2024), the "Maroc IA 2030" roadmap (January 2026) and a sovereign-cloud decree drafted by the cybersecurity directorate DGSSI (November 2024), but as of late 2026 the state's own compute assets are at land, convention or study stage and the only operational hyperscaler infrastructure is Oracle's Casablanca region. No entity qualifies as a national champion. The full analysis is in Section 11.

#### Brazil — a layered, state-led model without an integrator

The national AI plan PBIA commits R$23bn over 2024–2028; in August 2026 the government announced a tender for an AI supercomputer costing up to R$1bn, realistically a 2028 asset ([Mobile Time](https://www.mobiletime.com.br/noticias/20/08/2026/governo-supercomputador2/)). State IT companies (Serpro, Dataprev) are the sovereign operators; public compute sits at the national laboratory LNCC; the Redata tax regime, signed into law on 15 September 2026, suspends federal taxes on data-centre equipment to attract mostly foreign-owned capacity ([O Povo](https://mais.opovo.com.br/jornal/economia/2026/09/16/sancionado-regime-especial-para-incentivar-instalacao-de-datacenters.html)); and the development bank BNDES lends to domestic players such as Magalu Cloud (R$300m) ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/133020-brazils-bndes-backs-magalu-cloud-with-r-300m-for-cloud-expansion/)) [verify]. Private candidates are small: Maritaca AI is a technically credible Portuguese-model start-up with undisclosed funding, and the Piauí-led SoberanIA launched a 30bn-parameter public-sector model in May 2026 ([Teletime](https://teletime.com.br/19/05/2026/soberania-lanca-ia-generativa-em-portugues-com-foco-no-setor-publico/)). Brazil hedges between US and Chinese suppliers, with Huawei a homologated sovereign stack. **Origin:** state-created operators; no private champion.

#### Turkey — localisation with openness, and plural model efforts

The AI Vision and Action Plan 2026–2030 (June 2026) targets at least 1 GW of data-centre capacity and US$10bn of mostly private capital by 2030, earmarks at least 2% of public investment for AI and makes the public sector the main buyer of domestic AI ([Invest in Türkiye](https://agencyadm.invest.gov.tr/en/News/News-from-Turkey/Pages/turkiye-unveils-ai-action-plan-artificial-intelligence-summit.aspx)). The Cybersecurity Presidency, created in 2025, enforces in-country storage without banning foreign vendors. The Wealth Fund anchors both telecoms. Three parallel Turkish-model efforts exist — the Baykar-linked T3 AI, the research council TÜBİTAK's "Bilge", and defence firm Havelsan's platform — none with a confirmed general release or independent benchmarks; TÜBİTAK trained on a EuroHPC allocation at Barcelona ([EuroHPC JU](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/turkish-specific-large-language-model-information-processing-and-contextual-analysis_en)). **Origin:** state-anchored incumbents repositioned; no model champion.

#### South Africa — a deep market without a champion

South Africa has the continent's deepest data-centre and hyperscaler market, dominated by foreign-owned operators (Teraco/Digital Realty with nearly 190 MW, Microsoft, AWS, Google) ([TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/south-africa-puts-data-centres-on-par-with-energy-ports-in-big-policy-shift/278142/)). The 2024 National Data and Cloud Policy dropped the idea of a state-owned cloud in favour of brokerage by the state IT agency SITA plus domestic residency for security-sensitive data ([TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/state-scraps-plan-own-cloud-data-centres/245712/)). The draft national AI policy was withdrawn in 2026 after AI-hallucinated references were found in it; a revised version is targeted for public comment in January 2027 ([SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/south-africa/cabinet-approves-withdrawal-ai-policy)). Electricity is the main constraint. Elsewhere in Africa, Kenya's US$1bn Microsoft–G42 geothermal data centre stalled over power and payment guarantees ([Semafor](https://semafor.com/article/05/06/2026/energy-shortfall-problem-scuppers-kenyas-1b-microsoft-data-center)).

## 8. Cross-Country Business and Governance Models

### Seven models, and what each one requires

No published typology of champion ownership structures was found; the scheme below is this report's synthesis of the cases. It extends the six models named in the brief with a seventh that the evidence shows is spreading fastest.

| Model | Examples | What it requires | Demonstrated strength | Demonstrated weakness | Replicability in emerging economies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. State-created and state-backed vehicle | HUMAIN; Serpro; Qai; TII | Fiscal capacity or a captive mandate; political sponsorship | Speed of consolidation; formal demand | Delivery and disclosure lag; weak price discipline | Low for capital-heavy variants; moderate for a Serpro-style operator |
| 2. Sovereign-wealth-fund-backed technology group | G42/Core42/Khazna/MGX; Singtel (Temasek); stc (PIF) | Large sovereign capital; alignment with Washington for chips | Scale; audited subsidiaries; global deal access | Governance concentration; geopolitical contingency | Very low without sovereign capital |
| 3. Telecom-led digital infrastructure champion | DT/T-Systems; Singtel/Nxera; Turkcell; stc/center3; KT; Indosat/Lintasarta | A telecom with balance sheet, sites and state trust | Real assets; connectivity; cash flow | Sub-scale cloud products; landlord drift; foreign stacks | High — most countries have such a telecom |
| 4. Private-sector national champion | OVHcloud; Schwarz Digits; Mistral; Naver; YTL; Yotta; Sarvam | Domestic capital markets or a cash-rich parent; a home market | Product velocity; commercial customers | Capital-constrained against US rivals; foreign capital creeps in | Moderate; depends on a private actor existing |

### How governments mobilise capital

Four routes appear. **Direct sovereign equity** is confined to the Gulf: Aramco's SAR 9.9bn into HUMAIN, Mubadala's stake in G42, the QIA-owned Qai. **State banks and development banks as minority financiers** is the European and Latin American route: Bpifrance as shareholder, lender and joint-venture partner around Mistral; KfW's holding in Deutsche Telekom; BNDES credit to Magalu Cloud; Korea's National Growth Fund co-funding the National AI Computing Center. **Demand-side subsidy** is India's route: the state buys GPU-hours, so, as Yotta's CEO put it, service providers "take on the investment risks" ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/start-up/deeptech/yotta-to-provide-over-9000-gpus-in-support-of-india-ai-mission)). **Tax and zoning incentives** attract mostly foreign capacity: Brazil's Redata (an estimated R$5.2bn waiver in 2026), Saudi Arabia's cloud economic zone, the UK's AI Growth Zones. The first route buys speed and puts obsolescence risk on the public balance sheet; the third transfers that risk to private providers at the cost of weaker control.

### How sovereign investment, private capital and international partnerships are combined

The most developed financing structure is the offtake-backed blend. HUMAIN targets 30% shareholder and 70% third-party funding, raising debt against offtake agreements; Singtel funds S$0.7bn of S$1.2bn of data-centre and AI growth capex from partners and customer advances and co-owns its platforms with KKR; Khazna brought in MGX and Silver Lake; Mistral financed its first owned site with a seven-bank loan and pre-sells "compute units" to an anchor group; Yotta lets partners own the GPUs and keeps about a quarter of the revenue. The pattern converges with the neocloud model, and inherits its fragility: GPU-backed lending depends on residual value and utilisation, and investment-grade ratings on such debt reflect the creditworthiness of the take-or-pay tenant, not of the operator ([Quartz](https://qz.com/gpu-collateralized-debt-ai-neocloud-coreweave-financing-risks-050526?.tsrc=rss); [Creative Strategies](https://creativestrategies.com/research/neoclouds-the-backlog-quality-test/)). A state-backed GPU cloud without creditworthy long-term offtake carries utilisation and residual-value risk on the sovereign balance sheet.

### How countries secure GPUs and compute

Access to advanced chips outside the US and China is now a negotiated political good. The Biden-era diffusion rule was rescinded in May 2025; since then access has been set deal by deal. In November 2025 the US authorised 35,000 Nvidia Blackwell processors split between G42 and HUMAIN, "enough to fill a 100 MW data center in each country", against requests many times larger; in July 2026 it upgraded the UAE alone to ally status ([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/150631/the-trump-administration-throws-gulf-ai-a-lifeline/)). Five practical routes are visible in the cases: buy through a national tender (Korea's up-to-50,000 GPUs); let private providers buy and subsidise usage (India); let tenants bring their own chips and export licences (HUMAIN's build-to-suit model); rent hyperscaler or neocloud capacity (Mistral before 2026); and share regional public compute (Turkey training its model at Barcelona through EuroHPC; LUMI-AI's six-country consortium). Alternatives to Nvidia exist at the margin — AMD at HUMAIN and in the French and Finnish supercomputers, Cerebras in G42's work, Huawei in Brazil's sovereign stack — but Nvidia supplied 52% of tracked AI infrastructure projects worldwide ([Carnegie, June 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/06/early-lessons-in-the-pursuit-of-sovereign-ai)).

### How sovereignty is balanced against global partnerships

Every country in the study balances the same way in practice: control the jurisdictional, data and operational layers, and rent the technological layer. The differences are in honesty and in design. France writes the balance into a qualification (European ownership, immunity from non-EU law) and then allows US stacks inside it. Turkey requires in-country storage and institutional control without banning foreign vendors, which pulled a Google region into Turkcell's buildings. Brazil grades data: public data on hyperscalers through a state broker, classified data on state-operated infrastructure, and it proposes to test the boundary with a disconnection test. Korea alone tried to enforce technological sovereignty at the model layer, eliminating Naver for using frozen foreign weights — and the firm it eliminated then announced its next model would be built on Nvidia's. The UK says plainly that sovereignty is not self-sufficiency. The UAE has gone furthest toward partnership, to the point where its champion may become a US company.

### How domestic demand is created through procurement

Demand-side instruments have the best record in the evidence. The EU used its sovereignty framework as a pass/fail minimum and a scored award criterion in a concluded tender ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/2ad80a48-166f-4c77-a513-80c53ca2a128_en)). Germany designed its €250m federal AI cloud so that no lot went to a US hyperscaler. France's doctrine reserves sensitive public data for qualified offers and its armed forces signed a framework with Mistral. Brazil gives Serpro a priority rule with price caps. Abu Dhabi set a 100% sovereign-cloud target for its own government. Turkey's plan earmarks at least 2% of public investment for AI. The limits are also documented: Brazil's uptake was 11 bodies after the rule; the French state itself put its health data on Microsoft; Gartner expects about 80% of sovereign spend to come from new workloads rather than repatriation. Regulation alone does not move share: European providers' share has been flat at 15% since 2022.

### How talent and research are developed

The clearest instruments are Singapore's: a nine-month paid apprenticeship tied to real industry projects, and co-funded industry experiments. Abu Dhabi built a graduate university (MBZUAI) and a state lab (TII) whose researchers co-author the national models. Korea, Turkey and India use competitions and subsidised compute for model teams. Public supercomputers (Jean Zay, Alps, LUMI, Santos Dumont, Morocco's Toubkal) build skills and research access rather than commercial champions. The constraint is retention as much as training: only about 15% of UAE AI talent was educated in-country, against nearly 70% in the US ([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/150631/the-trump-administration-throws-gulf-ai-a-lifeline/)). The EU framework scores the existence of a local talent pool able to operate the service without the foreign vendor, which is the operational meaning of talent for sovereignty.

### How champions move from infrastructure to platforms and applications

Few have. The entities with real application revenue started there: Presight (government analytics), Naver (a consumer platform that exports digital-twin software), Mistral (models first, compute later), Sarvam (voice services on public infrastructure). Infrastructure-first champions have mostly stayed infrastructure: Khazna, center3, Turkcell, Nxera and Yotta sell space, power and GPU-hours. The attempts to climb are instructive. HUMAIN bundles a chat product and an operating layer with its compute. KT proposes a "token factory" that brokers and bills model usage. Deutsche Telekom relies on SAP for the platform layer. Mistral is moving the other way, down into owned compute, to control cost and offer sovereign capacity. The evidence suggests that the durable route up the stack is through a captive application domain — government services, a national language, an industrial vertical — not through generic platform competition with hyperscalers.

### How sustainable commercial models are built

Sustainable models in the set share three features: a cash-generating core (telecom at stc, Singtel, DT, Turkcell; retail at Schwarz; search and commerce at Naver; government IT at Serpro), contracted offtake before capacity is built (Nxera's Tuas site was over 90% committed before opening; DT's first Munich phase is reported sold out), and partner capital for the chip-heavy part. Unsustainable patterns are equally consistent: announcing gigawatts before securing tenants; relying on investees as customers; subsidised domestic prices below export prices; and leverage against assets that depreciate on an annual product cadence. Margin evidence is thin but pointed: OVHcloud converts a 42% EBITDA margin into €9.4m of net income; Naver's operating profit fell 20.9% in a quarter on AI investment before any AI-factory revenue; Sify loses money at group level.

### How champions expand internationally

Four export propositions exist. **Capital export** (MGX, QIA, HUMAIN's stakes in xAI and Luma) buys access and returns, not sovereignty. **Government-to-government systems** (Presight in Kazakhstan, Jordan and Albania; Naver's Saudi platforms) is the most proven. **Sovereign infrastructure abroad** (Khazna in Italy and Saudi Arabia; G42 in Kenya, Vietnam and India; Naver in Morocco; Nxera in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand; Mistral in Sweden) is mostly announced: Kenya stalled, Morocco "has not progressed", and only Nxera's regional joint ventures and Mistral's sites show construction. **Open models** (Jais, Falcon, SEA-LION, Mistral's open weights) travel freely and cheaply. No champion in this study has yet demonstrated an operating sovereign AI data centre outside its home region.

## 9. Success Factors, Risks and Lessons Learned

### What separates the cases that work from those that do not

Five success factors recur. **Committed demand precedes supply**: the failures (Andromède's two clouds, Aleph Alpha, India's under-used portal) had capital or capacity without bound customers; the successes (Presight, Nxera Tuas, DT Munich, Mistral's defence and enterprise contracts) had customers first. **A cash-generating parent or core** absorbs the long payback. **A defensible niche** — language, public-sector workflows, legal immunity — substitutes for scale. **Honest sovereignty claims** survive scrutiny: Singapore and the UK claim little and carry little narrative risk; Naver and KT claimed more than their stacks support and were challenged at home. **Disclosure** correlates with credibility: the listed entities (OVHcloud, Presight, stc, Singtel, DT) can be assessed; HUMAIN, G42 and Core42 cannot.

Five risks recur. **Announcement inflation**: 1.1 MW live against 3 GW at HUMAIN; no commissioning notice at Stargate UAE; about 36 MW with any dated commitment against roughly 1.4 GW announced in Morocco. **Substituted dependence**: sovereign capacity built on US chips and stacks shifts dependence to Washington's discretion rather than removing it, and that discretion moved in both directions for the UAE between 2023 and 2026. **Financial fragility**: chips are 40–70% of capital cost and depreciate fast. **Governance concentration and circularity**: one patron across operator, financier and regulator in Abu Dhabi; customers who are also shareholders and lenders at Mistral; investees as tenants at HUMAIN. **Physical and energy exposure**: the destroyed AWS Bahrain region; Singapore's power ceiling; Kenya's grid; water and grid constraints in Morocco and Brazil.

The lessons from failure are specific. France's Andromède split state money between two rival telecom-led consortia with no public demand commitment, and both were absorbed or shut. Gaia-X showed that a federation and labelling scheme does not create supply. Aleph Alpha showed that a champion cannot be declared into existence on about €110m of real equity. Kenya showed that a hyperscale project collapses when the host state will not guarantee capacity payments and the grid cannot carry the load. South Africa showed that a state-owned cloud ambition and a careless policy text can each cost years. Bahrain showed that single-country, single-provider residency is a concentration risk.

### Question 1. What makes a company a genuine national cloud or AI champion rather than simply a successful technology company?

A genuine champion combines four things, and the cases show each can be tested. First, **the state would be materially worse off without it**: it hosts government workloads (Serpro, Core42), holds a qualification others lack (OVHcloud's three SecNumCloud offers), or is the designated developer of a national capability (LG AI Research, Sarvam, AI Singapore). Second, **a conferring act exists** — ownership, a legal mandate, a tournament result, a procurement award — rather than self-description; Brazil's Portaria, Germany's 70% lot and Korea's Stage 1 ranking are conferring acts, while "sovereign" on a website is not. Third, **control sits in the country at the layers the country cares about**: the entity cannot be compelled by a foreign court to hand over data, and it can keep operating if a foreign vendor withdraws. Fourth, **delivery**: operating capacity, shipped models, paying customers. By these tests Lovable, DeepL and Nscale are successful technology companies; Aleph Alpha was a champion by declaration only; Bleu is a champion-in-waiting; and HUMAIN passes on the first two tests and has yet to pass the fourth. The corollary is that champion status is a relationship, not an attribute, and it can be withdrawn, as Korea showed with Naver.

### Question 2. What ownership and governance structures are most effective?

The evidence favours **listed or otherwise transparent companies with a state minority or anchor shareholder, private co-investors and independent price discipline**, over both wholly state-owned vehicles and purely private ones. Deutsche Telekom (28.6% state), Singtel (Temasek-majority but listed, with KKR as co-owner of the data-centre platforms), stc (PIF 62%, listed) and Presight (listed subsidiary) all publish audited results and face a market test. OVHcloud and Schwarz show that family or foundation control supplies patience, though OVHcloud's third founder return to the chief-executive seat in 2025 shows its cost. Wholly state-owned vehicles consolidate fast but disclose little (HUMAIN) or stay captive (Serpro). Venture-backed champions grow fastest but drift toward foreign strategic capital as rounds grow (Mistral: ASML, Samsung) or toward foreign control (Aleph Alpha: Cohere). Three governance features matter more than the ownership label: separation of the regulator and customer from the supplier (present in Saudi Arabia's institutional design and in France; absent in Abu Dhabi, where one individual chairs operator, fund and council); published segment financials for the cloud and AI business; and a rule against counting related-party and investee revenue as market validation. Presight's 2023 listing, when about 82% of 2022 sales were related-party, is the benchmark HUMAIN's planned listing will be measured against ([FWDstart](https://www.fwdstart.me/p/humain-starts-ipo-preparations-16-months-after-launch-with-revenues-still-undisclosed)).

### Question 3. What is the optimal relationship between government, national champion and private-sector ecosystem?

The government should be **rule-setter, anchor customer and minority financier — and should avoid being the sole owner, sole customer and selector of a single winner at once.** The strongest results come where the state defines measurable sovereignty requirements and buys against them, leaving several suppliers to compete: France's qualified offers (17 providers qualified, per the audit court), Germany's two-lot award, Korea's tournament, the EU's scored tender, India's twelve model teams. Even France's economy minister warns against putting "all our eggs in the same basket" with Mistral. The opposite design, a champion that crowds out or ignores existing domestic firms, is the Andromède error. The ecosystem relationship that works has the champion as platform and the ecosystem as tenants and developers: DT's Munich cloud with SAP and industrial start-ups on top; Khazna hosting e& and Microsoft; AI Singapore co-funding 300 industry projects. Two cautions qualify the pluralist view. Pluralism can fragment scarce resources: Brazil has four competing Portuguese-model tracks, Turkey three Turkish ones, Abu Dhabi two Arabic ones, and Korea's ministry is now considering concentrating on one model. And an anchor customer must actually buy: Brazil's priority rule produced 11 migrations, and the French state bypassed its own doctrine.

### Question 4. Should cloud and AI capabilities be integrated within one national champion or developed through separate specialised companies?

**Separate the layers and integrate them by contract, except where sovereign capital is abundant and speed is the overriding goal.** The integrated cases are G42 (infrastructure, cloud, models, applications and a fund under one group) and HUMAIN (four layers in one company). Integration lets the state cross-subsidise and move fast, and gives one counterparty for chip diplomacy. Its costs are visible: opacity about which layer earns money, circular demand between the group's own units and investees, and concentration of geopolitical risk — the whole of G42 is exposed to one US authorisation. The separated cases are more numerous and, on commercial evidence, healthier: France (OVHcloud for infrastructure, Mistral for models, integrators for services), Korea (cloud providers, model labs and a consortium compute centre), Singapore (Singtel for infrastructure, AISG for models and talent), India (GPU providers, model teams and public infrastructure as demand). Separation allows each layer to seek its own capital and partners and makes champion status contestable. The trend toward vertical integration from the model side (Mistral buying compute) and from the infrastructure side (OVHcloud buying model start-ups; Schwarz anchoring Cohere) suggests the layers are converging through ownership links and anchor tenancy rather than through single companies. For most countries the practical answer is a **telecom or infrastructure champion plus a separate, smaller model and applications effort**, joined by anchor-tenancy contracts and a shared public customer.

### Question 5. What capabilities must remain nationally controlled, and what can be secured through international partnerships?

Using the "control, steer, depend" logic, the cases support the following allocation. **Control nationally:** the legal entity and jurisdiction that operates sovereign workloads; data classification and residency for sensitive public data; encryption keys; identity and other digital public infrastructure; the operations workforce able to run and patch systems without the vendor; national-language datasets; procurement criteria and audit rights; energy, land and grid connections; and an exit plan — reversibility, source-code access and tested disconnection, as Brazil proposes. **Steer through regulation, procurement and minority capital:** domestic infrastructure operators; a fast-follower model and applications capability; public research compute; skills. **Depend, with diversification:** accelerators, where no country in the study has an alternative; hyperscale cloud software, licensed under local operation; and frontier models, accessed commercially or as open weights. The European Commission's own conclusion that full technological sovereignty is "not today relevant" sets the ceiling. Two additions come from 2026. Dependence should be diversified across suppliers and, where politically possible, across blocs — Brazil homologates US and Chinese stacks; HUMAIN mixes Nvidia, AMD and others — because withdrawal has been documented at the chip, cloud-service and model layers. And residency should not mean a single site: the Bahrain loss argues for replication under a trusted legal framework abroad, the idea behind "digital embassies" ([WEF](https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_AI_Infrastructure_in_the_Age_of_Sovereignty_Requirements_Strategies_and_a_Trusted_Framework_for_Digital_Embassies_2026.pdf)).

### Question 6. What are the minimum investments and capabilities needed to build a globally competitive national champion?

It depends entirely on the ambition, and the thresholds differ by orders of magnitude. The cost anchors are these: all-in AI capacity at roughly **US$35bn per gigawatt** on current-generation systems by Bernstein's estimate, about US$47bn on the next generation, and US$50–60bn by Nvidia's own figure; chips at about 39% of that, rising toward 70%; power at about US$1.3bn a year per gigawatt at US$0.15/kWh; and 100 MW equating to about 35,000 current-generation GPUs ([Investing.com on Bernstein](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/how-much-does-a-gw-of-data-center-capacity-actually-cost-4314046); [Digital Citizen](https://www.digitalcitizen.life/nvidia-vera-rubin-ai-data-centers-could-cost-47-billion-per-gigawatt/); [Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/150631/the-trump-administration-throws-gulf-ai-a-lifeline/)). These are estimates, not audited costs. From them, three tiers follow as this report's inference. **Tier one, a sovereign inference and fine-tuning cloud for government and regulated sectors:** tens to low hundreds of megawatts and low single-digit billions of dollars; DT's Munich site (12–20 MW, with a second phase near €250m) and Mistral's 44 MW site (financed with US$830m of debt) are real examples at the lower end. **Tier two, a regionally competitive GPU cloud:** gigawatt scale and tens of billions, with take-or-pay anchor tenants essential; only Abu Dhabi is building at this tier, and HUMAIN's CEO puts a 3 GW build above US$30bn before tenant-supplied chips. **Tier three, a frontier-model developer:** Carnegie puts a sovereign frontier effort at "tens of billions of dollars in upfront commitments" at minimum and cites up to US$900bn, and judges it the option that "pays off in the fewest scenarios and is the most expensive" ([Carnegie, Sept 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/09/artificial-intelligence-ai-development-breakout-germany-india-japan-uk-middle-power); [Carnegie, Oct 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/10/ai-frontier-technology-middle-power-countries-four-scenarios)). Mistral, with about €5.8bn raised, is the best-funded contender outside the US and China and is not at the frontier. No champion in this study is "globally competitive" in generalist cloud. The capabilities matter as much as the money: Carnegie lists latent compute, chip supply agreements, a critical mass of frontier talent, firms experienced in large-cluster integration, and durable state capacity. The encouraging counter-fact is that using capable models has become cheap — inference for a given capability level fell more than 280-fold in two years — and fast followers lag the frontier by about six to nine months ([Stanford HAI AI Index 2025](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report?sf223786129=1)). That supports tier one plus fast-following over tier three.

### Question 7. Which national champions have demonstrated commercial success beyond government contracts?

Few, and the list is short enough to state with its qualifications. **OVHcloud** is the cleanest case: €1.145bn of revenue from about 1.7 million customers, more than half outside France, with no state capital — though profit is marginal. **Mistral AI** has company-stated annualised revenue above US$400m and named private customers in shipping, banking, aerospace, automotive and software, though several are also its shareholders or lenders and the figure is unaudited. **Naver** and **KT** are large commercial companies; KT Cloud earned KRW 997.5bn in 2025 with growth from global customers' data-centre usage, and Naver holds a KRW 2tn supply contract with an unnamed customer. **Singtel/Nxera** fills commercial colocation capacity and **Khazna** hosts Microsoft and e&. **Deutsche Telekom**'s Munich AI cloud is reported sold out to industrial users. **Presight** is profitable and growing abroad, but its foreign customers are governments. **Yotta** sells most of its GPU capacity to foreign commercial customers — commercial success that undermines its national role. By contrast, no commercial revenue beyond government or related parties is evidenced for HUMAIN, Core42, AI71, Qai, Serpro's cloud, LG AI Research, AI Singapore, Bleu or Sarvam (whose flagship public deployment began under a volunteer policy). STACKIT's reported external revenue is €46.5m. Commercial success beyond government is thus concentrated in private champions and in infrastructure leasing; it is rare in state-created vehicles and in the model layer.

### Question 8. Which models are most replicable in emerging economies?

In descending order of replicability on the evidence: **(a) Regulatory and procurement instruments** — a cloud-first rule, graded data classification, a qualification regime for providers, in-country storage with institutional control, and testable sovereignty criteria. These are cheap to legislate and are the common foundation in France, Turkey, Brazil and Saudi Arabia; their effect depends on enforcement. **(b) The subsidised compute marketplace** (India): the state buys GPU-hours, not GPUs, leaving obsolescence risk with providers; it needs credible local providers and enough eligible users, and it leaves dependence on US chips intact. **(c) The telecom-anchored partnership** (Indonesia's Lintasarta with Nvidia; Turkcell with Google; Nxera's joint ventures): replicable wherever a telecom has sites and balance sheet; the negotiating priority is to reserve capacity and capability for domestic use rather than allocate everything to the foreign tenant. **(d) Research-led language models and talent programmes** (AI Singapore, TÜBİTAK, QCRI, the Swiss initiative): low-cost, feasible with small teams and shared or rented compute, valuable for skills and local-language capability, but not self-financing — they need guaranteed public procurement to matter. **(e) A state sovereign-cloud operator and broker** (Serpro): feasible where a competent state IT company exists; Indonesia's ransomware incident and South Africa's retreat show the capacity risk. **(f) Imported turnkey stacks** (hyperscaler-partner clouds; G42's build-own-operate offer; US export packages): fastest, and the weakest on sovereignty. **Not replicable** without large sovereign capital and privileged access in Washington: the Abu Dhabi and Saudi capital-led models, and any frontier-model ambition. Carnegie's broader finding points the same way: adoption capacity and supply-chain leverage are robust across scenarios; sovereign frontier development is not. Energy is the one layer where many emerging economies hold leverage, but hosting alone leaves a country a "compute south" host unless ownership or access rights are negotiated into each deal ([Oxford Internet Institute study](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/view/31683)).

## 10. Strategic Implications for Saudi Arabia

### HUMAIN and G42 are different models, and the comparison flatters neither

HUMAIN is often described as Saudi Arabia's G42. The evidence shows three structural differences. **Age and assets:** G42 was founded in 2018 and bought Khazna in 2020; by late 2025 Khazna reported nearly 650 MW operating. HUMAIN was launched in May 2025 and has disclosed 1.1 MW live, with 14 MW on its Nvidia track due from Q4 2026. **Structure:** G42 is a holding group with separately managed and, in two cases, listed subsidiaries that publish audited results; HUMAIN is a single unlisted company combining four layers and an investment arm, with no published financials. **Access to chips:** the UAE was moved to US ally status in July 2026 with G42 named in the rule; Saudi Arabia was not covered, and its licences remain case by case ([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/150631/the-trump-administration-throws-gulf-ai-a-lifeline/)). HUMAIN's advantages are real: two state shareholders with deep balance sheets and energy, a larger domestic market, and a build-to-suit model that shifts chip capital and export-licence burden to tenants.

As reference models, both carry warnings Saudi Arabia should read closely. G42 shows what the capital-led model delivers after eight years: scale, a closed US$49bn fund, an open Arabic model family — and a champion whose licence-free chip access has a hard review date of 6 April 2027 and which has reportedly weighed US majority ownership. Carnegie's judgement on the UAE, that its sovereignty efforts "have yet to fully take off" and have deepened reliance on Washington, applies with more force to a later entrant without ally status ([Carnegie, June 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/06/early-lessons-in-the-pursuit-of-sovereign-ai)). The same Carnegie analysis suggests that sector-specific and national models "may be more financially feasible" than full-stack builds for Saudi Arabia over the next decade.

### One owner, several vehicles: the Saudi relationship map

PIF is the common shareholder: 51% of HUMAIN, 62% of stc, 51% of TAWAL. Aramco holds 49% of HUMAIN. The planned HUMAIN–center3 joint venture would therefore be an arrangement between two PIF affiliates, and it has remained an MoU "subject to customer commitments" for almost a year ([Fast Company Middle East](https://fastcompanyme.com/news/stc-and-humain-extend-deal-to-build-up-to-1gw-ai-data-centers-in-saudi-arabia/)). Strategy and regulation sit with SDAIA, the communications regulator, the Digital Government Authority and the ICT ministry; commercial execution sits with HUMAIN and stc. That separation is a design strength Abu Dhabi lacks. The weaknesses are overlap and opacity. HUMAIN Cloud and stc's Oracle Alloy and SCCC offers address the same government customers. The division of labour between HUMAIN and SDAIA over the ALLaM model is described only in secondary sources. Which Aramco digital assets moved into HUMAIN is not public.

The hyperscalers occupy the cloud layer. A secondary guide counts five providers with live in-Kingdom regions (Google, Oracle, Alibaba through SCCC, Huawei, Tencent) [verify], with Microsoft confirmed for Q4 2026 and AWS scheduled for December 2026 with a reported US$5.3bn investment and an "AI Zone" with HUMAIN ([Microsoft Source EMEA](https://news.microsoft.com/source/emea/2026/02/microsoft-confirms-saudi-arabia-datacenter-region-available-for-customers-to-run-cloud-workloads-from-q4-2026/); [TechTrends KE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/02/aws-saudi-arabia-cloud-region-december-2026/)) [verify]. Saudi rules that keep government data in-Kingdom on registered providers favour any provider with a local region, foreign ones included. The domestic champions therefore concentrate on sites, power and connectivity, and the cloud software that government workloads actually run on is foreign. Technology alignment is also mixed: US chips at HUMAIN, a Chinese-linked stack at SCCC, and a reported Chinese open model inside a HUMAIN platform — which may complicate future US export approvals (an inference, not a reported regulatory finding).

### Gaps in the Saudi AI and cloud value chain

Reading the value chain from the bottom up, the evidence shows strength in capital, land, energy, connectivity and data-centre shells, and five gaps above them.

**Chips and systems.** There is no domestic chip design or fabrication; every accelerator is imported under US licence, reportedly reviewed shipment by shipment. HUMAIN declined in July 2026 to say how many chips had been delivered ([vision2030.ai on export controls](https://vision2030.ai/geopolitics/us-chip-export-controls-saudi-uae-divergence/)) [verify — aggregator source].

**Energised capacity.** The gap between targets and disclosed delivery is the largest in this study. One analysis cites 467 MW of national data-centre load in early 2026 against a multi-gigawatt pipeline and argues that grid capacity, not chips, is the binding constraint; HUMAIN's CEO disputes that land or grid are a concern ([vision2030.ai analysis](https://vision2030.ai/analysis/saudi-arabia-ai-power-constraint-not-chips/)) [verify — aggregator source].

**Cloud platform software.** No domestic hyperscale cloud stack exists. "Sovereign cloud" means Oracle, Alibaba, AWS, Microsoft or Google technology hosted locally. Nothing comparable to OVHcloud's or STACKIT's own stack, or to Brazil's planned source-code and disconnection requirements, was found.

**Models.** ALLaM is Arabic-first and in-house but small in scale (about 4,000 GPUs). The Gulf now has at least four Arabic model lines (ALLaM, Jais, Falcon, Fanar), with Abu Dhabi's released as open weights at up to 375bn parameters.

**Applications, private demand and talent.** The Ministry of Commerce is the only named domestic customer of HUMAIN in the sources; private Saudi enterprise demand is not evidenced. The only talent evidence is training pledges. No data on the actual AI workforce was retrieved.

Two cross-cutting gaps sit beside these: **disclosure** (no revenue, capacity or delivery data from HUMAIN; no center3 segment figures) and **demand validation** (several of the clearest external customers are HUMAIN investees).

### What the benchmarks imply for Saudi Arabia

The benchmarks point to seven implications, each tied to a case.

**Publish before listing.** HUMAIN's CEO has spoken of a dual listing around 2029. When Presight listed in 2023, about 82% of its prior-year sales were related-party, according to FWDstart; investors will ask the same question of HUMAIN ([FWDstart](https://www.fwdstart.me/p/humain-starts-ipo-preparations-16-months-after-launch-with-revenues-still-undisclosed)). Publishing energised megawatts, contracted megawatts, revenue by customer type and the share from non-government, non-PIF, non-investee customers would convert announcements into a track record. Singtel's and stc's segment reporting are the models.

**Resolve the HUMAIN–stc relationship by function.** The Singapore pattern (Nxera owns and operates facilities; others run platforms and models) and the Abu Dhabi pattern (Khazna builds; Core42 operates cloud) both assign the data-centre layer to one entity. Either sign the center3 venture with contracted anchor demand or let it lapse and define clear roles: center3 as the national carrier-neutral host; HUMAIN as the AI compute, platform and model company.

**Define sovereign cloud in testable terms.** Saudi regulation already fixes residency and registration. Adding operational and reversibility tests for the most sensitive government tiers — who holds keys, whether Saudi staff can operate and patch without the vendor, whether the service survives disconnection — would follow the EU framework and Brazil's design, and would differentiate locally operated stacks from foreign-run regions.

**Anchor demand in government and the Aramco–PIF portfolio, transparently.** The French, German and Abu Dhabi cases show that anchor public demand is what makes a champion bankable. The Aramco stake provides an industrial customer of global scale. The discipline to borrow from elsewhere is to report that demand separately from market demand.

**Pick the model niche deliberately.** Korea's ministry is considering concentrating on one model because three under-scale efforts trail the frontier; Carnegie's analysis favours fast-following. A realistic Saudi position is the best Arabic and Saudi-domain models and agents on top of open or licensed frontier systems, with ALLaM developed in the open so that adoption can be measured, as Abu Dhabi does with Jais and Falcon.

**Treat export-control exposure as the central strategic risk.** Diversifying suppliers (Nvidia, AMD, Qualcomm) helps commercially but every one is a US firm. The UAE's path shows that ally status follows security assurances and sustained alignment, and that it remains revocable.

**Price in physical resilience.** A source reports that the Future Investment Initiative planned for late October 2026 was postponed after an attack on Riyadh's airport (single source in the research; corroborate before use), and the Bahrain and UAE data-centre losses are documented ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2026/10/22/saudi-arabias-humain-expands-nvidia-deal-with-84mw-ai-computing-plan/)). Dispersed sites and cross-border replication for critical state data belong in the design, not in a later phase.

## 11. Strategic Implications for Morocco

### Where Morocco stands: strategies and regulation ahead of assets

Morocco has a dense strategy stack. Maroc Digital 2030, launched in September 2024 with a MAD 11bn envelope for 2024–2026, targets a MAD 100bn GDP contribution and 240,000 digital jobs ([maroc.ma](https://maroc.ma/fr/actualites/la-strategie-digital-morocco-2030-fait-du-numerique-un-moteur-de-competitivite-et-dinclusion-sociale)) [verify]. The "Maroc IA 2030" roadmap of January 2026 targets MAD 100bn of value added and 50,000 jobs by 2030 ([TelQuel](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2026/09/09/maroc-ia-2030-100-milliards-de-dh-50-000-emplois-et-200-000-talents-au-programme_2006438/)) [verify]. A decree drafted by the cybersecurity directorate DGSSI and published in November 2024 sets two qualification levels for cloud providers serving state bodies and vital infrastructure: the provider must be a Moroccan-law company running all operations on national territory, and for sensitive data must have no extraterritorial legal exposure; a two-year transition allowed non-qualified providers where no national offer exists ([Le360, 28 Nov 2024](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/cloud-souverain-le-maroc-franchit-un-pas-decisif-sur-le-terrain-juridique_Y37PYS2R5RDCXL7HS65VPE76Y4/)). A "Cloud First" circular is planned for 2027 ([Le360, 26 Oct 2026](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/plf-2027-le-gouvernement-detaille-sa-feuille-de-route-pour-la-souverainete-numerique_N44GARWRQREUBKH6EAAP5TR3XE/)).

The assets lag. As of September 2026 the 50 MW "Jazari-Root" sovereign data centre in Rabat had land secured but construction not begun, with launch scheduled for 2027–2029; the 500 MW "Igoudar Dakhla" green campus had a convention signed and land secured but feasibility studies not started; and the digital law had been waiting at the government's general secretariat since December ([Le Matin, 9 Sept 2026](https://lematin.ma/economie/le-maroc-pose-les-premieres-briques-de-son-infrastructure-nationale-dia/364164/amp)). No public list of DGSSI-qualified providers was found, although the transition period nominally ends around November 2026. Official numbers are inconsistent (100,000 or 200,000 AI talents; jobs targets for 2026 or 2030) and should always be cited with source and date.

The only operational hyperscaler infrastructure is **Oracle's Casablanca public cloud region**, opened in April 2026 and hosted by the Moroccan colocation firm N+ONE ([Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/468760-468760.html)). Every large AI announcement remains at MoU, convention or study stage. The Nexus AI Factory (London-based Nexus Core Systems, with Naver as operator and Nvidia as supplier) shrank from a "500 MW vision, 40 MW first phase" in June 2025 to an MoU in April 2026 for MAD 12bn and about 36 MW by end-2027 ([Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news/1304-54600-morocco-signs-1-28-billion-mou-to-build-ai-factory?hl=en-GB)) [verify]; Korean press reports that Naver's Morocco phase "has not progressed". The 386 MW Iozera project in Tétouan, announced in 2024 by a company incorporated in December 2023, shows no evidence of construction ([DCD](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/us-ai-startup-plans-massive-386mw-data-center-in-morocco)) [verify]. Roughly 1.4 GW has been announced against about 36 MW with any dated commitment.

**No Moroccan entity currently qualifies as a national cloud or AI champion.** Four operators hold more than 84% of the data-centre market according to a competition-council monograph: Maroc Telecom, inwi, Medasys and N+ONE ([AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202608100156.html)) [verify]. Installed capacity is small and poorly measured: BMI counts 14 data centres with only 1.5 MW operational as of mid-2026, a figure hard to reconcile with other evidence, while commercial reports cite far higher numbers ([Le360 English on BMI](https://en.le360.ma/economy/moroccos-data-center-capacity-set-to-surge-as-ai-and-cloud-investment-accelerates-report-says_MYVV3TMHRJHN7MNV3WDYZSZERQ/)). The safe statement is that reliable installed-megawatt data do not exist publicly.

### Opportunities to develop national cloud and AI capabilities

**A regulated sovereign segment.** The DGSSI regime is close to France's SecNumCloud logic and creates a reserved public-sector and vital-infrastructure market for domestic operators, pushing hyperscalers toward hosted and partner models, as Oracle has done with N+ONE. This is Morocco's most defensible lever because it does not depend on foreign promoters' financing.

**A state sovereign cloud of realistic size.** A 50 MW Rabat facility with a "Move to Cloud" programme for administrations is a tier-one project in the terms of Section 9: on the cost anchors cited there, tens of megawatts is a low-single-digit-billion-dollar undertaking if fully equipped with AI hardware (this report's arithmetic on third-party estimates, not a project budget; no budget for the facility has been published).

**Language and public-service AI.** Government-labelled models in Darija, Amazigh and Arabic are planned with a Mistral AI lab; a Darija model family, Atlas-Chat, already exists from researchers at MBZUAI's France lab and UM6P ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.17912v2)). The Toubkal supercomputer at UM6P, with a small GPU partition (20 A100 and 12 H100 GPUs in one description), is the country's research-compute base ([Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2025/12/270528/morocco-positions-itself-as-a-regional-tech-powerhouse-at-inaugural-supercomputingafrica-2025/)) [verify].

**Energy and connectivity.** Renewables are roughly 40–46% of installed capacity depending on the source, with a 52% target for 2030 ([IEA PVPS](https://iea-pvps.org/about-iea-pvps/members/morocco/)) [verify]; the Medusa submarine cable landed at Nador in December 2025 ([European Commission](https://north-africa-middle-east-gulf.ec.europa.eu/news/medusa-lands-nador-advancing-moroccos-role-mediterranean-connectivity-2025-12-17_en)) [verify]. Self-generation rules let operators bypass grid congestion.

**A services base.** Offshoring exports reached MAD 27.08bn in 2025 with 167,380 jobs ([Le360, 26 Oct 2026](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/plf-2027-le-gouvernement-detaille-sa-feuille-de-route-pour-la-souverainete-numerique_N44GARWRQREUBKH6EAAP5TR3XE/)). AI-enabled services built on that base look more exportable than raw compute.

### Potential roles of telecoms, public institutions, sovereign vehicles and private companies

**Telecom operators.** Maroc Telecom has the balance sheet (FY2025 revenue MAD 36.68bn; capex about MAD 9.4bn) and an African footprint of more than 73 million mobile customers in 11 markets, but trade press describes it as lacking a significant digital-infrastructure portfolio beyond its networks, and it is **53% owned by the UAE's e&**, with the Moroccan state holding 22% ([TelecomTV](https://www.telecomtv.com/content/digital-platforms-services/maroc-telecom-doubles-down-on-digital-sovereignty-55264/)). Foreign majority control complicates a sovereign role under the DGSSI's second qualification level, though it is also a channel to Gulf partners; no concrete e&, G42 or Khazna project in Morocco was found. inwi, part of the Moroccan Al Mada group, has offered "sovereign cloud" since 2018 and says it is the only operator with three Tier III-certified data centres ([FNH](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualites-marocaines/inwi-certification-tier-datacenters)) [verify — partly sponsored content]; it fits the sovereignty test better but is small in compute terms. Orange Maroc opened a 1.5 MW Tier III site in 2025. The two largest operators already share passive infrastructure through joint ventures created in 2025, which shows that co-investment between them is institutionally possible; no data-centre or cloud scope was found in those ventures ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/2025/06/26/deal-iam-wana-44-mmdh-pour-la-premiere-phase-de-trois-ans/)) [verify].

**Public institutions.** The digital ministry leads strategy; DGSSI qualifies providers; the data-protection commission CNDP is preparing an AI framework; the planned Jazari institutes in the 12 regions and the Jazari-Root facility are the state's own instruments. UM6P, backed by the phosphate group OCP, is the only actor with real high-performance computing and research depth, and its AI Movement centre is a UNESCO-designated AI centre ([SNRT News](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/4-questions-a-la-presidente-executive-du-ai-movement-um6p-designe-centre-de-categorie-ii-de)) [verify].

**Sovereign investment vehicles.** The state deposit fund CDG and the Fonds Mohammed VI pour l'Investissement appear as partners of the Dakhla programme and backers of two start-up programmes totalling MAD 1.3bn. They act as co-sponsors, not operators, and nothing in the evidence suggests capital on the scale of PIF, Mubadala or QIA.

**Private technology companies.** N+ONE is the neutral colocation layer hyperscalers actually use; Medasys is a specialist operator. Foreign partners include Oracle (a Casablanca R&D lab with more than 600 staff), Mistral, Capgemini, Vertiv and Nokia, mostly under MoUs.

The most plausible Moroccan "champion" is therefore **a consortium rather than a company**: the state sovereign cloud; DGSSI-qualified domestic operators (inwi, N+ONE, Medasys, Maroc Telecom); UM6P for research compute and models; and CDG and the Fonds Mohammed VI as capital.

### Which international models transfer, and under what conditions

**The Gulf capital-led models do not transfer.** HUMAIN and G42 require three conditions Morocco does not have: sovereign capital able to commit tens of billions; cheap, abundant, state-controlled energy at gigawatt scale; and a negotiated position in Washington on chip access. Morocco's state financial arms are co-sponsors; its grid around Casablanca is already constrained; and analysts warn that grid readiness for mega-projects may not arrive until 2035 ([Daba Finance](https://dabafinance.com/en/news/morocco-data-centers-ai-energy-europe)) [verify — single analysis]. The realistic Gulf link is as investor or partner, and none is confirmed.

**France's regulated-demand model transfers best**, because Morocco has already adopted its legal logic. The conditions are administrative rather than financial: publish the list of qualified providers, enforce the regime when the transition ends, and make the state a visible anchor customer. France's own lessons apply: the audit court criticised a strategy that was uncosted and that the state itself bypassed; and Andromède warns against funding rival telecom-led clouds without committed demand.

**Brazil's graded and testable approach transfers at low cost.** Classify data; let public data run on hyperscaler regions (Oracle today) through a state framework; reserve sensitive tiers for qualified domestic infrastructure; and write reversibility, source-code access and a disconnection test into the Jazari-Root procurement. Brazil's uptake figures warn that a priority rule without enforcement yields little.

**Indonesia's telecom-plus-Nvidia model transfers partially.** It needs a domestically controlled telecom willing to buy GPUs and a home market large enough to absorb them. inwi fits the control test; Morocco's domestic AI demand is far smaller than Indonesia's. A Naver-operated Nexus platform is a foreign-operator variant of this model, which weakens the sovereignty claim.

**Turkey's localisation-with-openness and telecom–hyperscaler partnership transfers with a negotiating caveat.** A state-linked telecom acting as landlord for a hyperscaler region is achievable; a reported Maroc Telecom–Google Cloud hub negotiation is unconfirmed. The lesson from Turkcell is to avoid allocating all capacity to the foreign tenant.

**India's marketplace and Singapore's talent programmes transfer in miniature.** Subsidised GPU-hours on qualified domestic or Oracle-hosted capacity for start-ups and universities, and an apprenticeship scheme tied to real projects, are affordable. Turkey's use of EuroHPC capacity at Barcelona to train a national-language model is a notable route given Morocco's proximity and EU association; whether Morocco is eligible was not researched.

**Cautionary precedents.** Kenya's Microsoft–G42 project stalled when the treasury declined capacity-payment guarantees and the grid could not carry the load; Dakhla-scale capacity would create a comparable contingent liability ([Semafor](https://semafor.com/article/05/06/2026/energy-shortfall-problem-scuppers-kenyas-1b-microsoft-data-center)). Commentators also cite a 2018 Dakhla data-centre and wind-farm proposal that did not materialise ([AllAfrica, Jan 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202601110115.html)) [verify]. The promoters of Morocco's largest private projects are young vehicles without a delivery record.

### Morocco as a regional hub for Africa and Europe: a conditional case

**Serving Europe** depends on three conditions. Dedicated renewable supply that bypasses a constrained grid is partly in place through power-purchase arrangements and self-generation rules. Low-latency connectivity exists through Medusa and other cables. The third condition is the hard one: the European Commission has issued **no adequacy decision** for Morocco, so transfers of EU personal data need contractual safeguards, and the 2009 data-protection law has not been reformed ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-data-protection-and-cyber-security-laws/morocco)) [verify]. For AI training and inference on non-personal data this is less binding; for regulated personal-data workloads it is a real barrier. BMI warns of excess-capacity risk precisely because much planned capacity targets European workloads. Siting matters: Dakhla lies in Western Sahara, which adds legal and reputational exposure for EU-facing customers ([Western Sahara Resource Watch](https://vest-sahara.no/en/news/morocco-plans-massive-ai-center-in-occupied-western-sahara)), whereas Casablanca and the northern cable landings do not carry that risk. The UK government's decision in June 2025 not to support the Xlinks Morocco–UK power project ([The Construction Index](https://www.theconstructionindex.co.uk/news/view/government-blocks-morocco-uk-power-project)) is a reminder that European counterparties' commitment cannot be assumed.

**Serving Africa** has a stronger demand anchor in Maroc Telecom's subsidiaries and Moroccan banks, but West African data-localisation rules and latency to sub-Saharan users limit a pure hosting-export model. Competition is arriving: Cassava lists Morocco among its planned AI-factory markets ([TelecomTV](https://www.telecomtv.com/content/ai/cassava-gives-its-ai-factories-a-local-flavour-55131/)), and Oracle already operates in Johannesburg with Nairobi announced.

The defensible positioning is therefore narrower than "regional cloud and AI hub": **a trusted, renewable-powered inference and services location for francophone and Arabic-speaking markets, with a sovereign core for its own state**, building outward from domestic and African service demand rather than from speculative European compute demand. The base case supported by the evidence is tens of megawatts of AI-grade capacity by 2027–2028, not hundreds.

### What Morocco should take from the benchmarks

Seven actions follow from the evidence, each sized to Morocco's institutions and capital.

**Enforce what is already legislated.** Publish the DGSSI qualified-provider list, clarify the end of the transition, and issue the Cloud First circular with a default-to-qualified rule and price references, as Brazil did.

**Build the sovereign core first and small.** Deliver Jazari-Root in phases against real migration demand from administrations, with reversibility and disconnection requirements in the contract. Avoid the Indonesian failure mode: mandate backups and geographic redundancy from day one.

**Structure a consortium, not a monopoly.** Use CDG and the Fonds Mohammed VI as minority capital alongside qualified operators, and avoid funding two rival state-backed clouds.

**Use Maroc Telecom's e& link pragmatically.** It is a channel for Gulf capital and know-how, but sensitive tiers should sit with operators that meet the second qualification level.

**Fund language and public-service models through UM6P and the Mistral lab, and guarantee their procurement.** Small teams and rented or shared compute suffice; without public purchase commitments such models do not become durable.

**Tie every foreign mega-project to verifiable milestones.** Require disclosed equity, power contracts and anchor tenants before granting land or guarantees, and refuse take-or-pay guarantees on the state balance sheet.

**Reform data-protection law to pursue EU adequacy.** It is the single regulatory change that most enlarges the European market for Moroccan-hosted services.

## 12. Conclusions and Recommendations

The benchmark changes the question. Countries set out asking how to build a national champion; the evidence says the better question is which layers of the stack a country must control, which it should steer and which it can safely rent — and then which institutional form fits each layer. Seen that way, the champions that look strongest on paper (integrated, state-capitalised, gigawatt-scale) carry the most concentrated risk, because their technological layer is rented from a single foreign government's discretion, while the champions that look modest (a qualified private cloud, a telecom with a sold-out 12 MW site, a programme office that trains apprentices) deliver sovereignty at the layers that law and procurement can actually secure. The 2026 record adds two facts no earlier strategy priced in: access terms can change within months in either direction, and data centres are targets. Both argue for diversification of suppliers and sites over size.

For governments designing a champion, six recommendations follow. Define sovereignty in testable terms and buy against them. Commit public demand before public capital. Keep regulator, customer and supplier institutionally distinct, and require segment disclosure from any state-backed champion. Separate the infrastructure champion from the model and applications effort, and join them by contract. Size compute to contracted demand and let partners carry the chip-heavy capital where possible. And treat a local-language, public-service AI capability on top of open or licensed frontier models as the realistic model ambition. For Saudi Arabia the priority is conversion: from announced to energised capacity, from investee tenants to market customers, from undisclosed to published performance, with the HUMAIN–stc roles settled and export-control exposure managed as the central risk. For Morocco the priority is sequencing: enforce the regulatory regime, build a small sovereign core with exit rights, organise domestic operators and state capital into a consortium, and let any hub ambition grow from proven domestic and African demand. The items most likely to change this assessment in the coming months are the 6 April 2027 expiry of G42's US authorisation, the commissioning of Stargate UAE, HUMAIN's first disclosed financials, Korea's decision on concentrating its model programme, the award of the EU gigafactories and Brazil's sovereign-cloud tender, and whether Morocco's qualified-provider regime takes effect.
